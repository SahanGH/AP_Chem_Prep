Zumdahl §15.4–15.5 • name the major species before choosing a method

Directions & data
\(K_a\): \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) (p\(K_a\) 4.74) •\ \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(3.5\times10^{-8}\) (p\(K_a\) 7.46). \(K_b(\ce{NH3}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}\), \(K_a(\ce{NH4+}) = 5.6\times10^{-10}\) (p\(K_a\) 9.25). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
1. For a weak acid titrated with a strong base, name the major species and the method used in each region:
  1. Before any base is added: \(\ce{HA}\) only — weak-acid ICE
  2. Between the start and equivalence: \(\ce{HA}\) and \(\ce{A-}\) — buffer, use H–H
  3. At equivalence: \(\ce{A-}\) only — weak-base ICE with \(K_b\)
  4. After equivalence: excess \(\ce{OH-}\) — strong base alone
2. 25.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{HCl}\) is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).
  1. Volume of \(\ce{NaOH}\) at equivalence: 25.0 mL
  2. pH before any base is added: 1.00
  3. pH after 10.0 mL:
    \(\ce{H+}\) left \(= 2.50 - 1.00 = 1.50\,\mathrm{mmol}\) in 35.0 mL; \([\ce{H+}] = 0.0429\); pH \(= \mathbf{1.37}\)
  4. pH at equivalence, with a reason:
  5. pH after 35.0 mL:
    excess \(\ce{OH-}\) \(= 3.50 - 2.50 = 1.00\,\mathrm{mmol}\) in 60.0 mL; \([\ce{OH-}] = 0.0167\); pOH \(= 1.78\); pH \(= \mathbf{12.22}\)
3. 25.00 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). Complete the table.
  1. 0.00 mL (weak acid alone): 2.87
  2. 5.00 mL:
    \(\ce{HA}\) \(= 2.500-0.500 = 2.000\), \(\ce{A-}\) \(= 0.500\) mmol; pH \(= 4.74 + \log(0.500/2.000) = 4.74 - 0.60 = \mathbf{4.14}\)
  3. 12.50 mL, and say what is special about it:
  4. 20.00 mL:
    \(\ce{HA}\) \(= 0.500\), \(\ce{A-}\) \(= 2.000\) mmol; pH \(= 4.74 + \log(2.000/0.500) = 4.74 + 0.60 = \mathbf{5.34}\)
  5. 25.00 mL (equivalence):
    \([\ce{C2H3O2^-}] = 2.500/50.00 = 0.0500\); \(K_b = 5.6\times10^{-10}\); \(x = 5.3\times10^{-6}\); pOH \(= 5.28\); pH \(= \mathbf{8.72}\)
  6. 30.00 mL:
    excess \(\ce{OH-}\) \(= 0.500\) mmol in 55.00 mL; \([\ce{OH-}] = 9.09\times10^{-3}\); pOH \(= 2.04\); pH \(= \mathbf{11.96}\)

  1. An indicator is itself a weak acid, whose \(\ce{HIn}\) and \(\ce{In-}\) forms differ in color.

7. FRQ (10 points). A 20.00 mL sample of a solution of an unknown monoprotic weak acid \(\ce{HA}\) is titrated with 0.1000 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). The equivalence point is reached after 22.40 mL. The pH after 11.20 mL of base is 4.85.
  1. Calculate the concentration of the acid.
    \(n = (22.40)(0.1000) = 2.240\,\mathrm{mmol}\); \(C = 2.240/20.00 = \mathbf{0.1120}~\mathrm{M}\)
  2. Explain why the pH at 11.20 mL gives p\(K_a\) directly.
  3. Determine \(K_a\). \(1.4\times10^{-5}\)
  4. Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.
    \([\ce{A^-}] = 2.240/42.40 = 0.05283\); \(K_b = 1.0\times10^{-14}/1.4\times10^{-5} = 7.1\times10^{-10}\); \(x = \sqrt{(7.1\times10^{-10})(0.05283)} = 6.1\times10^{-6}\); pOH \(= 5.21\); pH \(= \mathbf{8.79}\)
  5. Choose an indicator for this titration from methyl red (4.4–6.2), bromthymol blue (6.0–7.6) and phenolphthalein (8.2–10.0), and justify.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 mmol of base, 1 concentration •\ (b) 2 pts 1 identifies it as half the equivalence volume, 1 states \([\ce{HA}] = [\ce{A^-}]\) makes the log term zero — asserting “pH \(=\) p\(K_a\) at halfway” with no reasoning earns 1 •\ (c) 1 pt \(K_a = 10^{-4.85} = 1.4\times10^{-5}\) •\ (d) 3 pts 1 dilution to the total 42.40 mL, 1 \(K_b\) from \(K_w/K_a\), 1 pH via pOH — using \([\ce{A^-}] = 0.1120\) (forgetting dilution) caps this part at 2 •\ (e) 2 pts 1 phenolphthalein, 1 justification referring to the equivalence pH.
