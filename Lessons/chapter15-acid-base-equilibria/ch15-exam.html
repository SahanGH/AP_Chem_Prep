AP Chemistry lessons

Chapter 15 • Acid–Base Equilibria

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §15.1–15.5 • 60 minutes • $K$ and p$K_a$ values provided with Section II

Scope and format

Covers §15.1–15.5. Nothing from §15.6 (polyprotic titrations) is calculated; you are responsible only for the shape of such a curve, which is asked qualitatively.

This test carries one long and one short free response rather than the usual two short ones. A full titration is the canonical AP long-response topic and cannot be assessed in four points.

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Adding \(\ce{NaF}\) to a solution of \(\ce{HF}\) causes the pH to
  1. rise, because \(\ce{F-}\) shifts the ionization to the left
  2. fall, because \(\ce{NaF}\) is a salt
  3. stay the same, because \(\ce{NaF}\) is neutral
  4. rise, because \(\ce{Na+}\) is basic
2. Which addition would have no effect on the pH of a \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) solution?
  1. \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\)
  2. \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  3. \(\ce{HCl}\)
  4. \(\ce{NaOH}\)
3. Which pair constitutes a buffer?
  1. \(\ce{HCl}\) and \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  2. \(\ce{NaOH}\) and \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  3. \(\ce{NH3}\) and \(\ce{NH4Cl}\)
  4. \(\ce{HNO3}\) and \(\ce{KNO3}\)
4. A buffer can also be prepared by mixing a weak acid with
  1. less than a stoichiometric amount of strong base
  2. exactly a stoichiometric amount of strong base
  3. an excess of strong base
  4. a strong acid
5. The Henderson–Hasselbalch equation is
  1. pH \(=\) p\(K_a + \log([\text{base}]/[\text{acid}])\)
  2. pH \(=\) p\(K_a + \log([\text{acid}]/[\text{base}])\)
  3. pH \(=\) p\(K_a - [\text{base}]/[\text{acid}]\)
  4. pH \(=\) p\(K_a \times \log([\text{base}]/[\text{acid}])\)
6. A buffer contains equal concentrations of \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) and \(\ce{C2H3O2^-}\) (p\(K_a\) 4.74). Its pH is
  1. 1.00
  2. 4.74
  3. 7.00
  4. 9.26
7. A buffer contains ten times as much \(\ce{A-}\) as \(\ce{HA}\). Its pH is
  1. p\(K_a - 1\)
  2. p\(K_a + 1\)
  3. p\(K_a + 10\)
  4. \(10\,\mathrm{p}K_a\)
8. A buffer whose pH lies below its p\(K_a\) contains
  1. more acid than conjugate base
  2. more conjugate base than acid
  3. equal amounts of both
  4. no conjugate base at all
9. Diluting a buffer with water
  1. leaves the pH nearly unchanged but lowers the capacity
  2. raises the pH and raises the capacity
  3. lowers the pH and lowers the capacity
  4. leaves both the pH and the capacity unchanged
10. When \(\ce{NaOH}\) is added to a \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\)/\(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\) buffer, the species consumed is
  1. \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\)
  2. \(\ce{C2H3O2^-}\)
  3. \(\ce{Na+}\)
  4. water
11. The correct first step in a “buffer plus strong base” problem is
  1. a stoichiometry calculation taken to completion
  2. an ICE table containing the added \(\ce{OH-}\)
  3. substituting the original concentrations into Henderson–Hasselbalch
  4. calculating \(K_b\) for the conjugate base
12. Buffering capacity is greatest when
  1. the two components are present in equal, large amounts
  2. the conjugate base greatly exceeds the acid
  3. the acid greatly exceeds the conjugate base
  4. the solution is as dilute as possible
13. A buffer is useful over a pH range of approximately
  1. p\(K_a \pm 0.1\)
  2. p\(K_a \pm 1\)
  3. p\(K_a \pm 7\)
  4. any pH at all
14. To prepare a buffer of pH 9.2, the best choice of weak acid has p\(K_a\)
  1. 3.4
  2. 4.7
  3. 7.5
  4. 9.2
15. A buffer fails once
  1. one of the two components has been essentially used up
  2. the solution has been diluted
  3. the pH reaches exactly p\(K_a\)
  4. any strong acid at all has been added
16. The equivalence point of a titration is defined by
  1. the stoichiometry of the reaction
  2. the pH reaching 7.00
  3. the indicator changing color
  4. the steepest part of the curve
17. 25.0 mL of 0.10 M \(\ce{HCl}\) is titrated with 0.10 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). The pH at the equivalence point is
  1. below 7
  2. exactly 7.00
  3. above 7
  4. impossible to predict
18. A weak acid titrated with a strong base has an equivalence point that is
  1. acidic
  2. exactly neutral
  3. basic
  4. dependent on the titrant concentration only
19. A weak base titrated with a strong acid has an equivalence point that is
  1. acidic
  2. exactly neutral
  3. basic
  4. at pH equal to p\(K_b\)
20. Halfway to the equivalence point of a weak acid titration,
  1. pH \(=\) p\(K_a\)
  2. pH \(= 7.00\)
  3. pH \(=\) p\(K_b\)
  4. the buffer has failed

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 30 minutes

Data
\(K_a\): \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(3.5\times10^{-8}\) (p\(K_a\) 7.46) •\ \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) (p\(K_a\) 4.74). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
Indicator ranges: methyl red 4.4–6.2 • bromthymol blue 6.0–7.6 • phenolphthalein 8.2–10.0 • alizarin yellow 10.1–12.0.

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A 25.00 mL sample of 0.100 M hypochlorous acid, \(\ce{HOCl}\), is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).

  1. Calculate the volume of \(\ce{NaOH}\) required to reach the equivalence point.
  2. Calculate the pH before any base is added.
  3. Calculate the pH after 12.50 mL of base has been added, and explain why this value could have been written down without calculation.
  4. Calculate the pH after 20.00 mL of base.
  5. Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.
  6. Select an indicator for this titration from the list provided and justify your choice.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

1.00 L of a buffer is 0.30 M in \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) and 0.30 M in \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\).

  1. Calculate the initial pH.
  2. 0.060 mol of \(\ce{NaOH}\) is added. Write the reaction that occurs and calculate the new pH.
  3. The same 0.060 mol of \(\ce{NaOH}\) added to 1.00 L of pure water gives pH 12.78. Account for the difference.
