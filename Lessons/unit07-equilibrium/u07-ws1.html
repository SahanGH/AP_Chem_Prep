Unit 7 • Equilibrium

Worksheet 1 • Dynamic Equilibrium, $Q$ and $K$

CED 7.1–7.4 • equal rates, not equal amounts

Directions & data
\(K = [\text{products}]^{\text{coef}}/[\text{reactants}]^{\text{coef}}\), omitting pure solids and liquids • \(Q\) has the same form at any moment • \(K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}\), \(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\).
1. CED 7.1 Describe what is happening at equilibrium at the particle level, and state precisely what is equal.
2. CED 7.1 Starting from pure reactants:
  1. Initially the forward rate is: at its maximum
  2. Initially the reverse rate is: zero
  3. As the reaction proceeds, the forward rate falls and the reverse rate rises.
  4. Equilibrium is reached when: the two rates become equal
3. CED 7.1 A student looking at a concentration–time graph says “the reactant and product curves level off at the same height, so the concentrations are equal at equilibrium.” Identify both errors.
4. CED 7.3 Write the \(K\) expression for each:
  1. \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}\) \([\ce{NH3}]^2/([\ce{N2}][\ce{H2}]^3)\)
  2. \(\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)}\) \([\ce{SO3}]^2/([\ce{SO2}]^2[\ce{O2}])\)
  3. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\) \([\ce{CO2}]\)
  4. \(\ce{Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g) <=> 3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g)}\) \([\ce{H2O}]^4/[\ce{H2}]^4\)
  5. \(\ce{2H2O(l) <=> 2H2(g) + O2(g)}\) \([\ce{H2}]^2[\ce{O2}]\)
5. CED 7.3 Why are pure solids and pure liquids omitted, and what decides whether water appears?
6. CED 7.4 Calculate \(K\) from each set of equilibrium concentrations:
  1. \(\ce{H2 + I2 <=> 2HI}\) with \([\ce{H2}] = 0.20\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.40\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.60\) M.
    \(K = (0.60)^2/[(0.20)(0.40)] = 0.36/0.080 = \mathbf{4.5}\)
  2. \(\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}\) with \([\ce{N2}] = 0.50\), \([\ce{H2}] = 0.30\), \([\ce{NH3}] = 0.20\) M.
    \(K = (0.20)^2/[(0.50)(0.30)^3] = 0.040/0.0135 = \mathbf{3.0}\). Note \((0.30)^3 = 0.027\), not \(0.90\).
  3. In (b), what is the most likely arithmetic slip?
7. CED 7.3 \(Q\) and \(K\).
  1. How does the expression for \(Q\) differ from that for \(K\)?
  2. \(K = 4.5\) for \(\ce{H2 + I2 <=> 2HI}\). A vessel holds \([\ce{H2}] = 0.40\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.30\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.40\) M. Calculate \(Q\) and state the direction of change.
    \(Q = (0.40)^2/[(0.40)(0.30)] = 0.16/0.12 = \mathbf{1.3}\). Since \(Q < K\), there are too few products, so the reaction proceeds to the right.
  3. Repeat for \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.10\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.30\) M. \(Q = 9.0 > K\); shifts left
8. CED 7.4 \(K_p\) and \(K_c\).
  1. Write the relationship. \(K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}\)
  2. For \(\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}\), \(\Delta n = \) \(-2\)
  3. At 500 K with \(K_c = 0.50\), find \(K_p\).
    \(K_p = 0.50[(0.08206)(500)]^{-2} = 0.50/(41.03)^2 = \mathbf{3.0\times10^{-4}}\)
  4. For which reactions are \(K_p\) and \(K_c\) numerically equal? those with \(\Delta n = 0\)
