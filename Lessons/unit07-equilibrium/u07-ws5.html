Unit 7 • Equilibrium

Worksheet 5 • Solubility Equilibria & FRQ

CED 7.11–7.12 • $K_sp$ is a constant; solubility is a position

Directions & data
\(\ce{MX}\): \(K_{sp} = s^2\) • \(\ce{MX2}\) or \(\ce{M2X}\): \(K_{sp} = 4s^3\) • common ion \(\Rightarrow\) \(Q\) rises above \(K_{sp}\) \(\Rightarrow\) less dissolves.
\(K_{sp}\): \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.6\times10^{-10}\) • \(\ce{BaSO4}\) \(1.5\times10^{-9}\) • \(\ce{CaF2}\) \(4.0\times10^{-11}\) • \(\ce{PbI2}\) \(1.4\times10^{-8}\) • \(\ce{Ag2CrO4}\) \(9.0\times10^{-12}\) • \(\ce{BaCrO4}\) \(8.5\times10^{-11}\).
1. CED 7.11 Write the dissolution equation and \(K_{sp}\) expression:
  1. \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(K_{sp} = [\ce{Ag+}][\ce{Cl-}]\)
  2. \(\ce{CaF2}\) \(K_{sp} = [\ce{Ca^2+}][\ce{F-}]^2\)
  3. \(\ce{Ag2CrO4}\) \(K_{sp} = [\ce{Ag+}]^2[\ce{CrO4^2-}]\)
  4. Why does the solid never appear? it is a pure solid
2. CED 7.11 Calculate the molar solubility in pure water:
  1. \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.3\times10^{-5}\) M
  2. \(\ce{BaSO4}\) \(3.9\times10^{-5}\) M
  3. \(\ce{CaF2}\)
    \(4s^3 = 4.0\times10^{-11}\); \(s^3 = 1.0\times10^{-11}\); \(s = \mathbf{2.2\times10^{-4}}\) M
  4. \(\ce{PbI2}\) \(1.5\times10^{-3}\) M
3. CED 7.11 \(\ce{CaF2}\) has \(K_{sp} = 4.0\times10^{-11}\) and \(\ce{BaCrO4}\) has \(K_{sp} = 8.5\times10^{-11}\).
  1. Which has the larger \(K_{sp}\)? \(\ce{BaCrO4}\)
  2. Calculate both molar solubilities.
    \(\ce{CaF2}\): \(s = \sqrt[3]{1.0\times10^{-11}} = 2.2\times10^{-4}\) M. \(\ce{BaCrO4}\): \(s = \sqrt{8.5\times10^{-11}} = 9.2\times10^{-6}\) M
  3. Which is actually more soluble, and by what factor? \(\ce{CaF2}\), about 24 times
  4. State the rule this illustrates.
4. CED 7.12 The common-ion effect.
  1. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{AgCl}\) in 0.10 M \(\ce{NaCl}\).
    \([\ce{Cl-}] \approx 0.10\); \(s = K_{sp}/[\ce{Cl-}] = 1.6\times10^{-10}/0.10 = \mathbf{1.6\times10^{-9}}\) M
  2. By what factor is it suppressed relative to pure water? about 7900
  3. Explain the effect using Le Ch\^atelier.
  4. Explain the same effect using \(Q\).
  5. What happened to \(K_{sp}\)? nothing
5. CED 7.12 Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{CaF2}\) in 0.025 M \(\ce{NaF}\), and state the assumption you make.
Assume \([\ce{F-}] \approx 0.025\), since the dissolution contributes negligibly beside the \(\ce{NaF}\). Then \(4.0\times10^{-11} = s(0.025)^2\), giving \(s = \mathbf{6.4\times10^{-8}}\) M — about 3400 times less than in pure water.
6. CED 7.12 A student says “the common ion lowers \(K_{sp}\), which is why less dissolves.” Rewrite the sentence correctly.
7. FRQ (10 points). CED 7.3 CED 7.7 CED 7.10 CED 7.12 At 700 K, \(K = 50.0\) for \(\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}\).
  1. Write the equilibrium expression. \(K = [\ce{HI}]^2/([\ce{H2}][\ce{I2}])\)
  2. A flask initially contains 1.00 M \(\ce{H2}\) and 1.00 M \(\ce{I2}\). Set up an ICE table and find all three equilibrium concentrations.
    Change: \(-x\), \(-x\), \(+2x\). \(K = (2x)^2/(1.00-x)^2 = 50.0\), so \(2x/(1.00-x) = 7.07\) and \(x = 0.780\). \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = \mathbf{0.220}\) M, \([\ce{HI}] = \mathbf{1.56}\) M
  3. Explain why the quadratic formula was not required here.
  4. Verify your answer by substituting back into \(K\).
    \((1.56)^2/(0.220)^2 = 2.43/0.0484 = \mathbf{50.2}\), agreeing with 50.0 to rounding
  5. A second flask at the same temperature contains \([\ce{H2}] = 0.40\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.30\) and \([\ce{HI}] = 0.40\) M. Calculate \(Q\) and predict the direction of change.
    \(Q = (0.40)^2/[(0.40)(0.30)] = 1.3\). Since \(Q < K\), the reaction proceeds to the right until \(Q\) rises to 50.0.
  6. State the effect on \(K\) of (i) adding more \(\ce{H2}\), and (ii) raising the temperature, given that the forward reaction is exothermic.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 1 pt correct expression • (b) 3 pts 1 ICE table with \(+2x\) for \(\ce{HI}\), 1 correct algebra to \(x = 0.780\), 1 all three concentrations • (c) 1 pt identifies the perfect square • (d) 1 pt substitution recovering \(\approx 50\) • (e) 2 pts 1 value of \(Q\), 1 direction with the \(Q < K\) justification — a bare “shifts right” earns 1 • (f) 2 pts 1 states \(K\) unchanged for the concentration change, 1 states \(K\) decreases on heating with the exothermic reason.
Spiral review • Unit 7 • blocks 1–7
  1. \(Q > K\) means the system shifts: left
  2. Inert gas at constant \(V\): no shift
  3. \(K\) for \(\ce{MX2}\) in terms of \(s\): \(4s^3\)
  4. Only what alters \(K\)? temperature
  5. Compressing shifts toward: fewer moles of gas
