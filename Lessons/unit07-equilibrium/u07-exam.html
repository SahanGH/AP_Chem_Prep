Unit 7 • Equilibrium

Unit Exam

CED 7.1–7.12 • Mon Jan 25 • 76 minutes • 44 points

Format

Section I: 30 multiple choice, no calculator, 45 minutes. Section II: one short and one long free response, calculator permitted, 31 minutes. Constants are provided with Section II.

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 31 minutes

Data
\(K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}\) •\ \(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\) •\ \(K_{sp}\): \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.6\times10^{-10}\) • \(\ce{CaF2}\) \(4.0\times10^{-11}\).

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

  1. Write the dissolution equation and \(K_{sp}\) expression for \(\ce{CaF2}\).
  2. Calculate its molar solubility in pure water.
  3. Calculate its solubility in 0.025 M \(\ce{NaF}\).
  4. State what happened to \(K_{sp}\) between (b) and (c) and why.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

At 700 K, \(K = 50.0\) for \(\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}\). The forward reaction is exothermic.

  1. Write the equilibrium expression.
  2. A flask is charged with 1.00 M \(\ce{H2}\) and 1.00 M \(\ce{I2}\). Construct an ICE table and determine all three equilibrium concentrations.
  3. Verify your answer by substituting back into the expression.
  4. A second flask at the same temperature contains \([\ce{H2}] = 0.40\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.30\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.40\) M. Calculate \(Q\) and predict the direction of change, justifying your answer.
  5. Explain why Le Ch\^atelier's principle is not the appropriate tool for part (d).
  6. State the effect on the equilibrium position and on the value of \(K\) of (i) adding more \(\ce{H2}\) and (ii) raising the temperature.
