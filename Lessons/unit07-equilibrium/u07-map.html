Unit 7 • Equilibrium

Unit Map

CED Unit 7 • 7–9% of the AP Exam • 8 blocks + float + exam

How this unit is graded — and what makes it different

Unit exam Mon Jan 25: 30 multiple choice (no calculator) \(+\) 1 short and 1 long free-response, 76 minutes, 44 points. This is one of the two largest unit exams in the course.

Kinetics asked how fast. This unit asks how far — and the answer is a single number, \(K\), that does not care about the path or the rate. The central habit is comparing \(Q\) with \(K\): it decides which way a system moves, whether it has arrived, and what happens when you disturb it.

Solubility (7.11–7.12) is the same machinery applied to a dissolving solid, which is why it sits at the end rather than in a unit of its own.

By the end of this unit you can…

  • Explain equilibrium as equal forward and reverse rates, not equal amounts. CED 7.1, CED 7.2
  • Write \(Q\) and \(K\) expressions, omitting pure solids and liquids. CED 7.3
  • Calculate \(K\) from equilibrium concentrations or pressures. CED 7.4
  • Interpret the magnitude of \(K\) and predict which side is favored. CED 7.5
  • Adjust \(K\) when a reaction is reversed, scaled, or added to another. CED 7.6
  • Solve for equilibrium concentrations with an ICE table, and test any approximation. CED 7.7
  • Interpret particulate and graphical representations of an equilibrium. CED 7.8
  • Predict the effect of a disturbance using Le Ch\^atelier's principle. CED 7.9
  • Justify that prediction quantitatively by comparing \(Q\) with \(K\). CED 7.10
  • Relate \(K_{sp}\) to molar solubility, and explain the common-ion effect. CED 7.11, CED 7.12
Where students lose points in this unit

Saying equilibrium means equal concentrations; including pure solids or liquids in \(K\); forgetting to raise a concentration to its coefficient; using the small-\(x\) approximation without checking it, or checking it and ignoring the result; claiming a catalyst or an inert gas shifts an equilibrium; saying a concentration change alters \(K\) (only temperature does); and comparing \(K_{sp}\) values for salts with different ion counts.
