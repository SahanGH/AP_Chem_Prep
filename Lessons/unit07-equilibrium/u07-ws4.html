Worksheet 4 • Le Chatelier and Q versus K

CED 7.8–7.10 • only temperature changes the value of K

Directions & data
Add reactant \(\to\) right • add product \(\to\) left • compress \(\to\) fewer moles of gas • inert gas at constant \(V\) \(\to\) no shift • catalyst \(\to\) no shift • temperature is the only change that alters \(K\).
1. CED 7.9 For \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -92\) kJ, state the direction of shift and whether \(K\) changes:
  1. Add \(\ce{N2}\) right; \(K\) unchanged
  2. Remove \(\ce{NH3}\) right; \(K\) unchanged
  3. Compress the vessel right; \(K\) unchanged
  4. Add argon at constant volume no shift; \(K\) unchanged
  5. Add a catalyst no shift; \(K\) unchanged
  6. Raise the temperature left; \(K\) decreases
2. CED 7.9 Explain, in terms of partial pressures, why adding an inert gas at constant volume causes no shift.
3. CED 7.9 Explain why compressing the vessel shifts \(\ce{N2 + 3H2 <=> 2NH3}\) to the right.
4. CED 7.9 Temperature.
  1. For an exothermic forward reaction, heat behaves as a: product
  2. Raising the temperature therefore shifts it: left
  3. And \(K\) therefore: decreases
  4. For an endothermic forward reaction, raising the temperature does what to \(K\)? increases it
  5. Why is temperature the only disturbance that changes \(K\)?
5. CED 7.10 \(K = 4.5\) for \(\ce{H2 + I2 <=> 2HI}\). For each mixture calculate \(Q\) and state the direction of change:
  1. \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = [\ce{HI}] = 0.50\) M \(Q = 1.0\); shifts right
  2. \([\ce{H2}] = 0.40\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.30\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.40\) M
    \(Q = (0.40)^2/[(0.40)(0.30)] = 0.16/0.12 = \mathbf{1.3}\); \(Q < K\), so it shifts right
  3. \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.10\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.30\) M \(Q = 9.0\); shifts left
  4. \([\ce{H2}] = 0.20\), \([\ce{I2}] = 0.20\), \([\ce{HI}] = 0.424\) M \(Q = 4.5\); already at equilibrium
6. CED 7.10 A system at equilibrium has extra product added. Explain the resulting shift using \(Q\) rather than Le Ch\^atelier.
7. CED 7.10 When is comparing \(Q\) with \(K\) the right tool and Le Ch\^atelier's principle the wrong one?
8. CED 7.8 Particulate representations. A sealed flask is drawn at three successive times. The first two pictures differ; the second and third are identical.
  1. What can you conclude? equilibrium was reached by the second picture
  2. What must be true of the total number of atoms of each element across all three? it is conserved
  3. If a diagram shows 8 product particles and 2 reactant particles for \(\ce{A <=> B}\), estimate \(K\). about 4
  4. Why is “the reaction stopped” the wrong description of the last two pictures?
9. CED 7.9 For \(\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) <=> 2SO3(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -198\) kJ, a manufacturer wants to maximise the yield of \(\ce{SO3}\). Recommend a temperature and a pressure, and note the practical tension.
