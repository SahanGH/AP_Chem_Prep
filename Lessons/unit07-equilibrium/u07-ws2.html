Unit 7 • Equilibrium

Worksheet 2 • Magnitude and Properties of K

CED 7.5–7.6 • equilibrium constants multiply where enthalpies add

Directions & data
Reverse \(\Rightarrow\) \(1/K\) • multiply coefficients by \(n\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(K^n\) • divide by \(n\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(K^{1/n}\) • add two reactions \(\Rightarrow\) \(K_1K_2\) • only temperature changes \(K\).
1. CED 7.5 Classify each as products-favored, reactants-favored, or appreciable amounts of both:
  1. \(K = 1\times10^{-12}\) reactants strongly favored
  2. \(K = 2.5\) appreciable amounts of both
  3. \(K = 4\times10^{8}\) products strongly favored
  4. \(K = 3\times10^{-3}\) reactants favored
2. CED 7.5 A reaction has \(K = 10^{35}\), yet mixing the reactants produces no observable change over several hours.
  1. Is the value of \(K\) necessarily wrong? no
  2. Explain.
  3. What single change would speed it up without altering \(K\)? adding a catalyst
3. CED 7.6 For a reaction with \(K = 4.0\), give \(K\) for:
  1. the reverse reaction 0.25
  2. the reaction with all coefficients doubled 16
  3. the reaction with all coefficients halved 2.0
  4. this reaction added to a second one with \(K = 3.0\) 12
4. CED 7.6 In Unit 6 you learned that combining reactions adds their \(\Delta H\) values. Here, combining reactions multiplies their \(K\) values. Explain why the two behave differently.
5. CED 7.6 \(K = 2.5\times10^{-3}\) for \(\ce{A + B <=> C}\) at a given temperature.
  1. \(K\) for \(\ce{C <=> A + B}\): \(4.0\times10^{2}\)
  2. \(K\) for \(\ce{2A + 2B <=> 2C}\): \(6.3\times10^{-6}\)
  3. Which side is favored for the original reaction? reactants
  4. Which side is favored for the reverse? products
6. CED 7.6 A student claims that doubling the concentration of a reactant doubles \(K\). Explain what actually happens.
7. CED 7.5 Two reactions at the same temperature have \(K_1 = 5\times10^{-4}\) and \(K_2 = 5\times10^{4}\).
  1. Which lies further toward products? the second
  2. If the second reaction is reversed, how does its \(K\) compare with \(K_1\)?
  3. Does either value tell you which reaction is faster? no
