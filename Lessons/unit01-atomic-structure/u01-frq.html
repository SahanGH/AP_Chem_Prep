Unit 1 • Atomic Structure and Properties

Free-Response Set

CED 1.1–1.8 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — two CED exclusions apply

Covers CED 1.1 through CED 1.8. Unit 1 is worth 7 %–9 % of the exam.

Topic CED 1.5 excludes assigning quantum numbers to individual electrons, and CED 1.7 excludes electron configurations of the aufbau exceptions (\(\ce{Cr}\), \(\ce{Cu}\)). Neither is asked below.

Trend explanations must be Coulombic — nuclear charge, distance, shielding. The rubrics award zero for answers resting on “stability” or “wants a full octet”.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Boron occurs as two isotopes. Its mass spectrum shows peaks at 10.013 u (19.9 %) and 11.009 u (80.1 %).

  1. Calculate the average atomic mass of boron.
  2. State which peak is taller and explain what peak height represents.
  3. Calculate the number of moles in a 5.00 g sample of boron, and the number of atoms it contains.
  4. Explain why the average atomic mass must be used for the mole calculation in (c) rather than either isotope mass.
  5. A second element has two isotopes of nearly equal abundance and an average atomic mass almost exactly halfway between them. State what this implies about the relative peak heights.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A photoelectron spectrum of an unknown element from period 3 shows four peaks with relative heights \(2\), \(2\), \(6\), \(2\) and a fifth of height \(1\), listed from highest binding energy to lowest.

  1. Identify the element and write its complete electron configuration.
  2. State which peak lies at the highest binding energy and justify it in Coulombic terms.
  3. The first ionization energies of magnesium and aluminium are 738 and 578 kJ/mol respectively. Magnesium's is higher, even though aluminium has the greater nuclear charge. Explain.
  4. State the ion aluminium forms and give the formula of the compound it forms with oxygen.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A 0.500 g sample of a compound containing only carbon, hydrogen and oxygen is burned completely, producing 0.733 g of \(\ce{CO2}\) and 0.300 g of \(\ce{H2O}\). Its molar mass is 60.05 g/mol.

  1. Determine the empirical and molecular formulas.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

A 5.00 g sample of a sand-and-salt mixture is stirred with water, filtered, and the filtrate evaporated to dryness, leaving 3.20 g of \(\ce{NaCl}\).

  1. Calculate the percent by mass of \(\ce{NaCl}\) in the mixture.
  2. Explain why this separation works, in terms of a property that differs between the two components.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Calcium reacts with nitrogen to form a binary ionic compound. Give the ions formed and the formula of the compound.
  2. Predict which has the larger lattice energy, \(\ce{CaO}\) or \(\ce{KCl}\), and justify in Coulombic terms.
