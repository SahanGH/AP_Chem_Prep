The five tools

Mole conversions\(n = m/M\);   particles \(= n \times N_A\);   always route through moles
Average atomic mass\(\bar m = \sum(\text{abundance}\times\text{mass})\) — weighted, never a plain average; leans toward the abundant isotope
Empirical formula% \(\to\) grams \(\to\) moles \(\to\) divide by smallest \(\to\) multiply to whole numbers; molecular \(= \) empirical \(\times\, (M/M_{\text{emp}})\)
PES readingposition \(=\) binding energy (subshell); area \(=\) electron count; total area identifies the element
Trend explanationsthree levers only: nuclear charge \(Z\), distance/shell \(n\), shielding. Rank \(\to\) claim \(\to\) Coulombic sentence

Decision tree: which formula problem is this?

  • Given formula, want % \(\to\) mass of element in 1 mol \(\div\) molar mass.
  • Given %, want formula \(\to\) the four-step recipe (assume 100 g).
  • Given empirical + molar mass \(\to\) divide, multiply subscripts.

The traps, one line each

  • Unweighted isotope averages. The setup must show abundances.
  • Mass spectrum vs PES: mass-spec height \(=\) abundance of an isotope; PES area \(=\) electrons in a subshell.
  • Ion configurations: build the ion, don't label the atom. \(\ce{Ca^2+}\) is \([\ce{Ar}]\), not \([\ce{Ar}]4s^2\).
  • Excited vs impossible: a vacancy below an occupied orbital is excited; more than \(2/6/10\) in a subshell is impossible.
  • “Octet” and “wants to be stable” are not explanations. Every trend answer needs \(Z\), distance, or shielding.
  • Ratios ending in .33 / .5 / .67: multiply, never round.
