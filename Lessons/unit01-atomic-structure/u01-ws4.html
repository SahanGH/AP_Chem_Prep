Worksheet 4 • FRQ Practice

Type C • CED 1.6–1.8 • answer first, then self-score against the rubric

Directions & data
Write your answers completely before looking at the scoring boxes. Then grade yourself the way a reader would: a point is earned only if the required element is explicitly on the page — readers do not award what you “meant.”
1. FRQ 1 (4 points). The PES spectrum of element Q shows five peaks (MJ/mol : relative area): 239 : 2, 22.7 : 2, 16.5 : 6, 2.05 : 2, 1.00 : 4.
  1. Write the full electron configuration of Q and identify it.
  2. Which peak's electrons are removed when Q forms its most common ion, and what is that ion's charge?
  3. A student claims the 239 MJ/mol peak represents the most chemically important electrons because they are held most tightly. Evaluate.
Rubric — FRQ 1 (score yourself)
1 pt (a) correct configuration matching all five areas •\ 1 pt (a) sulfur, tied to 16 total electrons •\ 1 pt (b) gains 2 electrons \(\to\) \(\ce{S^2-}\) (accept: 3p peak involved) • 1 pt (c) rejects claim AND names valence electrons as the chemically active ones.
2. FRQ 2 (4 points). Successive ionization energies of element Z (kJ/mol): \(IE_1=590\), \(IE_2=1145\), \(IE_3=4912\), \(IE_4=6491\).
  1. Identify Z's group and justify from the data.
  2. Write the formula of the compound Z forms with phosphorus.
  3. Explain why \(IE_2 > IE_1\) even though both remove valence electrons.
Rubric — FRQ 2 (score yourself)
1 pt (a) group 2 with the \(IE_2\to IE_3\) jump cited •\ 1 pt (b) correct 3:2 formula • 1 pt (c) removal from a cation / reduced repulsion or radius argument •\ 1 pt overall: no octet-stability language used as an “explanation” anywhere.
