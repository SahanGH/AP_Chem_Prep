Chapter 5 • Gases

Worksheet 3 • KMT, Real Gases & FRQ

Zumdahl §5.6–5.9 • name the failed postulate, not just ``it's real''

Directions & data
Deviation answers must name which postulate fails and which direction \(PV/nRT\) moves. “Because it is a real gas” earns nothing.
1. State which KMT postulate each observation relies on:
  1. A gas fills its container uniformly. constant random motion
  2. Gas pressure in a sealed flask does not decay over time. elastic collisions
  3. \(\ce{He}\) and \(\ce{Xe}\) at the same \(T\) have equal average kinetic energies. \(KE \propto T\)
2. At 300 K, rank \(\ce{H2}\), \(\ce{O2}\), and \(\ce{SF6}\) (\(M = 146.06\)) by:
  1. Average kinetic energy: all equal — depends only on \(T\)
  2. Average molecular speed: \(\ce{H2}\) \(>\) \(\ce{O2}\) \(>\) \(\ce{SF6}\)
  3. Explain how both answers can be true at once.
3. Sketch, on one set of axes, Maxwell–Boltzmann distributions for one gas at 200 K and at 600 K. Label both curves and state what stays the same.
600 K curve: broader, flatter, peak shifted right. 200 K: narrower, taller, peak left. The area under both is the same — it represents the total number of molecules.
4. Real gas behaviour:
  1. Under what two conditions does a real gas behave most ideally? high \(T\), low \(P\)
  2. At high pressure, \(PV/nRT\) rises above 1. Name the failed postulate and explain.
  3. At low temperature, \(PV/nRT\) dips below 1. Name the failed postulate and explain.
  4. Which deviates more, \(\ce{Ne}\) or \(\ce{NH3}\)? Why? \(\ce{NH3}\) — hydrogen bonding
5. FRQ (10 points). A student generates oxygen by decomposing potassium chlorate and collects it over water: \[ \ce{2 KClO3(s) -> 2 KCl(s) + 3 O2(g)} \] A sample of 2.45 g \(\ce{KClO3}\) (\(M = 122.55\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) is fully decomposed. The gas is collected at 25 °C with a total pressure of 754 torr. The vapour pressure of water at 25 °C is 23.8 torr.
  1. Calculate the moles of \(\ce{O2}\) that should be produced.
    \(n(\ce{KClO3}) = 2.45/122.55 = 0.0200\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(\times\tfrac32 = 0.0300\,\mathrm{mol}\) \(\ce{O2}\)
  2. Determine the partial pressure of \(\ce{O2}\), in atm.
  3. Calculate the volume of gas collected.
    \(V = nRT/P = (0.0300)(0.08206)(298)/0.9608 = 0.764\,\mathrm{L}\)
  4. Explain why the water-vapour correction is a subtraction, and state the effect of omitting it.
  5. The student repeats the collection at 35 °C (\(P_{\ce{H2O}} = 42.2\,\mathrm{torr}\)), same total pressure. Predict whether the collected volume is larger or smaller, and give both reasons.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 moles of \(\ce{KClO3}\), 1 the 3:2 ratio applied • (b) 2 pts 1 subtraction performed, 1 conversion to atm • (c) 2 pts 1 correct rearrangement with kelvin, 1 value with units • (d) 2 pts 1 explains the mixture/Dalton reasoning, 1 states the direction of the error •\ (e) 2 pts 1 predicts larger, 1 gives BOTH reasons — one reason only earns 1.
