Chapter 5 • Gases

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §5.1–§5.9 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope

Covers CED 3.4 (ideal gas law, §5.2–5.5), 3.5 (kinetic molecular theory, §5.6–5.7) and 3.6 (deviation from ideal behaviour, §5.8–5.9). §5.10 (atmospheric chemistry) is context, not assessed content, and does not appear.

\(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\). The vapour pressure of water at 25 °C is 23.8 torr.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Calcium carbonate decomposes on strong heating: \[ \ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)} \] A 10.0 g sample is decomposed completely and the carbon dioxide is collected in a rigid 2.00 L vessel at 25 °C.

  1. Calculate the number of moles of \(\ce{CO2}\) produced.
  2. Calculate the pressure exerted by the \(\ce{CO2}\) in the vessel.
  3. The gas is instead collected over water at 25 °C, and the total pressure in the collection tube is 755 torr. Calculate the partial pressure of the \(\ce{CO2}\).
  4. State what happens to the pressure in the rigid vessel if the temperature is raised to 100 °C, and justify your answer using kinetic molecular theory.
  5. Explain why the identity of the gas does not appear anywhere in the ideal gas law.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A sealed flask contains a mixture of helium (4.003 g/mol) and sulfur dioxide (64.07 g/mol) at the same temperature.

  1. State how the average kinetic energies of the two gases compare, and justify your answer.
  2. State how their average molecular speeds compare, and explain the difference from (a).
  3. Calculate the ratio of the effusion rate of helium to that of sulfur dioxide.
  4. Sketch, in words, how the distribution of molecular speeds for helium differs in shape from that for sulfur dioxide at the same temperature.
  5. At high pressure the measured pressure of a real gas is lower than the ideal gas law predicts. Explain why, in terms of intermolecular forces.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A sample of nitrogen occupies 4.00 L at 1.50 atm and 300 K.

  1. Calculate the volume it would occupy at 1.00 atm and 450 K.
  2. State whether the number of moles of nitrogen has changed, and why.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

A rigid vessel contains 0.300 mol \(\ce{N2}\), 0.100 mol \(\ce{O2}\) and 0.100 mol \(\ce{Ar}\). The total pressure is 2.50 atm.

  1. Calculate the partial pressure of nitrogen.
  2. Argon has the largest molar mass of the three. State whether it therefore exerts the largest partial pressure, and justify your answer.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. State the two conditions under which a real gas behaves most nearly ideally.
  2. Explain, for each condition, why the gas approaches ideal behaviour.
