Chapter 5 • Gases

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §5.1–5.9 • 50 minutes • periodic table and constants provided

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Which pressure is equivalent to 2.00 atm?
  1. 380 torr
  2. 1520 torr
  3. 760 torr
  4. 101 kPa
2. A sealed can collapses when the steam inside condenses because
  1. a vacuum pulls the walls inward
  2. the internal pressure drops far below atmospheric pressure
  3. condensation makes the metal contract
  4. the water expands as it cools
3. A gas at 4.0 atm in 3.0 L is expanded to 12.0 L at constant temperature. Its new pressure is
  1. 16 atm
  2. 1.0 atm
  3. 4.0 atm
  4. 0.25 atm
4. A gas occupies 2.0 L at 200 K. At constant pressure, its volume at 400 K is
  1. 1.0 L
  2. 4.0 L
  3. 2.0 L
  4. 8.0 L
5. Which variable must always be expressed in kelvin when using a gas law?
  1. pressure
  2. volume
  3. temperature
  4. moles
6. What volume does 2.00 mol of an ideal gas occupy at STP?
  1. 22.4 L
  2. 44.8 L
  3. 11.2 L
  4. 2.00 L
7. The molar volume of 22.4 L/mol may be used only when
  1. the gas is diatomic
  2. the gas is at room temperature
  3. the gas is at 0 °C and 1 atm
  4. the gas behaves non-ideally
8. Which expression gives the density of an ideal gas?
  1. \(PM/RT\)
  2. \(RT/PM\)
  3. \(MRT/P\)
  4. \(nRT/V\)
9. At constant temperature and pressure, which gas has the greatest density?
  1. \(\ce{H2}\)
  2. \(\ce{He}\)
  3. \(\ce{N2}\)
  4. \(\ce{CO2}\)
10. A 1.00 L sample of gas at STP has a mass of 1.96 g. Its molar mass is closest to
  1. 22.4 g/mol
  2. 44 g/mol
  3. 2.0 g/mol
  4. 88 g/mol
11. For \(\ce{N2(g) + 3 H2(g) -> 2 NH3(g)}\) with all gases at the same \(T\) and \(P\), 9.0 L of \(\ce{H2}\) reacts completely with
  1. 3.0 L of \(\ce{N2}\)
  2. 9.0 L of \(\ce{N2}\)
  3. 27 L of \(\ce{N2}\)
  4. 6.0 L of \(\ce{N2}\)
12. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\). Decomposing 1.00 mol of \(\ce{CaCO3}\) produces what volume of \(\ce{CO2}\) at STP?
  1. 11.2 L
  2. 22.4 L
  3. 44.8 L
  4. 100 L
13. Gas volumes may be compared directly to identify a limiting reactant only when
  1. the gases have equal molar masses
  2. all gases are at the same temperature and pressure
  3. the reaction occurs at STP
  4. the gases are all diatomic
14. A flask holds 0.20 mol \(\ce{He}\) and 0.60 mol \(\ce{Ar}\) at a total pressure of 4.0 atm. The partial pressure of helium is
  1. 1.0 atm
  2. 2.0 atm
  3. 3.0 atm
  4. 0.80 atm
15. In a mixture of ideal gases, the partial pressure exerted by one component depends on
  1. its molar mass
  2. the molar masses of the other gases
  3. its mole fraction and the total pressure
  4. the size of its molecules
16. A gas collected over water at 25 °C shows a total pressure of 760 torr. If the vapour pressure of water is 23.8 torr, the partial pressure of the collected gas is
  1. 783.8 torr
  2. 736.2 torr
  3. 760 torr
  4. 23.8 torr
17. A student collecting gas over water forgets to subtract the water vapour pressure. The calculated number of moles of gas will be
  1. too high
  2. too low
  3. unaffected
  4. negative
18. Two gases at the same temperature must have the same
  1. average molecular speed
  2. average kinetic energy
  3. density
  4. molar mass
19. As the temperature of a gas rises, its Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
  1. narrows and shifts left
  2. broadens and shifts right, with unchanged area
  3. keeps its shape but grows in area
  4. becomes a straight line
20. A real gas shows \(PV/nRT\) less than 1. The best explanation is that
  1. intermolecular attractions reduce the force of wall collisions
  2. the molecules occupy significant volume
  3. the temperature is measured in Celsius
  4. the gas is decomposing

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 20 minutes

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

Magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid: \[ \ce{Mg(s) + 2 HCl(aq) -> MgCl2(aq) + H2(g)} \] A 0.243 g sample of Mg (\(M = 24.31\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) reacts completely. The hydrogen is collected over water at 25 °C, where the vapour pressure of water is 23.8 torr. The total pressure is 752 torr.

  1. Calculate the moles of \(\ce{H2}\) produced.
  2. Determine the partial pressure of \(\ce{H2}\) in atm.
  3. Calculate the volume of gas collected.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

Consider samples of \(\ce{He}\) and \(\ce{CO2}\), each at 300 K.

  1. Compare their average kinetic energies and their average molecular speeds, with justification.
  2. At 200 K and 50 atm, which gas is expected to deviate more from ideal behaviour? Justify.
  3. State one change in conditions that would make both gases behave more ideally, and explain why.
