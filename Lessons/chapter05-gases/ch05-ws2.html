Worksheet 2 • Gas Stoichiometry and Partial Pressures

Zumdahl §5.4–5.5 • the molar-volume shortcut works ONLY at STP

Directions & data
Before using 22.42 L/mol, check that the problem says STP. If it gives any other temperature or pressure, use \(PV = nRT\).
1. \(\ce{2 KClO3(s) -> 2 KCl(s) + 3 O2(g)}\). \(M(\ce{KClO3}) = 122.55\).
  1. Volume of \(\ce{O2}\) at STP from 24.51 g \(\ce{KClO3}\):
    \(n = 24.51/122.55 = 0.2000\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(\times\tfrac32 = 0.3000\,\mathrm{mol}\) \(\ce{O2}\); \(\times 22.42 = 6.73\,\mathrm{L}\)
  2. Volume of that same \(\ce{O2}\) at 25 °C and 1.00 atm:
    \(V = (0.3000)(0.08206)(298)/1.00 = 7.34\,\mathrm{L}\)
  3. Explain why (b) is larger than (a).
2. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\). What mass of \(\ce{CaCO3}\) (\(M = 100.09\)) is needed to produce 11.2 L of \(\ce{CO2}\) at STP?
\(n(\ce{CO2}) = 11.2/22.42 = 0.500\,\mathrm{mol}\); 1:1 \(\to 0.500 \times 100.09 = 50.0\,\mathrm{g}\)
3. \(\ce{N2(g) + 3 H2(g) -> 2 NH3(g)}\), all gases at the same temperature and pressure.
  1. Volume of \(\ce{H2}\) needed to react with 12.0 L \(\ce{N2}\): 36.0 L
  2. Volume of \(\ce{NH3}\) produced: 24.0 L
  3. Explain why you can work entirely in volumes here without ever computing moles.
4. 6.00 L of \(\ce{H2}\) and 2.00 L of \(\ce{O2}\), both at 1.00 atm and 298 K, react completely.
  1. Balanced equation: \(\ce{2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O}\)
  2. Limiting reactant, using volumes:
    Needed ratio 2:1. 6.00 L \(\ce{H2}\) requires 3.00 L \(\ce{O2}\), but only 2.00 L is present \(\to\) \(\ce{O2}\) is limiting.
  3. Volume of \(\ce{H2}\) left over: 2.00 L
5. A 5.00 L vessel at 300. K holds 0.150 mol \(\ce{N2}\), 0.250 mol \(\ce{O2}\), and 0.100 mol \(\ce{CO2}\).
  1. Total pressure: 2.46 atm
  2. Mole fraction of \(\ce{O2}\): 0.500
  3. Partial pressure of \(\ce{O2}\): 1.23 atm
  4. If the \(\ce{CO2}\) were removed at constant \(T\) and \(V\), what happens to \(P_{\ce{N2}}\)?
6. Hydrogen is collected over water at 20 °C. The total pressure is 738 torr, the volume 0.400 L. The vapour pressure of water at 20 °C is 17.5 torr.
  1. Partial pressure of \(\ce{H2}\) in torr and atm: 720.5 torr \(=\) 0.9480 atm
  2. Moles of \(\ce{H2}\) collected:
    \(n = (0.9480)(0.400)/((0.08206)(293)) = 0.01577\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  3. A student forgets the water-vapour correction. Is their answer too high or too low, and by roughly what percentage?
