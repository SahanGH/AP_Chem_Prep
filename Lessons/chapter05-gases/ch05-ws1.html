Directions & data
Convert all temperatures to kelvin and all pressures to atm before using \(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\). Show the rearranged equation before plugging in.
1. Pressure conversions:
  1. 1.25 atm to torr: 950. torr
  2. 608 mmHg to atm: 0.800 atm
  3. 50.66 kPa to atm: 0.5000 atm
  4. 3.00 atm to kPa: 304 kPa
2. Explain why a sealed metal can collapses when the steam inside it condenses. Refer to what is pushing in which direction.
3. Single-variable gas laws:
  1. 2.00 L at 3.00 atm expands to 6.00 L at constant \(T\). New pressure: 1.00 atm
  2. 500. mL at 250. K is heated to 400. K at constant \(P\). New volume: 800. mL
  3. 1.50 L holding 0.200 mol is expanded to 4.50 L at constant \(T\) and \(P\). Moles now present: 0.600 mol
4. A student heats a gas from 20 °C to 40 °C and predicts the volume will double. Explain the error and give the correct factor.
5. Ideal gas law calculations:
  1. Volume of 1.50 mol at 2.00 atm and 350. K: 21.5 L
  2. Moles in 4.00 L at 760 torr and 27 °C: 0.163 mol
  3. Pressure of 0.500 mol in 12.0 L at 100. °C: 1.28 atm
  4. Temperature at which 2.00 mol occupies 50.0 L at 1.00 atm: 305 K
6. Density and molar mass:
  1. Density of \(\ce{N2}\) at STP: 1.25 g/L
  2. A gas has density 2.86 g/L at STP. Its molar mass is: 64.1 g/mol
  3. Suggest an identity for that gas. \(\ce{SO2}\) (64.07)
  4. A 1.00 g sample occupies 0.500 L at 1.00 atm and 273 K. Molar mass?
    \(n = (1.00)(0.500)/((0.08206)(273)) = 0.02232\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(M = 1.00/0.02232 = 44.8\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)
7. Why is it meaningless to state “the density of oxygen gas” with no further information, while “the density of liquid water” is a useful figure?
