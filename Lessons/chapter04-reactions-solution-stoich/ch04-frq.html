Chapter 4 • Types of Chemical Reactions and Solution Stoichiometry

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §4.1–§4.11 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — two CED exclusions apply

This chapter is heavily examinable: CED 3.7 (§4.3), 3.8 (§4.1–4.2), 3.10 (§4.5), 4.2–4.3 (§4.6–4.7), 4.6 (§4.8, §4.11), 4.7 (§4.4–4.5), 4.8 (§4.8) and 4.9 (§4.9–4.10).

Two things Zumdahl teaches here are off the framework and are deliberately absent from these questions. The CED exclusion on topic 4.7 removes the terms “oxidizing agent” and “reducing agent”, and removes rote memorization of solubility rules beyond the short list in 4.7.A.5. You will be asked which species is oxidized and which is reduced — never to label an “agent”, and never to recite a solubility table from memory.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

50.0 mL of 0.200 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\) is mixed with 25.0 mL of 0.150 M \(\ce{CaCl2}\). A white precipitate of \(\ce{AgCl}\) forms. Assume volumes are additive.

  1. Write the balanced net ionic equation for the reaction.
  2. Identify the spectator ions and explain what makes them spectators.
  3. Determine which reactant is limiting.
  4. Calculate the mass of \(\ce{AgCl}\) precipitated.
  5. Calculate the concentration of silver ion remaining in solution after the reaction.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Acidified potassium permanganate is used to determine the iron(II) content of a solution: \[ \ce{MnO4^-(aq) + Fe^2+(aq) + H+(aq) -> Mn^2+(aq) + Fe^3+(aq) + H2O(l)} \quad \text{(unbalanced)} \]

  1. Assign the oxidation number of manganese in \(\ce{MnO4-}\) and in \(\ce{Mn^2+}\), and state whether manganese is oxidized or reduced.
  2. Balance the equation in acidic solution.
  3. A 25.00 mL sample of the iron(II) solution required 22.15 mL of 0.0200 M \(\ce{KMnO4}\) to reach the endpoint. Calculate the concentration of \(\ce{Fe^2+}\).
  4. This titration needs no added indicator. Explain why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. 25.0 mL of 6.00 M \(\ce{HCl}\) is diluted with water to a final volume of 250.0 mL. Calculate the concentration of the diluted solution.
  2. State how many moles of \(\ce{HCl}\) are present after the dilution, and explain your answer without calculating.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

Write the balanced net ionic equation for each reaction.

  1. Aqueous sodium carbonate is mixed with aqueous calcium chloride, forming a precipitate of calcium carbonate.
  2. Aqueous nitric acid is neutralized by aqueous potassium hydroxide.
  3. Explain why the net ionic equation in (b) is the same for any strong acid neutralized by any strong hydroxide base.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

Solutions of \(\ce{HCl}\) and of \(\ce{HF}\), both 0.10 M, are tested for electrical conductivity. The \(\ce{HCl}\) solution conducts strongly; the \(\ce{HF}\) solution conducts only weakly.

  1. Account for the difference in terms of the particles present.
  2. State what a particulate drawing of the \(\ce{HF}\) solution should show that a drawing of the \(\ce{HCl}\) solution should not.
