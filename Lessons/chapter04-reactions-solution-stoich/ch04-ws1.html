Chapter 4 • Types of Chemical Reactions and Solution Stoichiometry

Worksheet 1 • Solutions and Electrolytes

Zumdahl §4.1–4.3 • strong is not the same word as concentrated

Directions & data
Show the setup for every calculation. For classification questions, state what happens to the solute in water — not just the label.
1. Classify each as a strong electrolyte, weak electrolyte, or nonelectrolyte, and state what species are present in solution:
  1. \(\ce{KBr}\): strong; \(\ce{K+}\) and \(\ce{Br-}\)
  2. \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\): weak; mostly intact molecules
  3. \(\ce{C6H12O6}\) (glucose): nonelectrolyte; molecules
  4. \(\ce{HNO3}\): strong; \(\ce{H+}\) and \(\ce{NO3-}\)
  5. \(\ce{NH3}\): weak; mostly \(\ce{NH3}\) molecules
2. Two beakers each hold 0.10 M solutions. Beaker A (\(\ce{HCl}\)) lights a conductivity bulb brightly; beaker B (\(\ce{HF}\)) lights it dimly.
  1. Explain the difference.
  2. A student concludes “beaker A is more concentrated.” Evaluate.
3. Explain at the particle level why water dissolves \(\ce{NaCl}\) but \(\ce{CCl4}\) does not.
4. Molarity calculations:
  1. Molarity of 29.2 g \(\ce{NaCl}\) in 500. mL: 1.00 M
  2. Mass of \(\ce{KMnO4}\) (\(M = 158.03\)) in 250. mL of 0.0200 M: 0.790 g
  3. Moles in 40.0 mL of 0.125 M: 5.00e-3 mol
  4. Volume of 0.250 M containing 0.0500 mol: 200. mL
5. Dilutions:
  1. 15.0 mL of 18.0 M \(\ce{H2SO4}\) diluted to 500. mL: 0.540 M
  2. Volume of 12.0 M \(\ce{HCl}\) needed for 100. mL of 0.500 M: 4.17 mL
  3. State the safety rule for diluting concentrated acid, and why.
6. Ion concentrations in strong electrolyte solutions:
  1. \([\ce{K+}]\) and \([\ce{SO4^2-}]\) in 0.30 M \(\ce{K2SO4}\): 0.60 M; 0.30 M
  2. \([\ce{NO3-}]\) in 0.15 M \(\ce{Fe(NO3)3}\): 0.45 M
  3. Total ion concentration in 0.20 M \(\ce{Na3PO4}\): 0.80 M
7. Describe, step by step, how to prepare 250.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{K2Cr2O7}\) (\(M = 294.20\)) from the solid.
