Worksheet 2 • Precipitation and Net Ionic Equations

Zumdahl §4.5–4.7 • cite the rule number for every solubility call

Directions & data
For every solubility decision, name the rule that decided it. For every net ionic equation, list the spectator ions separately.
1. Soluble or insoluble? Cite the rule number.
  1. \(\ce{Na2CO3}\): soluble — rule 2
  2. \(\ce{AgBr}\): insoluble — rule 3 exception
  3. \(\ce{Ba(NO3)2}\): soluble — rule 1
  4. \(\ce{PbSO4}\): insoluble — rule 4 exception
  5. \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\): insoluble — rule 5
  6. \(\ce{(NH4)3PO4}\): soluble — rule 2 beats rule 6
2. For each pair of solutions, predict the products by ion interchange, decide whether a precipitate forms, and write the balanced formula equation (or “no reaction”).
  1. \(\ce{Pb(NO3)2(aq) + 2 KI(aq)}\)
  2. \(\ce{NaNO3(aq) + KCl(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq)}\)
3. For \(\ce{AgNO3(aq) + NaCl(aq)}\):
  1. Formula equation:
  2. Complete ionic equation:
  3. Spectator ions: \(\ce{Na+}\) and \(\ce{NO3-}\)
  4. Net ionic equation: \(\ce{Ag+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) -> AgCl(s)}\)
4. Write the net ionic equation for each:
  1. \(\ce{Na2CO3(aq) + CaCl2(aq)}\) \(\ce{Ca^2+ + CO3^2- -> CaCO3(s)}\)

  3. \(\ce{(NH4)2SO4(aq) + Ba(OH)2(aq)}\)
5. Three different reactions all give the net ionic equation \(\ce{Ba^2+(aq) + SO4^2-(aq) -> BaSO4(s)}\). Write formula equations for two of them, using different spectator ions.
6. A student writes this net ionic equation for mixing \(\ce{HC2H3O2(aq)}\) with \(\ce{NaOH(aq)}\): \(\ce{H+ + OH^- -> H2O}\). Identify the error and give the correct equation.
7. Precipitation stoichiometry. 40.0 mL of 0.250 M \(\ce{Pb(NO3)2}\) is mixed with excess \(\ce{KI}\).
  1. Moles of \(\ce{Pb^2+}\): 0.0100 mol
  2. Mass of \(\ce{PbI2}\) formed (\(M = 461.0\)):
    1:1 ratio \(\to 0.0100 \times 461.0 = 4.61\,\mathrm{g}\)
