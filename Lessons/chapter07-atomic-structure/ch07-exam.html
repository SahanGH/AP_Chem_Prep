Chapter 7 • Atomic Structure and Periodicity

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §7.1–7.2, 7.5, 7.8–7.9, 7.11–7.12 • 50 minutes

Scope

This test covers only the CED-relevant sections. Nothing from §7.3–7.4 (hydrogen spectrum, Bohr model), §7.6–7.7 (quantum numbers, orbital shapes), §7.10, or §7.13 appears.

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Which relationship correctly connects the wavelength and frequency of light?
  1. \(\lambda\nu = c\)
  2. \(\lambda + \nu = c\)
  3. \(\lambda/\nu = c\)
  4. \(\lambda\nu = h\)
2. Which type of electromagnetic radiation has photons of the greatest energy?
  1. microwave
  2. infrared
  3. red visible light
  4. ultraviolet
3. A photon of 300 nm light has how many times the energy of a photon of 900 nm light?
  1. \(1/3\)
  2. 3
  3. 9
  4. the same
4. Light below a metal's threshold frequency is shone on it at very high intensity. The result is
  1. many electrons are ejected
  2. a few electrons are ejected
  3. no electrons are ejected
  4. electrons are ejected only after a delay
5. The photoelectric effect provides evidence that light
  1. travels as a continuous wave only
  2. is quantized into photons of energy \(h\nu\)

  4. slows down in a vacuum
6. An atomic orbital is best described as

  2. a region of space where an electron is likely to be found

7. How many orbitals are in the \(n = 3\) shell?
  1. 3
  2. 6
  3. 9
  4. 18
8. In a polyelectronic atom, the 2\(s\) orbital is lower in energy than the 2\(p\) because

  2. 2\(s\) penetrates closer to the nucleus and is shielded less
  3. 2\(s\) holds more electrons
  4. 2\(p\) orbitals are unstable

  2. different energies, because 2\(s\) penetrates more
  3. the same energy, because hydrogen has one proton
  4. different energies, because of the Pauli principle
10. A 2\(p\) electron in fluorine experiences a larger effective nuclear charge than one in oxygen because fluorine has
  1. one more proton with essentially the same shielding
  2. one more electron shell
  3. a larger atomic radius
  4. fewer core electrons

  2. \([\ce{Ar}]4s^2 3d^{10} 4p^3\)
  3. \([\ce{Ar}]4s^2 3d^{10} 4p^5\)

  2. \([\ce{Ar}]3d^6\)
  3. \([\ce{Ar}]4s^2 3d^6\)

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 20 minutes

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

A certain metal has a threshold frequency of 8.5e14 /s. Constants: \(c = 3.00e8\,\mathrm{m/s}\), \(h = 6.626e-34\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\).

  1. Calculate the minimum photon energy required to eject an electron from this metal.
  2. Determine whether light of wavelength 400. nm will eject electrons. Show your reasoning.
  3. Explain why increasing the intensity of that 400. nm light would not change your answer to (b).

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

The complete photoelectron spectrum of an element shows peaks at 126, 9.07, 5.31, and 0.74 MJ/mol with relative areas \(2 : 2 : 6 : 2\).

  1. Determine the electron configuration and identify the element, citing your evidence.
  2. Explain why the peak at 5.31 MJ/mol lies at lower binding energy than the peak at 9.07, even though both correspond to electrons in the \(n = 2\) shell.
  3. Predict whether aluminium's 1\(s\) binding energy is greater or less than 126 MJ/mol, and justify with Coulombic reasoning.
