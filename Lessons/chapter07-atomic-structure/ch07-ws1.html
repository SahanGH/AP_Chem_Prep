Chapter 7 • Atomic Structure and Periodicity

Worksheet 1 • Light, Photons, and Orbitals

Zumdahl §7.1–7.2, 7.5, 7.9 • wavelengths in metres, always

Directions & data
Constants: \(c = 3.00e8\,\mathrm{m/s}\), \(h = 6.626e-34\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\), \(N_A = 6.022e23\,\mathrm{/mol}\). Convert nanometres to metres before substituting.
1. Photon calculations:
  1. Frequency of 750. nm light: 4.00e14 /s
  2. Energy of one photon at that wavelength: 2.65e-19 J
  3. Energy per mole of those photons: 160. kJ/mol
  4. Wavelength of a photon carrying 3.00e-19 J:
    \(\lambda = hc/E = (6.626\times10^{-34})(3.00\times10^{8})/(3.00\times10^{-19}) = 6.63e-7\,\mathrm{m} = 663\,\mathrm{nm}\)
2. Without a calculator, state which member of each pair has the greater photon energy and why:
  1. 300 nm or 600 nm: 300 nm — shorter \(\lambda\), higher \(E\)
  2. Infrared or ultraviolet: ultraviolet
  3. \(\nu = 2e15\,\mathrm{/s}\) or \(\nu = 5e14\,\mathrm{/s}\): \(2\times10^{15}\) — higher frequency
3. The photoelectric effect. A certain metal has a threshold frequency of 9.0e14 /s.
  1. Will light of 5.0e14 /s eject electrons, no matter how intense? Explain.
  2. Will dim light at 1.2e15 /s eject electrons?
  3. Explain what these two results together prove about the nature of light.
4. Orbitals and the Pauli principle:
  1. Define an orbital in one sentence.
  2. Number of orbitals in the \(n=4\) shell: 16
  3. Maximum electrons in \(n=4\): 32
  4. Why can an orbital hold only two electrons? Pauli — two spin states only
5. Shielding and penetration.
  1. In hydrogen, 2\(s\) and 2\(p\) have identical energy; in lithium they do not. Explain what changed.
  2. The 2\(p\) orbital has its maximum probability closer to the nucleus than 2\(s\) does. Why, then, is 2\(s\) lower in energy?
  3. Write the expression relating effective and actual nuclear charge. \(Z_{\text{eff}} \approx Z - S\)
