Chapter 7 • Atomic Structure and Periodicity

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §7.1–§7.12 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — two CED exclusions apply

Covers CED 1.5 (§7.5, §7.8–7.11), 1.6 (photoelectron spectroscopy, §7.12), 1.7 (periodic trends, §7.12), 3.11 (§7.1) and 3.12 (§7.2).

Two exclusions shape what is asked. Topic 1.5 excludes assigning quantum numbers to individual electrons, so §7.6 is not examined — you will never be asked for \(n\), \(\ell\), \(m_\ell\), \(m_s\). Topic 1.7 excludes electron configurations of the aufbau exceptions, so \(\ce{Cr}\) and \(\ce{Cu}\) do not appear as configuration questions. Both remain worth understanding; neither will be tested.

\(h = 6.626e-34\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\), \(c = 3.00e8\,\mathrm{m/s}\).

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

  1. Write the complete electron configuration of a phosphorus atom (\(Z = 15\)).
  2. A photoelectron spectrum of phosphorus shows five peaks. State how many peaks correspond to each subshell and give the relative height of each.
  3. State which peak appears at the highest binding energy, and explain why.
  4. The first ionization energy of sulfur is lower than that of phosphorus, breaking the general left-to-right trend. Explain.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Excited hydrogen atoms emit a bright blue-green line at 486 nm.

  1. Calculate the energy of a single photon of this light.
  2. Calculate the energy emitted per mole of photons, in kJ/mol.
  3. Hydrogen also emits a violet line at 434 nm. State whether its photons carry more or less energy than those at 486 nm, and justify without calculating.
  4. Explain why the emission spectrum of hydrogen consists of discrete lines rather than a continuous band.
  5. State what the existence of these lines tells you about the energy of the electron in a hydrogen atom.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Arrange \(\ce{Na}\), \(\ce{Mg}\) and \(\ce{K}\) in order of increasing atomic radius.
  2. Justify your ordering using Coulombic reasoning.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

A photoelectron spectrum of an unknown element from period 3 shows peaks at relative heights \(2\), \(2\), \(6\) and \(1\), from highest binding energy to lowest.

  1. Identify the element, showing your reasoning.
  2. State which peak lies at the lowest binding energy, and what that indicates chemically.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Light of 300 nm strikes a metal whose electrons are bound with an energy of 5.0e-19 J. Determine whether an electron is ejected.
  2. Doubling the intensity of the light does not change whether an electron is ejected. Explain.
