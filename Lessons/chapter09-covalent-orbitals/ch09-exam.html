Chapter 9 • Covalent Bonding: Orbitals

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §9.1 only • 35 minutes • shorter by design — see scope note

Scope — why this test is shorter

Only §9.1 of this chapter is on the AP framework, so this test is 15 multiple choice \(+\) 1 free response rather than the usual 20 \(+\) 2. Nothing from §9.2–9.5 (molecular orbital theory, MO diagrams, bond order from filled orbitals) appears — that material was marked enrichment in the notes and is not assessed.

Section I • Multiple Choice 15 questions • 22 minutes • 1 point each

1. Hybrid orbitals are proposed in order to explain
  1. why atoms have discrete energy levels
  2. observed molecular geometries that atomic orbitals cannot account for
  3. the existence of isotopes
  4. why some molecules are coloured
2. The hybridization of the central atom is determined by the number of
  1. bonds it forms
  2. electron domains around it
  3. valence electrons it has
  4. atoms in the molecule
3. A central atom with three electron domains is
  1. sp hybridized
  2. sp\(^2\) hybridized
  3. sp\(^3\) hybridized
  4. not hybridized
4. The hybridization of oxygen in \(\ce{H2O}\) is
  1. sp
  2. sp\(^2\)
  3. sp\(^3\)
  4. unhybridized
5. How many hybrid orbitals result from sp\(^2\) hybridization?
  1. 2
  2. 3
  3. 4
  4. 1
6. An sp\(^2\) hybridized carbon atom has how many unhybridized \(p\) orbitals remaining?
  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 3
7. The hybridization of carbon in \(\ce{HCN}\) is
  1. sp
  2. sp\(^2\)
  3. sp\(^3\)
  4. dsp\(^3\)
8. A \(\sigma\) bond is formed by
  1. head-on overlap along the internuclear axis
  2. side-by-side overlap of \(p\) orbitals
  3. transfer of electrons
  4. overlap of two lone pairs
9. A triple bond consists of
  1. three \(\sigma\) bonds
  2. three \(\pi\) bonds
  3. one \(\sigma\) and two \(\pi\) bonds
  4. two \(\sigma\) and one \(\pi\) bond
10. How many \(\sigma\) and \(\pi\) bonds are in \(\ce{C2H4}\)?
  1. 4 \(\sigma\), 2 \(\pi\)
  2. 5 \(\sigma\), 1 \(\pi\)
  3. 6 \(\sigma\), 0 \(\pi\)
  4. 5 \(\sigma\), 2 \(\pi\)
11. Rotation about a carbon–carbon double bond is restricted because
  1. the \(\sigma\) bond is too strong
  2. rotating would destroy the parallel overlap of the \(\pi\) bond
  3. the carbons are sp\(^3\) hybridized
  4. double bonds are longer than single bonds
12. Cis and trans isomers of a disubstituted alkene are separable compounds because
  1. the \(\pi\) bond prevents interconversion at ordinary temperatures
  2. they contain different numbers of atoms
  3. they have different molecular formulas
  4. one is ionic
13. In \(\ce{CH3OH}\), the hybridizations of carbon and oxygen are respectively
  1. sp\(^3\) and sp\(^3\)
  2. sp\(^3\) and sp\(^2\)
  3. sp\(^2\) and sp\(^3\)
  4. sp and sp\(^3\)
14. Which molecule contains an sp hybridized central atom?
  1. \(\ce{NH3}\)
  2. \(\ce{SO3}\)
  3. \(\ce{CO2}\)
  4. \(\ce{CH4}\)
15. \(\ce{SO2}\) and \(\ce{SO3}\) both have sp\(^2\) hybridized sulfur, yet only \(\ce{SO2}\) is bent. The reason is that \(\ce{SO2}\)
  1. has a lone pair occupying one of the three domains
  2. has fewer electron domains
  3. contains a different hybridization
  4. has stronger bonds

Section II • Free Response 1 question • 13 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Consider the molecule \(\ce{CH3-CH=CH-C#CH}\).

  1. Give the hybridization of each of the five carbon atoms, reading left to right, with the domain count that justifies each.
  2. State the total number of \(\sigma\) bonds and \(\pi\) bonds in the molecule.
  3. Explain why the C1–C2 bond permits free rotation while the C2=C3 bond does not.
  4. Predict the C1–C2–C3 bond angle and the C3–C4–C5 bond angle.
