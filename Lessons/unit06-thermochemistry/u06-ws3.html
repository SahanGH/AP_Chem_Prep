Unit 6 • Thermochemistry

Worksheet 3 • Enthalpy of Reaction and Hess's Law

CED 6.6, 6.9 • reverse a reaction, flip the sign — every time

Directions & data
\(\Delta H\) scales with the coefficients and flips sign when the reaction is reversed • enthalpy is a state function, so reactions and their \(\Delta H\) values add.
1. CED 6.6 For \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -890.5\) kJ.
  1. Energy released per mole of \(\ce{CH4}\): 890.5 kJ
  2. Energy released by 0.500 mol of \(\ce{CH4}\): 445.3 kJ
  3. \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{2CH4 + 4O2 -> 2CO2 + 4H2O(l)}\): \(-1781\) kJ
  4. \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{CO2(g) + 2H2O(l) -> CH4(g) + 2O2(g)}\): \(+890.5\) kJ
  5. Which of those three manipulations changed the sign, and which changed the magnitude?
2. CED 6.6 For \(\ce{2CO(g) + O2(g) -> 2CO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -566\) kJ.
  1. Energy released per mole of \(\ce{CO}\): 283 kJ
  2. \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{2CO2(g) -> 2CO(g) + O2(g)}\): \(+566\) kJ
  3. \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{CO(g) + \tfrac{1}{2}O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\): \(-283\) kJ
3. CED 6.9 State Hess's law and say what property of enthalpy makes it valid.
4. CED 6.9 Given:
(1) \(\ce{C(gr) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\)    \(\Delta H = -393.5\) kJ
(2) \(\ce{CO(g) + \tfrac{1}{2}O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\)    \(\Delta H = -283.0\) kJ
find \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{C(gr) + \tfrac{1}{2}O2(g) -> CO(g)}\).
  1. Which equation must be reversed, and why?
  2. Carry out the combination.
    \((1)\) as written: \(-393.5\).
    \((2)\) reversed: \(\ce{CO2 -> CO + \tfrac{1}{2}O2}\), \(+283.0\).
    Adding, \(\ce{CO2}\) cancels and half the \(\ce{O2}\) cancels: \(\Delta H = -393.5 + 283.0 = \mathbf{-110.5}~\text{kJ}\)
  3. The tabulated \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) of \(\ce{CO}\) is \(-110.5\) kJ/mol. What does the agreement confirm?
  4. Why is this reaction's \(\Delta H\) hard to measure directly?
5. CED 6.9 A student reverses one equation in a Hess's law problem but forgets to change its sign. The correct answer is \(-110.5\) kJ.
  1. What do they report? \(-676.5\) kJ
  2. By how much are they wrong, and how does that relate to the equation they mis-signed?
6. CED 6.9 Given \(\ce{N2 + O2 -> 2NO}\), \(\Delta H = +180\) kJ, and \(\ce{2NO + O2 -> 2NO2}\), \(\Delta H = -112\) kJ, find \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{N2 + 2O2 -> 2NO2}\).
Both equations are used as written and simply added; \(\ce{NO}\) cancels. \(\Delta H = +180 + (-112) = \mathbf{+68}~\text{kJ}\) — endothermic overall.
7. CED 6.6 A thermochemical equation is written with fractional coefficients: \(\ce{H2(g) + \tfrac{1}{2}O2(g) -> H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -286\) kJ. Is that legitimate, and what does the value mean?
