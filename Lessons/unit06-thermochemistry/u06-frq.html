Unit 6 • Thermochemistry

Free-Response Set

CED 6.1–6.9 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope

Covers CED 6.1 through CED 6.9, worth 7 %–9 % of the exam. Note that CED 6.5 (energy of phase changes) is sourced from Zumdahl §10.8 and CED 6.7 (bond enthalpies) from §8.8 — both sit outside the thermochemistry chapter, and both are examined here.

Topics CED 6.6 and CED 6.9 carry an exclusion covering the enthalpy-versus-internal-energy distinction and formal treatment of state functions. Hess's law itself is fully examinable; the thermodynamic formalism behind it is not.

Take \(c(\ce{H2O}) = 4.18\,\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 334\,\mathrm{J/g}\) and \(\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 2260\,\mathrm{J/g}\).

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A 75.0 g block of an unknown metal is heated to 98.0 °C and dropped into 150.0 g of water at 22.0 °C in an insulated calorimeter. The final temperature of both is 24.6 °C.

  1. State the direction of heat flow and the sign of \(q\) for the water and for the metal.
  2. Calculate the heat gained by the water.
  3. Calculate the specific heat capacity of the metal.
  4. The water's temperature rose only 2.6 °C while the metal fell 73.4 °C, for the same quantity of heat. Explain.
  5. State one assumption made in this calculation.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Consider these data at 298 K:
Reaction\(\Delta H^\circ\) (kJ)
\(\ce{N2(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO(g)}\)\(+180.5\)
\(\ce{2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\)\(-114.1\)
  1. Use Hess's law to find \(\Delta H^\circ\) for \(\ce{N2(g) + 2O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\).
  2. Hence determine \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) for \(\ce{NO2(g)}\), and explain the relationship.
  3. State \(\Delta H^\circ\) for \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> 2NO(g) + O2(g)}\) and justify.
  4. Using average bond enthalpies \(\ce{N#N}\) 941, \(\ce{O=O}\) 495 and \(\ce{N=O}\) 607 kJ/mol, estimate \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{N2 + O2 -> 2NO}\) and compare with the tabulated \(+180.5\) kJ.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

Barium hydroxide octahydrate and ammonium chloride are mixed in a beaker. The beaker becomes so cold that it freezes to a wet block of wood.

  1. State the sign of \(\Delta H\) and explain the direction of heat flow.
  2. Describe the energy diagram for this reaction.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. Calculate the total energy needed to convert 25.0 g of ice at 0 °C into steam at 100 °C.
  2. State which step requires the most energy and why.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Two blocks at different temperatures are placed in contact and isolated. Describe what happens at the particle level until they reach thermal equilibrium.
  2. State whether the two blocks must contain the same amount of thermal energy at equilibrium, and why.
