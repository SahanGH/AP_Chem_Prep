Worksheet 4 • Bond and Formation Enthalpies & FRQ

CED 6.7–6.8 • bonds: broken minus formed, the one reversed convention

Directions & data
\(\Delta H = \sum D(\text{broken}) - \sum D(\text{formed})\) •\ \(\Delta H^\circ = \sum n\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{prod}) - \sum n\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{react})\) • elements in their standard states have \(\Delta H_f^\circ = 0\).
Bond energies (kJ/mol): H–H 432 • Cl–Cl 239 •\ H–Cl 427 • C–H 413 • C–C 347 •\ O–H 467 • O=O 495 • C=O (in \(\ce{CO2}\)) 799 • C=C 614 • N\(\equiv\)N 941 • N–H 391.
\(\Delta H_f^\circ\) (kJ/mol): \(\ce{CO2}\) \(-393.5\) • \(\ce{CO}\) \(-110.5\) • \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) \(-286\) • \(\ce{H2O(g)}\) \(-242\) • \(\ce{CH4}\) \(-75\) • \(\ce{NH3}\) \(-46\) •\ \(\ce{C2H4}\) \(+52\) • \(\ce{C2H6}\) \(-84.7\).
1. CED 6.7 Bond enthalpy basics.
  1. Breaking a bond is: endothermic
  2. Forming a bond is: exothermic
  3. The expression is broken minus formed. Why does that ordering look backwards next to every other rule in this unit?
2. CED 6.7 Estimate \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{H2(g) + Cl2(g) -> 2HCl(g)}\).
  1. Total energy of bonds broken: 671 kJ
  2. Total energy of bonds formed: 854 kJ
  3. \(\Delta H\): \(-183\) kJ
  4. Does the sign make sense?
3. CED 6.7 Estimate \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(g)}\) from bond enthalpies.
Broken: \(4(413) + 2(495) = 1652 + 990 = 2642\) kJ. Formed: \(2(799) + 4(467) = 1598 + 1868 = 3466\) kJ. \(\Delta H = 2642 - 3466 = \mathbf{-824}~\text{kJ}\)
4. CED 6.8 Now calculate \(\Delta H^\circ\) for that same reaction from enthalpies of formation.
\([-393.5 + 2(-242)] - [-75 + 0] = -877.5 + 75 = \mathbf{-802.5}~\text{kJ}\)
5. The two routes gave \(-824\) kJ and \(-802.5\) kJ.
  1. Which is the more reliable, and why?
  2. When should you use bond enthalpies at all?
  3. Is a discrepancy of about 20 kJ evidence that one calculation is wrong?
6. CED 6.8 Enthalpies of formation.
  1. \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) of \(\ce{O2(g)}\): 0
  2. \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) of \(\ce{C}\) (graphite): 0
  3. Why are these zero by definition?
  4. Calculate \(\Delta H^\circ\) for \(\ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3}\). \(-92\) kJ
  5. Estimate the same reaction from bond enthalpies.
    Broken \(941 + 3(432) = 2237\); formed \(6(391) = 2346\); \(\Delta H = \mathbf{-109}~\text{kJ}\)
7. CED 6.8 Calculate \(\Delta H^\circ\) for \(\ce{C2H4(g) + H2(g) -> C2H6(g)}\) from formation enthalpies.
\((-84.7) - (52 + 0) = \mathbf{-136.7}~\text{kJ}\)
8. FRQ (10 points). CED 6.4 CED 6.6 CED 6.8 Methane is burned in a calorimeter. A 0.0400 mol sample is completely combusted in 500.0 g of water, whose temperature rises from 21.00 °C to 38.05 °C.
  1. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of methane, with liquid water as a product.
  2. Calculate the heat absorbed by the water.
    \(q = (500.0)(4.18)(17.05) = \mathbf{35{,}635}~\text{J} = 35.6~\text{kJ}\)
  3. Calculate the experimental \(\Delta H\) of combustion in kJ/mol.
    \(q_{\text{rxn}} = -35.6\) kJ; \(\Delta H = -35.6/0.0400 = \mathbf{-891}~\mathrm{kJ/mol}\)
  4. Calculate the accepted value from enthalpies of formation.
    \([-393.5 + 2(-286)] - [-75] = -965.5 + 75 = \mathbf{-890.5}~\mathrm{kJ/mol}\)
  5. Comment on the agreement, and state one reason a real calorimetry experiment usually gives a magnitude that is too small.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 1 pt balanced equation with states •\ (b) 2 pts 1 correct \(\Delta T = 17.05\), 1 answer 35.6 kJ • (c) 3 pts 1 sign reversal to \(q_{\text{rxn}}\), 1 division by moles, 1 answer with kJ/mol — an answer in bare kJ earns at most 2 • (d) 2 pts 1 correct setup products minus reactants, 1 answer \(-890.5\) • (e) 2 pts 1 notes the close agreement, 1 gives heat loss to the calorimeter or surroundings as the reason for a low magnitude.
