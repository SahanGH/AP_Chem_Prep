Unit 6 • Thermochemistry

Worksheet 2 • Calorimetry and Phase Changes

CED 6.4–6.5 • $q = mc T$ only while the temperature is changing

Directions & data
\(q = mc\Delta T\) • \(q = n\Delta H\) for a phase change • \(c\): water 4.18, ice 2.09, steam 2.03 J/g/°C •\ \(\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 6.02\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 40.7\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) •\ \(M(\ce{H2O}) = 18.02\,\mathrm{g/mol}\).
1. CED 6.4 Straight substitution.
  1. Energy to raise 50.0 g of water from 20.0 °C to 80.0 °C:
    \((50.0)(4.18)(60.0) = 12{,}540\) J \(= \mathbf{12.5}\) kJ
  2. Energy released when 250.0 g of water cools by 15.0 °C: -15.7 kJ
  3. Why is water's specific heat unusually large, and name one consequence.
2. CED 6.4 A reaction is run in 100.0 g of water in a coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature rises 5.20 °C, and 0.0250 mol of the limiting reactant was consumed.
  1. Calculate \(q_{\text{water}}\).
    \((100.0)(4.18)(5.20) = \mathbf{2174}~\text{J}\), absorbed by the water
  2. State \(q_{\text{rxn}}\) and explain the sign.
  3. Calculate \(\Delta H\) in kJ/mol.
    \(-2174/0.0250 = -86{,}900\) J/mol \(= \mathbf{-86.9}\) kJ/mol
  4. Why is the answer reported per mole rather than in kilojoules alone?
3. CED 6.4 A 50.0 g metal block at 100.0 °C is dropped into 100.0 g of water at 22.0 °C. The final temperature is 24.6 °C.
  1. Calculate the heat gained by the water.
    \((100.0)(4.18)(2.6) = \mathbf{1087}~\text{J}\)
  2. Calculate the specific heat of the metal.
    \(-1087 = (50.0)(c)(24.6-100.0) = (50.0)(c)(-75.4)\); \(c = \mathbf{0.288}~\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}\)
  3. The metal's temperature changed by more than 75 °C while the water's changed by less than 3. Give two reasons.
4. CED 6.5 Phase changes.
  1. Energy to melt 36.0 g of ice at 0 °C:
    \(n = 36.0/18.02 = 2.00\) mol; \(q = (2.00)(6.02) = \mathbf{12.0}\) kJ
  2. Energy to vaporize 18.0 g of water at 100 °C: 40.7 kJ
  3. Energy to melt 90.0 g of ice:
    \(n = 4.99\) mol; \(q = \mathbf{30.1}\) kJ
  4. Vaporizing a mole costs about seven times what melting a mole costs. Explain in terms of intermolecular forces.
5. CED 6.5 Why can \(q = mc\Delta T\) not be used during melting?
6. CED 6.5 Take 25.0 g of ice at -10.0 °C all the way to steam at 110.0 °C.
  1. Which equation applies to the sloped regions of the heating curve? \(q = mc\Delta T\)
  2. Which applies to the flat regions? \(q = n\Delta H\)
  3. Calculate the total energy required.
    \(n = 25.0/18.02 = 1.39\) mol.
    ice \(-10\to0\): \((25.0)(2.09)(10.0) = 522\) J \(= 0.52\) kJ
    melt: \((1.39)(6.02) = 8.35\) kJ
    water \(0\to100\): \((25.0)(4.18)(100.0) = 10.45\) kJ
    boil: \((1.39)(40.7) = 56.47\) kJ
    steam \(100\to110\): \((25.0)(2.03)(10.0) = 0.51\) kJ
    Total \(= \mathbf{76.3}\) kJ
  4. Which single step dominates, and what fraction of the total is it? boiling, about 74%
7. CED 6.5 Explain why a steam burn at 100 °C is worse than a burn from liquid water at the same temperature.
