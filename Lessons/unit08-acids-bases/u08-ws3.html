5. CED 8.7 Calculate the pH of 0.20 M \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\).
  1. Write the hydrolysis equation.
  2. Calculate \(K_b\). \(5.6\times10^{-10}\)
  3. Calculate the pH.
    \(x = \sqrt{(5.6\times10^{-10})(0.20)} = 1.06\times10^{-5} = [\ce{OH-}]\); pOH \(= 4.98\); pH \(= \mathbf{9.02}\)
6. CED 8.7 Calculate the pH of 0.30 M \(\ce{NaF}\).
\(K_b = 1.0\times10^{-14}/7.2\times10^{-4} = 1.4\times10^{-11}\); \(x = \sqrt{(1.4\times10^{-11})(0.30)} = 2.0\times10^{-6}\); pOH \(= 5.69\); pH \(= \mathbf{8.31}\)
7. CED 8.4 The common-ion effect. For 1.0 M \(\ce{HF}\) alone, \([\ce{H+}] = 2.7\times10^{-2}\) and the acid is 2.7% ionized.
  1. Calculate \([\ce{H+}]\) in a solution 1.0 M in \(\ce{HF}\) and 1.0 M in \(\ce{NaF}\).
    \([\ce{H+}] = K_a[\ce{HF}]/[\ce{F-}] = (7.2\times10^{-4})(1.0/1.0) = 7.2\times10^{-4}\); pH \(= \mathbf{3.14}\)
  2. Percent ionization now: 0.072%
  3. Explain the suppression using Le Ch\^atelier.
8. CED 8.4 Buffer or not? Give a one-phrase reason.
  1. \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(+\) \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\) buffer — weak acid and its conjugate base
  2. \(\ce{HCl}\) \(+\) \(\ce{NaCl}\) not — \(\ce{HCl}\) is strong, \(\ce{Cl-}\) inert
  3. \(\ce{NH3}\) \(+\) \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) buffer — weak base and its conjugate acid
  4. \(\ce{NaOH}\) \(+\) \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\) not — no weak conjugate pair present
