First: which of the four cases are you in?

Strong acid or base alonecomplete ionization; pH directly. Double \([\ce{OH-}]\) for \(\ce{Ba(OH)2}\), \(\ce{Ca(OH)2}\), \(\ce{Sr(OH)2}\)
Weak acid or base aloneone equilibrium. \([\ce{H+}] = \sqrt{K_aC_0}\), or for a base \([\ce{OH-}] = \sqrt{K_bC_0}\) — then convert through pOH
Both members of a pairbuffer. pH \(=\) p\(K_a + \log(\text{base}/\text{acid})\)
Strong reagent addedreact to completion first, in moles; then whichever case is left

The numbers and relationships

  • \(\mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = 14.00\); \(K_aK_b = K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\); p\(K_a = -\log K_a\).
  • Six strong acids: \(\ce{HCl}\), \(\ce{HBr}\), \(\ce{HI}\), \(\ce{HNO3}\), \(\ce{H2SO4}\), \(\ce{HClO4}\). \(\ce{HF}\) is not one.
  • Neutral means \([\ce{H+}] = [\ce{OH-}]\) — not pH 7.
  • Optimal buffering at pH \(=\) p\(K_a\); useful range p\(K_a \pm 1\); indicators change over p\(K_a \pm 1\) too.

Equivalence pH: strong\(+\)strong \(= 7.00\) exactly; weak acid \(+\) strong base \(>7\); weak base \(+\) strong acid \(<7\).

The traps, one line each

  • Reporting \(-\log x\) as the pH for a weak base — it is the pOH.
  • Forgetting to double \([\ce{OH-}]\) for a Group 2 hydroxide.
  • Skipping the 5% check, or running it and ignoring the result.
  • Putting added strong base into an ICE table instead of reacting it to completion first.
  • Inverting the base/acid ratio in Henderson–Hasselbalch.
  • Assuming any equivalence point is at pH 7.
  • Calculating solubility from pH — topic 8.11 excludes it.

How to study for this exam

This is a 44-point exam covering nine blocks — the second-heaviest unit in the course. Budget more time than for any unit except 3.

Redo WS 5's FRQ cold; the long free-response is its twin, and it deliberately walks the whole titration curve. Then drill the method selection table on page one until naming the case is automatic, because almost every lost mark in this unit comes from running the right arithmetic on the wrong situation.

Two sanity checks worth thirty seconds each: for a base problem, does your pH come out above 7? And after a buffer calculation, is the pH within one unit of p\(K_a\)? If either fails, you have almost certainly picked the wrong case.
