Unit 8 • Acids and Bases

Free-Response Set

CED 8.1–8.11 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — three CED exclusions apply

Covers CED 8.1 through CED 8.11. Unit 8 is worth 11 %–15 % of the exam, the second-heaviest unit.

Topic CED 8.5 excludes species computations on polyprotic titration curves; CED 8.9 excludes deriving Henderson–Hasselbalch and computing the pH change on adding acid or base to a buffer; CED 8.11 restricts pH-and-solubility to qualitative treatment only. None of the three is asked below.

\(K_w = 1.0e-14\) at 25 °C.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Hydrofluoric acid has \(K_a = 7.2e-4\). Consider 0.250 M \(\ce{HF}\).

  1. Write the ionization equilibrium and the \(K_a\) expression.
  2. Test whether the approximation \(0.250 - x \approx 0.250\) is valid, and state your conclusion.
  3. Calculate the pH.
  4. Calculate the percent ionization and the \(\mathrm{p}K_a\).

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A buffer is made from \(\ce{HF}\) (\(\mathrm{p}K_a = 3.14\)) and \(\ce{NaF}\).

  1. Explain how this mixture resists pH change when a small amount of strong acid is added, and when strong base is added.
  2. Calculate the pH of a buffer that is 0.20 M in \(\ce{HF}\) and 0.30 M in \(\ce{F-}\).
  3. State the pH at the half-equivalence point of an \(\ce{HF}\)–\(\ce{NaOH}\) titration, with justification.
  4. Explain why the equivalence point of this titration lies above pH 7, and name a suitable indicator.
  5. Two buffers both have pH 3.14; one is 1.0 M in each component, the other 0.010 M. State which has the greater buffer capacity and why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Calculate the pH of 0.0050 M \(\ce{Ba(OH)2}\), assuming complete dissociation.
  2. A student reports pH 2.30 for this solution. Identify the likely error.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. Acid strength increases along \(\ce{HClO}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HClO2}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HClO3}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HClO4}\). Explain.
  2. Acid strength also increases along \(\ce{HF}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HCl}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HBr}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HI}\), even though electronegativity decreases down the group. Explain.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

For each solid, predict whether acidifying the solution increases, decreases or barely changes its solubility, and justify in one sentence. Do not calculate.

  1. \(\ce{CaCO3}\)
  2. \(\ce{AgCl}\)
  3. \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\)
