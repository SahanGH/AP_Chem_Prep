Worksheet 5 • Titrations, Indicators & FRQ

CED 8.5, 8.11 • name the major species before choosing a method

Directions & data
\(K_a\): \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) (p\(K_a\) 4.74) • \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(3.5\times10^{-8}\) (p\(K_a\) 7.46). \(K_b(\ce{NH3}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}\), \(K_a(\ce{NH4+}) = 5.6\times10^{-10}\) (p\(K_a\) 9.25). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\). Indicator ranges: methyl red 4.4–6.2 • bromthymol blue 6.0–7.6 • phenolphthalein 8.2–10.0.
1. CED 8.5 For a weak acid titrated with a strong base, name the major species and the method in each region:
  1. Before any base is added: \(\ce{HA}\) only — weak-acid ICE
  2. Between the start and equivalence: \(\ce{HA}\) and \(\ce{A-}\) — buffer, use H–H
  3. At equivalence: \(\ce{A-}\) only — weak-base ICE with \(K_b\)
  4. After equivalence: excess \(\ce{OH-}\) — strong base alone
2. CED 8.5 25.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{HCl}\) is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).
  1. Volume at equivalence: 25.0 mL
  2. pH before any base: 1.00
  3. pH after 10.0 mL:
    \(\ce{H+}\) left \(= 2.50 - 1.00 = 1.50\) mmol in 35.0 mL; \([\ce{H+}] = 0.0429\); pH \(= \mathbf{1.37}\)
  4. pH at equivalence, with a reason.
3. CED 8.5 50.0 mL of 0.10 M \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) is titrated with 0.10 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).
  1. Volume at equivalence: 50.0 mL
  2. pH before any base: 2.87
  3. pH after 25.0 mL, and what is special about it.
  4. pH at equivalence.
    \([\ce{C2H3O2^-}] = 5.0/100.0 = 0.050\) M; \(K_b = 5.6\times10^{-10}\); \(x = 5.3\times10^{-6}\); pOH \(= 5.28\); pH \(= \mathbf{8.72}\)
  5. Why is this equivalence point basic rather than neutral?
4. CED 8.5 Compare the two titrations in problems 2 and 3, which use the same volumes and concentrations.
  1. Are the equivalence volumes the same? Explain.
  2. Are the equivalence pH values the same? Explain.
  3. Which curve has a flat buffer region, and why?
5. CED 8.5 100.0 mL of 0.050 M \(\ce{NH3}\) is titrated with 0.10 M \(\ce{HCl}\).
  1. Volume at equivalence: 50.0 mL
  2. pH at the halfway point, with a one-line reason.
  3. pH at equivalence.
    \([\ce{NH4+}] = 5.0/150.0 = 0.0333\) M; \(x = \sqrt{(5.6\times10^{-10})(0.0333)} = 4.3\times10^{-6}\); pH \(= \mathbf{5.36}\)
  4. Why is this equivalence point acidic? only \(\ce{NH4+}\) remains, a weak acid
6. CED 8.5 Indicators.
  1. An indicator is itself a weak acid whose two forms differ in colour.
  2. It changes colour over roughly: p\(K_a \pm 1\)
  3. Distinguish end point from equivalence point.
  4. Which indicator suits problem 3 (equivalence 8.72)? phenolphthalein
  5. Which suits problem 5 (equivalence 5.36)? methyl red
7. CED 8.11 pH and solubility — qualitative only.
  1. State the test that decides whether a salt's solubility depends on pH.
  2. More soluble in acid? \(\ce{CaCO3}\) yes — \(\ce{H2CO3}\) is weak
  3. \(\ce{AgCl}\) no — \(\ce{HCl}\) is strong
  4. \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\) yes — \(\ce{H2O}\) is very weak
  5. Refute the claim “acids are reactive, so every salt dissolves better in acid.”
8. FRQ (10 points). CED 8.3 CED 8.5 CED 8.9 A 25.00 mL sample of 0.100 M hypochlorous acid, \(\ce{HOCl}\), is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).
  1. Calculate the volume of \(\ce{NaOH}\) needed to reach equivalence. 25.00 mL
  2. Calculate the pH before any base is added.
    \(x = \sqrt{(3.5\times10^{-8})(0.100)} = 5.9\times10^{-5}\); pH \(= \mathbf{4.23}\)
  3. Calculate the pH after 12.50 mL of base, and explain why it could have been written down without calculation.
  4. Calculate the pH after 20.00 mL of base.
    \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(= 2.500 - 2.000 = 0.500\), \(\ce{OCl-}\) \(= 2.000\) mmol; pH \(= 7.46 + \log(2.000/0.500) = 7.46 + 0.60 = \mathbf{8.06}\)
  5. Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.
    All 2.500 mmol is \(\ce{OCl-}\) in 50.00 mL, so \([\ce{OCl^-}] = 0.0500\) M. \(K_b = 1.0\times10^{-14}/3.5\times10^{-8} = 2.9\times10^{-7}\); \(x = 1.2\times10^{-4}\); pOH \(= 3.92\); pH \(= \mathbf{10.08}\)
  6. Select an indicator and justify.
