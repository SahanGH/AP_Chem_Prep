Unit 8 • Acids and Bases

Unit Map

CED Unit 8 • 11–15% of the AP Exam • 9 blocks + float + exam

How this unit is graded — and what makes it different

Unit exam Fri Feb 19: 30 multiple choice (no calculator) \(+\) 1 long and 1 short free-response, 76 minutes, 44 points.

At 11–15% this is the second-heaviest unit in the course, behind only Unit 3. It is also the longest — nine blocks — because it is really three units stacked: weak-acid equilibria, buffers, and titrations.

None of it is new machinery. A weak acid problem is a Unit 7 ICE table with \(K_a\) instead of \(K\). A buffer is that same equilibrium with the conjugate base already present. A titration is a stoichiometry problem followed by whichever equilibrium survives. The skill being tested is deciding which of those four situations you are in before you calculate anything.

By the end of this unit you can…

  • Identify Brønsted–Lowry conjugate acid–base pairs and relate \(K_a\) to \(K_b\). CED 8.1
  • Calculate pH and pOH for strong acids and bases. CED 8.2
  • Solve weak acid and weak base equilibria with an ICE table. CED 8.3
  • Predict the products of an acid–base reaction and recognize a buffer. CED 8.4
  • Analyse a titration curve region by region and choose an indicator. CED 8.5
  • Explain acid strength from molecular structure. CED 8.6
  • Use p\(K_a\) to rank strength and predict salt behaviour. CED 8.7
  • Explain how a buffer resists pH change. CED 8.8
  • Apply the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation. CED 8.9
  • Compare buffering capacity and design a buffer for a target pH. CED 8.10
  • Predict qualitatively how pH affects the solubility of a salt. CED 8.11
Where students lose points in this unit

Reporting \(-\log x\) as the pH for a weak base (it is the pOH); forgetting to double \([\ce{OH-}]\) for \(\ce{Ba(OH)2}\) and \(\ce{Ca(OH)2}\); skipping the 5% check; putting added strong base into an ICE table instead of reacting it to completion first; inverting the \(\log([\text{base}]/[\text{acid}])\) ratio; assuming a titration's equivalence point is at pH 7; and calculating solubility from pH, which topic 8.11 explicitly excludes.
