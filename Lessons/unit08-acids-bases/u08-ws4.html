Worksheet 4 • Buffers and Henderson–Hasselbalch

CED 8.8–8.10 • stoichiometry first, then Henderson–Hasselbalch

Directions & data
p\(K_a\): \(\ce{HF}\) 3.14 • \(\ce{HNO2}\) 3.40 •\ \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) 4.74 • \(\ce{HOCl}\) 7.46 • \(\ce{HCN}\) 9.21 • \(\ce{NH4+}\) 9.25. \(K_a(\ce{HC2H3O2}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}\), \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
1. CED 8.9 Derive the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation from the \(K_a\) expression, showing each step.
2. CED 8.9 Calculate the pH of each buffer:
  1. 0.20 M \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(+\) 0.20 M \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\): 4.74
  2. 0.10 M \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(+\) 0.30 M \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\):
    pH \(= 4.74 + \log(0.30/0.10) = 4.74 + 0.48 = \mathbf{5.22}\)
  3. 0.50 M \(\ce{HF}\) \(+\) 0.25 M \(\ce{NaF}\): 2.84
  4. 0.50 M \(\ce{NH4Cl}\) \(+\) 0.25 M \(\ce{NH3}\):
    pH \(= 9.25 + \log(0.25/0.50) = 9.25 - 0.30 = \mathbf{8.95}\)
3. CED 8.9 For each buffer in problem 2, state without calculating whether its pH is above, below, or equal to p\(K_a\), and why.
4. CED 8.10 1.00 L of a buffer is 0.50 M in \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) and 0.50 M in \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\).
  1. Initial pH: 4.74
  2. Add 0.010 mol \(\ce{NaOH}\). Write the reaction and the new amounts.
  3. New pH:
    pH \(= 4.74 + \log(0.51/0.49) = 4.74 + 0.02 = \mathbf{4.76}\)
  4. Instead add 0.010 mol \(\ce{HCl}\). New pH: 4.72
  5. Add 0.010 mol \(\ce{NaOH}\) to 1.00 L of pure water instead. pH: 12.00
  6. Compare the pH changes in (c) and (e) and state the point.
5. CED 8.10 State the two-step method and say what makes each step different.
6. CED 8.10 A student writes: “Adding 0.010 mol \(\ce{NaOH}\) to the acetate buffer, I put 0.010 into the ICE table as the initial \([\ce{OH-}]\) and solved for \(x\).” Identify the error and describe the correct first move.
7. CED 8.10 Buffering capacity. Two acetate buffers each receive 0.01 mol of \(\ce{H+}\):
  1. Buffer A is 1.00 M in each component. Ratio before and after: 1.00, then 0.98
  2. Buffer B is 1.00 M in \(\ce{C2H3O2^-}\) and 0.01 M in \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\). Ratio before and after: 100, then 49.5
  3. Which resists better, and by roughly what factor in percentage change of the ratio?
  4. State the general condition for optimal buffering and the useful range. equal amounts, pH \(=\) p\(K_a\); p\(K_a \pm 1\)
8. CED 8.10 Buffer design. For each target pH, choose the best acid from the data strip and give the required \([\text{base}]/[\text{acid}]\) ratio:
  1. pH 3.50: \(\ce{HNO2}\), ratio 1.3
  2. pH 7.50: \(\ce{HOCl}\), ratio 1.1
  3. pH 9.00: \(\ce{HCN}\), ratio 0.62
  4. Explain why \(\ce{HCN}\) would be a poor choice for a pH 3.50 buffer even though a ratio can be calculated.
9. CED 8.8 Two acetate buffers: X is 1.0 M in each component, Y is 0.010 M in each.
  1. Which has the higher pH? neither — both 4.74
  2. Which is the better buffer, and why?
