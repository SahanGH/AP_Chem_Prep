Unit 8 • Acids and Bases

Unit Exam

CED 8.1–8.11 • Fri Feb 19 • 76 minutes • 44 points

Format and scope

Section I: 30 multiple choice, no calculator, 45 minutes. Section II: one long and one short free response, calculator permitted, 31 minutes.

Per the CED exclusion on topic 8.11, pH and solubility is assessed qualitatively only — predict a direction and justify it, never compute a solubility from a pH.

Section I • Multiple Choice 30 questions • 45 minutes • 1 point each

  1. a proton donor
  2. a substance that produces \(\ce{OH-}\) in water

  4. any substance with pH below 7

  4. \(\ce{OH-}\)

  1. 1
  2. \(K_w\)

  4. lower its concentration
5. Which is not one of the six strong acids?
  1. \(\ce{HClO4}\)
  2. \(\ce{HNO3}\)
  3. \(\ce{HF}\)
  4. \(\ce{HBr}\)
6. Pure water at 50 °C has pH slightly below 7. This water is
  1. acidic
  2. neutral, because \([\ce{H+}] = [\ce{OH-}]\)
  3. basic
  4. impossible
7. The pH of 0.010 M \(\ce{Ba(OH)2}\) is
  1. 12.00
  2. 12.30
  3. 11.70
  4. 2.00
8. A solution with pH 3 is how many times more acidic than one with pH 6?
  1. 2

9. If pOH \(= 4.20\), the pH is
  1. 4.20
  2. 9.80
  3. 10.20
  4. \(-4.20\)
10. Which solution has the lowest pH?
  1. 0.10 M \(\ce{HCl}\)

  4. 0.10 M \(\ce{HCN}\)
11. For a weak acid, the small-\(x\) approximation gives \([\ce{H+}]\) approximately equal to
  1. \(\sqrt{K_aC_0}\)

  4. \(C_0\)
12. When solving a weak base problem, the \(x\) from the ICE table is

  2. decrease
  3. stay the same
  4. become zero

  2. only one proton is acidic
  3. \(K_{a2}\) is zero

15. Which is the strongest acid?
  1. \(\ce{HClO}\)
  2. \(\ce{HClO2}\)
  3. \(\ce{HClO3}\)

16. \(\ce{HF}\) is the weakest hydrohalic acid mainly because

17. An aqueous solution of \(\ce{NH4Cl}\) is

20. Adding \(\ce{NaF}\) to a solution of \(\ce{HF}\) causes the pH to

22. A buffer with equal concentrations of both components has

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 31 minutes

Data
\(K_a\): \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(3.5\times10^{-8}\) (p\(K_a\) 7.46) •\ \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) (p\(K_a\) 4.74) •\ \(K_b(\ce{NH3}) = 1.8\times10^{-5}\). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
Indicator ranges: methyl red 4.4–6.2 • bromthymol blue 6.0–7.6 • phenolphthalein 8.2–10.0.

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A 25.00 mL sample of 0.100 M \(\ce{HOCl}\) is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).

  1. Calculate the volume of \(\ce{NaOH}\) required to reach equivalence.
  2. Calculate the pH before any base is added.
  3. Calculate the pH after 12.50 mL and explain why the value could have been predicted without calculation.
  4. Calculate the pH after 20.00 mL.
  5. Calculate the pH at the equivalence point.
  6. Select an indicator from the list and justify.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

1.00 L of a buffer is 0.30 M in \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) and 0.30 M in \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\).

  1. Calculate the initial pH.
  2. 0.060 mol of \(\ce{NaOH}\) is added. Write the reaction and calculate the new pH.
  3. \(\ce{CaCO3}\) and \(\ce{AgCl}\) are each treated with dilute \(\ce{HNO3}\). Predict the effect on each solubility and justify the difference. Do not calculate.
