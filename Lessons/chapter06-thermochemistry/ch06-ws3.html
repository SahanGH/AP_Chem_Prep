1. Definitions:
  1. Define standard enthalpy of formation precisely.
  2. Why is \(\Delta H^\circ_f\) of \(\ce{O2(g)}\) zero, while that of \(\ce{O3(g)}\) is not?
  3. Write the formation equation for \(\ce{NO2(g)}\). \(\ce{1/2 N2(g) + O2(g) -> NO2(g)}\)
2. Compute \(\Delta H^\circ\) for each reaction:
  1. \(\ce{2 NH3(g) -> N2(g) + 3 H2(g)}\)
    \([0] - [2(-46.1)] = +92.2\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  2. \(\ce{2 Al(s) + Fe2O3(s) -> Al2O3(s) + 2 Fe(s)}\)
    \([(-1676)+0] - [0+(-826)] = -850\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  3. \(\ce{2 C2H6(g) + 7 O2(g) -> 4 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(l)}\)
    \([4(-393.5)+6(-285.8)] - [2(-84.7)+0] = (-1574.0-1714.8) - (-169.4) = -3119.4\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
3. Compute \(\Delta H^\circ\) for methane combustion producing gaseous water: \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2 O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2 H2O(g)}\). Then compare with the liquid-water value of -890.3 kJ and explain the difference.
\([(-393.5)+2(-241.8)] - [(-74.8)] = (-877.1)+74.8 = -802.3\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Less heat is released because producing vapour instead of liquid leaves the \(2 \times 44 = 88\) kJ of vaporization enthalpy unreleased.
4. FRQ (10 points). Hydrazine, \(\ce{N2H4}\), is used as a rocket fuel. Its combustion is \[ \ce{N2H4(l) + O2(g) -> N2(g) + 2 H2O(l)} \qquad \Delta H^\circ = -622\,\mathrm{kJ} \]
  1. Using \(\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{H2O(l)}) = -285.8\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\), determine \(\Delta H^\circ_f\) for \(\ce{N2H4(l)}\).
    \(-622 = [0 + 2(-285.8)] - [\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{N2H4}) + 0]\), so \(\Delta H^\circ_f(\ce{N2H4}) = -571.6 + 622 = +50.4\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\)
  2. Explain what the sign of your answer means about hydrazine.
  3. Calculate the heat released when 32.0 g of \(\ce{N2H4}\) (\(M = 32.05\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) burns completely.
    \(n = 32.0/32.05 = 0.998\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(q = 0.998 \times (-622) = -621\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  4. If the reaction instead produced gaseous water, would the magnitude of \(\Delta H^\circ\) be larger or smaller? Justify quantitatively.
  5. The combustion is run in a coffee-cup calorimeter containing 500.0 g of water initially at 25.0 °C. If 0.0500 mol of hydrazine burns and all the heat is absorbed by the water, find the final temperature.
    \(q = 0.0500 \times 622 = 31.1\,\mathrm{kJ}\) released; \(\Delta T = 31100/[(500.0)(4.184)] = 14.9\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\); \(T_f = 39.9\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\)
