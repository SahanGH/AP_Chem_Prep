Chapter 6 • Thermochemistry

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §6.1–§6.4 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope

Covers CED 6.1–6.4 (§6.1–6.2), 6.6 (§6.2), 6.8 (§6.4) and 6.9 (§6.3). Two neighbouring CED topics are sourced from other chapters and are not examined here: 6.5 (energy of phase changes) comes from §10.8, and 6.7 (bond enthalpies) from §8.8. §6.5–6.6 of Zumdahl (energy sources) are context and are not assessed.

Take the specific heat of water as 4.18 J/g/°C and assume dilute aqueous solutions have the density and specific heat of water.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A student mixes 50.0 mL of 1.00 M \(\ce{HCl}\) with 50.0 mL of 1.00 M \(\ce{NaOH}\) in a polystyrene cup. Both solutions start at 21.0 °C. The highest temperature reached is 27.8 °C.

  1. Write the net ionic equation for the reaction.
  2. Calculate the heat released by the reaction.
  3. Calculate the enthalpy of neutralization in kJ/mol, and state its sign.
  4. The student repeats the experiment using 100.0 mL of each solution at the same concentration. State what happens to the temperature change and to \(\Delta H\) per mole, and explain.
  5. Give one reason why the measured magnitude of \(\Delta H\) is typically smaller than the accepted value.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Consider the following data at 298 K:
Reaction\(\Delta H^\circ\) (kJ)
\(\ce{C(s) + 1/2O2(g) -> CO(g)}\)\(-110.5\)
\(\ce{CO(g) + 1/2O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\)\(-283.0\)
  1. Use Hess's law to determine \(\Delta H^\circ\) for \(\ce{C(s) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\).
  2. Explain why this value is also the standard enthalpy of formation of \(\ce{CO2}\).
  3. State the value of \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) for \(\ce{O2(g)}\), with a reason.
  4. Explain why Hess's law works — that is, why enthalpy changes may be added in this way.
  5. If the second reaction were reversed, state the value of its \(\Delta H^\circ\) and explain.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A 125 g block of an unknown metal at 95.0 °C is dropped into 200.0 g of water at 22.0 °C. The final temperature of both is 27.1 °C.

  1. Calculate the specific heat of the metal.
  2. State the assumption about the surroundings that this calculation requires.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. When ammonium nitrate dissolves in water, the beaker becomes cold to the touch. State the sign of \(\Delta H\) for the dissolution and explain the direction of heat flow.
  2. On an energy diagram for this process, state whether the products lie above or below the reactants, and why.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

For the combustion of methane, \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2H2O(l)}\), use \(\Delta H_f^\circ\): \(\ce{CH4(g)}\) \(= -74.8\), \(\ce{CO2(g)}\) \(= -393.5\), \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) \(= -285.8\) kJ/mol.

  1. Calculate \(\Delta H^\circ\) for the reaction.
