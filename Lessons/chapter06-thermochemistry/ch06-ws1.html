Chapter 6 • Thermochemistry

Worksheet 1 • Energy, Enthalpy, Calorimetry

Zumdahl §6.1–6.2 • every sign is written from the system's point of view

Directions & data
State the sign of every energy quantity explicitly, and say which way the energy moved. A magnitude with no sign is an incomplete answer.
1. Assign the sign of \(q\) and of \(w\) for the system in each case:
  1. A reaction makes its flask feel hot. \(q < 0\) (exothermic)
  2. A gas is compressed by a piston. \(w > 0\)
  3. An instant cold pack absorbs heat from your hand. \(q > 0\) for the pack
  4. A gas expands, lifting a weight. \(w < 0\)
2. First law calculations:
  1. A system absorbs 650 J of heat and does 225 J of work on its surroundings. Find \(\Delta E\). \(+425\) J
  2. A system releases 1.20 kJ of heat while 450 J of work is done on it. Find \(\Delta E\). \(-750\) J
  3. A gas expands from 1.5 L to 4.5 L against 2.0 atm. Find \(w\) in L·atm and in joules (1 L·atm \(=\) 101.3 J).
    \(w = -(2.0)(3.0) = -6.0\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm} = -608\,\mathrm{J}\)
3. Explain why \(\Delta E\) is a state function but \(q\) and \(w\) separately are not. Use an everyday analogy.
4. Specific heat calculations. For water, \(c = 4.184\,\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}\).
  1. Heat needed to raise 250. g of water from 22.0 °C to 98.0 °C:
    \(q = (250.)(4.184)(76.0) = 7.95e4\,\mathrm{J} = 79.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  2. Heat released when 75.0 g of water cools from 60.0 °C to 25.0 °C: -1.10e4 J
  3. Final temperature when 5.02 kJ is added to 120. g of water initially at 20.0 °C:
    \(\Delta T = 5020/[(120.)(4.184)] = 10.0\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\); \(T_f = 30.0\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\)
5. A 45.0 g sample of an unknown metal at 95.0 °C is placed in 80.0 g of water at 20.0 °C. The final temperature is 23.5 °C.
  1. Heat gained by the water: 1172 J
  2. Specific heat of the metal:
    \(q_{\text{metal}} = -1172\,\mathrm{J}\); \(c = 1172/[(45.0)(71.5)] = 0.364\,\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}\)
  3. The metal's temperature changed by 71.5 °C while the water's changed by only 3.5 °C. Explain.
6. 50.0 mL of 1.00 M \(\ce{HCl}\) at 22.0 °C is mixed with 50.0 mL of 1.00 M \(\ce{NaOH}\) at 22.0 °C in a coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature rises to 28.8 °C. Assume the solution has the density and specific heat of water.
  1. Heat absorbed by the solution:
    \(q = (100.0)(4.184)(6.8) = 2845\,\mathrm{J} = 2.85\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  2. Moles of water formed: 0.0500 mol
  3. \(\Delta H\) per mole, with sign: -56.9 kJ/mol
  4. Give one reason the measured magnitude is slightly smaller than the accepted 57.3 kJ/mol.
