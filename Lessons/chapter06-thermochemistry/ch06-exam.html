Chapter 6 • Thermochemistry

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §6.1–6.4 • 50 minutes • formation data provided with Section II

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. A reaction is run in a beaker, and the beaker becomes noticeably colder. For the reaction,
  1. \(q < 0\) and the process is exothermic
  2. \(q > 0\) and the process is endothermic
  3. \(q = 0\)
  4. \(q > 0\) and the process is exothermic
2. A gas expands against a constant external pressure. The work done is
  1. positive, because the gas gained energy
  2. negative, because the system did work on the surroundings
  3. zero, because pressure is constant
  4. negative, because the gas was compressed
3. A system absorbs 500 J of heat and has 200 J of work done on it. Its change in internal energy is
  1. 300 J
  2. 700 J
  3. -300 J
  4. -700 J
4. Which quantity is NOT a state function?
  1. internal energy
  2. enthalpy
  3. work
  4. temperature
5. Energy transferred between two objects because of a temperature difference is called
  1. heat
  2. work
  3. enthalpy
  4. internal energy
6. The enthalpy change of a reaction equals the heat transferred when the reaction is carried out at constant
  1. volume
  2. pressure
  3. temperature
  4. internal energy
7. For \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2 O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2 H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -890\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Burning 0.500 mol of \(\ce{CH4}\) releases
  1. 890 kJ
  2. 445 kJ
  3. 1780 kJ
  4. 222 kJ
8. Which substance requires the most energy to raise 1 g of it by 1 °C?
  1. water, \(c = 4.184\)
  2. aluminium, \(c = 0.897\)
  3. copper, \(c = 0.385\)
  4. gold, \(c = 0.129\)
9. 100. g of water is warmed by 10.0 °C. The heat absorbed is closest to
  1. 418 J
  2. 4180 J
  3. 41.8 J
  4. 41800 J
10. In a coffee-cup calorimeter the solution warms up. For the reaction,
  1. \(q_{\text{rxn}}\) is negative and equals \(-q_{\text{solution}}\)
  2. \(q_{\text{rxn}}\) is positive and equals \(q_{\text{solution}}\)
  3. \(q_{\text{rxn}} = 0\)
  4. \(q_{\text{rxn}}\) cannot be determined
11. Two samples absorb the same quantity of heat. The one with the smaller specific heat capacity will show
  1. a larger temperature change
  2. a smaller temperature change
  3. no temperature change
  4. the same temperature change
12. Hess's law is a direct consequence of the fact that enthalpy is
  1. always negative
  2. a state function
  3. measured at constant volume
  4. proportional to temperature
13. If \(\ce{A -> B}\) has \(\Delta H = -150\,\mathrm{kJ}\), then \(\ce{B -> A}\) has \(\Delta H =\)
  1. -150 kJ
  2. +150 kJ
  3. -300 kJ
  4. 0 kJ
14. For \(\ce{2 X -> 2 Y}\), \(\Delta H = -80\,\mathrm{kJ}\). For \(\ce{X -> Y}\), \(\Delta H =\)
  1. -80 kJ
  2. -40 kJ
  3. -160 kJ
  4. +40 kJ
15. Two reactions sum to a target reaction. The target's \(\Delta H\) is
  1. the average of the two
  2. the sum of the two
  3. the difference of the two
  4. independent of the two
16. The standard enthalpy of formation of \(\ce{Br2(l)}\) is
  1. 0 kJ/mol
  2. positive
  3. negative
  4. equal to that of \(\ce{Br2(g)}\)
17. Which equation correctly represents the formation of \(\ce{NH3(g)}\)?
  1. \(\ce{N2(g) + 3 H2(g) -> 2 NH3(g)}\)
  2. \(\ce{1/2 N2(g) + 3/2 H2(g) -> NH3(g)}\)
  3. \(\ce{N(g) + 3 H(g) -> NH3(g)}\)
  4. \(\ce{NH3(l) -> NH3(g)}\)
18. \(\Delta H^\circ_{\text{rxn}}\) is calculated as
  1. reactants minus products
  2. products minus reactants, each weighted by its coefficient
  3. the sum of all \(\Delta H^\circ_f\) values
  4. the average of products and reactants
19. \(\Delta H^\circ_f\) for \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) is \(-285.8\) and for \(\ce{H2O(g)}\) is \(-241.8\) kJ/mol. The difference of 44 kJ/mol corresponds to
  1. the bond energy of \(\ce{O-H}\)
  2. the enthalpy of vaporization of water
  3. an experimental error
  4. the ionization energy of water
20. A reaction has \(\Delta H^\circ = +92\,\mathrm{kJ}\). This reaction
  1. releases heat and warms its surroundings
  2. absorbs heat and cools its surroundings
  3. does no work
  4. cannot occur

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

A student mixes 100.0 mL of 0.500 M \(\ce{HCl}\) with 100.0 mL of 0.500 M \(\ce{NaOH}\) in a coffee-cup calorimeter. Both solutions start at 21.0 °C; the mixture reaches 24.4 °C. Assume the solution has a density of 1.00 g/mL and a specific heat of 4.184 J/g/°C.

  1. Calculate the heat absorbed by the solution.
  2. Determine the moles of water formed.
  3. Calculate \(\Delta H\) per mole of water formed, with the correct sign.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

Ethane burns according to \[ \ce{2 C2H6(g) + 7 O2(g) -> 4 CO2(g) + 6 H2O(l)} \]

  1. Calculate \(\Delta H^\circ\) for this reaction using the formation data provided.
  2. Determine the heat released when 15.0 g of ethane (\(M = 30.07\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) burns.
  3. If the reaction produced gaseous rather than liquid water, would the magnitude of \(\Delta H^\circ\) increase or decrease? Justify quantitatively.
