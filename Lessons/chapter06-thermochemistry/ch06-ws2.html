Chapter 6 • Thermochemistry

Worksheet 2 • Hess's Law

Zumdahl §6.3 • write the target first, then work backwards

Directions & data
For every problem, show which given equation you reversed or scaled and by what factor. An answer with no manipulation shown cannot be checked — or given partial credit.
1. State the two rules for manipulating a thermochemical equation, and give the physical reason for each.
2. Given \(\ce{N2(g) + 3 H2(g) -> 2 NH3(g)}\), \(\Delta H = -92.2\,\mathrm{kJ}\), find \(\Delta H\) for:
  1. \(\ce{2 NH3(g) -> N2(g) + 3 H2(g)}\): +92.2 kJ
  2. \(\ce{1/2 N2(g) + 3/2 H2(g) -> NH3(g)}\): -46.1 kJ
  3. \(\ce{2 N2(g) + 6 H2(g) -> 4 NH3(g)}\): -184.4 kJ
3. Verify Hess's law using Zumdahl's nitrogen oxides. Given \begin{align*} \ce{N2(g) + O2(g) &-> 2 NO(g)} &\Delta H &= 180\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \ce{2 NO(g) + O2(g) &-> 2 NO2(g)} &\Delta H &= -112\,\mathrm{kJ} \end{align*}
  1. Add the two equations and write the net reaction.
  2. Compute \(\Delta H\) for the net reaction. +68 kJ
  3. What property of enthalpy guarantees this equals the directly measured value? it is a state function
4. Determine \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{C(s) + 1/2 O2(g) -> CO(g)}\) given \begin{align*} \text{(i)}\ \ce{C(s) + O2(g) &-> CO2(g)} &\Delta H &= -393.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \text{(ii)}\ \ce{2 CO(g) + O2(g) &-> 2 CO2(g)} &\Delta H &= -566.0\,\mathrm{kJ} \end{align*}
Reverse and halve (ii): \(\ce{CO2 -> CO + 1/2 O2}\), \(\Delta H = +283.0\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Add to (i); \(\ce{CO2}\) cancels. \(\Delta H = -393.5 + 283.0 = -110.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
5. Determine \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{2 C(s) + H2(g) -> C2H2(g)}\) given \begin{align*} \text{(i)}\ \ce{C2H2(g) + 5/2 O2(g) &-> 2 CO2(g) + H2O(l)} &\Delta H &= -1300.\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \text{(ii)}\ \ce{C(s) + O2(g) &-> CO2(g)} &\Delta H &= -393.5\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \text{(iii)}\ \ce{H2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) &-> H2O(l)} &\Delta H &= -286\,\mathrm{kJ} \end{align*}
Reverse (i): \(+1300.\); take \(2\times\)(ii): \(-787.0\); take (iii) as written: \(-286\). Sum: \(\Delta H = +227\,\mathrm{kJ}\) — acetylene is endothermic to form.
6. Determine \(\Delta H\) for \(\ce{N2H4(l) + O2(g) -> N2(g) + 2 H2O(l)}\) given \begin{align*} \text{(i)}\ \ce{N2H4(l) + O2(g) &-> N2(g) + 2 H2O(g)} &\Delta H &= -534\,\mathrm{kJ}\\ \text{(ii)}\ \ce{H2O(l) &-> H2O(g)} &\Delta H &= +44\,\mathrm{kJ} \end{align*}
Reverse (ii) and double it: \(\ce{2 H2O(g) -> 2 H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta H = -88\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Add to (i): \(\Delta H = -534 - 88 = -622\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
7. A student solving problem 6 forgets to double the reversed step and reports -578 kJ. Explain why the doubling is required.
