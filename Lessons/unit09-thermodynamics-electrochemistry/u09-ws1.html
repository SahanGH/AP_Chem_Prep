Worksheet 1 • Entropy

CED 9.1–9.2 • count the gas moles before anything else

Directions & data
\(\Delta S^\circ = \sum nS^\circ_{\text{prod}} - \sum nS^\circ_{\text{react}}\) • \(S^\circ\) in J/K/mol: \(\ce{H2}\) 131 • \(\ce{O2}\) 205 • \(\ce{N2}\) 192 • \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) 70 •\ \(\ce{H2O(g)}\) 189 • \(\ce{NH3}\) 193 • \(\ce{NO2}\) 240 • \(\ce{N2O4}\) 304 • \(\ce{CaCO3}\) 93 • \(\ce{CaO}\) 40 • \(\ce{CO2}\) 214.
1. CED 9.1 What entropy measures.
  1. Complete: entropy measures how dispersed the energy and matter of a system are.
  2. Explain why “a messy room has high entropy” is a poor analogy.
  3. Rank by absolute entropy at the same temperature: \(\ce{H2O(s)}\), \(\ce{H2O(l)}\), \(\ce{H2O(g)}\). \(\ce{H2O(s)}\) \(<\) \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) \(<\) \(\ce{H2O(g)}\)
2. CED 9.1 Predict the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) and give the controlling reason in three words or fewer:
  1. \(\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}\) \(-\); 3 mol gas to 0
  2. \(\ce{NH4NO3(s) -> NH4+(aq) + NO3^-(aq)}\) \(+\); crystal disperses
  3. \(\ce{CO2(s) -> CO2(g)}\) \(+\); sublimation
  4. \(\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}\) \(-\); 3 mol gas to 2
  5. \(\ce{H2(g) + Cl2(g) -> 2HCl(g)}\) \(\approx 0\); 2 mol gas to 2
3. CED 9.1 Part (e) above has no change in the number of gas moles. Explain what that implies about the magnitude of \(\Delta S^\circ\) and why the sign is hard to predict by inspection.
4. CED 9.1 The second law.
  1. State it as an inequality. \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}} > 0\) for a favored process
  2. Water freezing at -10 °C is favored, yet the water's entropy falls. Resolve this.
  3. True or false: a reaction with \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}} < 0\) can never be favored. false — see part (b)
5. CED 9.2 The third law.
  1. A perfect crystal at 0 K has entropy exactly zero.
  2. \(S^\circ\) for \(\ce{O2(g)}\) is 205 J/K/mol, but \(\Delta H_f^\circ\) for \(\ce{O2(g)}\) is zero. Explain why the two conventions differ.
6. CED 9.2 Calculate \(\Delta S^\circ\) for each:
  1. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\)
    \((40 + 214) - 93 = \mathbf{+161}\) J/K
  2. \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) -> 2NH3(g)}\)
    \(2(193) - [192 + 3(131)] = 386 - 585 = \mathbf{-199}\) J/K
  3. \(\ce{H2O(l) -> H2O(g)}\) \(+119\) J/K
  4. \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> N2O4(g)}\) \(-176\) J/K
  5. \(\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}\)
    \(2(70) - [2(131) + 205] = 140 - 467 = \mathbf{-327}\) J/K
7. CED 9.2 A student computes \(\Delta S^\circ\) for the Haber process as \(193 - (192 + 131) = -130\) J/K. Identify both errors and give the correct value.
8. CED 9.2 Check each sign you predicted in problem 2 against the value you calculated in problem 6 where both exist. State what this confirms about the gas-mole rule.
