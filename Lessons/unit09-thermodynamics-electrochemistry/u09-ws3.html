Unit 9 • Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry

Worksheet 3 • Free Energy, K, and Coupling

CED 9.5–9.7 • ΔG is not ΔG°

Directions & data
\(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\) •\ \(\Delta G = \Delta G^\circ + RT\ln Q\) •\ \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\) — note joules • free energies of coupled steps add.
1. CED 9.5 Complete the correspondence:
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ\) negative \(\Rightarrow\) \(K\) \(> 1\) \(\Rightarrow\) products favored
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ\) zero \(\Rightarrow\) \(K\) \(= 1\)
  3. \(\Delta G^\circ\) positive \(\Rightarrow\) \(K\) \(< 1\) \(\Rightarrow\) reactants favored
  4. Why must \(\Delta G^\circ\) be converted to joules before using \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\)? \(R\) is in J/(mol\(\cdot\)K)
2. CED 9.5 Reaction A has \(\Delta G^\circ = -5\) kJ; reaction B has \(\Delta G^\circ = -50\) kJ. Compare their equilibrium constants qualitatively, without a calculator.
3. CED 9.5 \(\Delta G\) against \(\Delta G^\circ\).
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ\) refers to standard conditions; \(\Delta G\) refers to the current mixture.
  2. \(Q < K\): sign of \(\Delta G\) is negative and the reaction shifts forward.
  3. \(Q = K\): \(\Delta G = \) 0
  4. \(Q > K\): sign of \(\Delta G\) is positive
  5. A student writes “at equilibrium \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\).” Correct them.
4. CED 9.5 A reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ = +20\) kJ. A student concludes it can never proceed forward under any circumstances. Explain why that is wrong.
5. CED 9.5 For each, state whether the quantity is fixed at a given temperature or depends on the current composition:
  1. \(K\): fixed
  2. \(Q\): depends on mixture
  3. \(\Delta G^\circ\): fixed
  4. \(\Delta G\): depends on mixture
6. CED 9.6 Free energy of dissolution. Give the sign of the contribution named:
  1. \(\Delta H\) of breaking up the lattice: positive
  2. \(\Delta S\) of breaking up the lattice: positive
  3. \(\Delta S\) of solvent reorganizing around the ions: negative
  4. \(\Delta H\) of ion–solvent attraction: negative
7. CED 9.6 \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) dissolves endothermically — the beaker gets cold — yet it is very soluble. Account for this using both terms of \(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\).
8. CED 9.6 The CED does not ask you to predict the overall \(\Delta G^\circ\) of dissolution from scratch. State the reason, and say what you are expected to produce instead.
9. CED 9.7 Driving an unfavorable process.
  1. Name the two general strategies.
  2. External energy driving electrolysis of water: electrical
  3. External energy driving photosynthesis: light
  4. Free energies of coupled steps add, provided the steps share a common intermediate.
10. CED 9.7 Phosphorylating glucose has \(\Delta G^\circ = +13.8\) kJ/mol. ATP hydrolysis has \(\Delta G^\circ = -30.5\) kJ/mol.
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the coupled process:
    \(+13.8 + (-30.5) = \mathbf{-16.7}\) kJ/mol — favored
  2. Which species is the common intermediate? \(\ce{P_i}\) (phosphate)
  3. Explain what “ATP is the cell's energy currency” actually means.
11. CED 9.7 Reaction X has \(\Delta G^\circ = +25\) kJ; reaction Y has \(\Delta G^\circ = -18\) kJ. A student couples them and reports the pair as favored. Evaluate.

  3. A catalyst changes: \(E_a\) only
  4. \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) of an element in its standard state: 0
