Unit 9 • Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry

Worksheet 2 • Gibbs Free Energy

CED 9.3–9.4 • convert the entropy to kJ before you subtract

Directions & data
\(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\) •\ crossover \(T = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ\) •\ \(\Delta G^\circ = \sum n\Delta G_f^\circ{}_{\text{prod}} - \sum n\Delta G_f^\circ{}_{\text{react}}\) • \(\Delta H^\circ\) in kJ, \(\Delta S^\circ\) in J/K • take \(T = 298\,\mathrm{K}\) unless told otherwise.
1. CED 9.3 The four cases. Complete the table without calculating:
  1. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\): favored at all temperatures
  2. \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ < 0\): favored at no temperature
  3. \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\): favored at high \(T\)
  4. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ < 0\): favored at low \(T\)
  5. Which two cases need a crossover temperature calculated, and why?
2. CED 9.3 \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\) has \(\Delta H^\circ = +178.5\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +161\) J/K.
  1. Which of the four cases is this? high \(T\) only
  2. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K.
    \(178.5 - 298(0.161) = 178.5 - 48.0 = \mathbf{+131}\) kJ — not favored
  3. Calculate the crossover temperature.
    \(T = 178.5/0.161 = \mathbf{1109}\) K \(\approx 836\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\)
  4. Relate your answer to why limestone buildings survive but lime kilns work.
3. CED 9.3 The Haber process has \(\Delta H^\circ = -92\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = -199\) J/K.
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K:
    \(-92 - 298(-0.199) = -92 + 59 = \mathbf{-33}\) kJ — favored
  2. Crossover temperature: 462 K
  3. Is the reaction favored above or below that temperature? Explain.
  4. Industrial ammonia synthesis is run at about 700 K, above the crossover. Suggest why, given Unit 5.
4. CED 9.3 For \(\ce{H2O(l) -> H2O(g)}\), \(\Delta H^\circ = +44\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +119\) J/K. Calculate the crossover temperature and state what it physically is.
\(T = 44/0.119 = \mathbf{370}\) K \(\approx 97\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\) — the normal boiling point. The tabulated value is 373 K; the difference is rounding in the table.
5. CED 9.3 A student calculates \(\Delta G^\circ = 178.5 - 298(161) = -47{,}800\) kJ for problem 2. Identify the error and state how you would catch it without redoing the arithmetic.
6. CED 9.3 The formation route. For \(\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}\), \(\Delta G_f^\circ(\ce{H2O(l)}) = -237\) kJ/mol.
  1. Why is \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) for \(\ce{H2(g)}\) and \(\ce{O2(g)}\) zero? elements in their standard states
  2. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\). \(-474\) kJ
  3. Route 1 gives \(-475\) kJ from \(\Delta H^\circ = -572\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = -327\) J/K. Verify, and account for the difference.
    \(-572 - 298(-0.327) = -572 + 97.4 = \mathbf{-475}\) kJ. The 1 kJ gap is rounding in the tabulated values, not an error in either method.
  4. State the one place \(S^\circ\) and \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) differ in convention.
7. CED 9.4 Thermodynamic versus kinetic control.
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ\) tells you whether, and how far; \(E_a\) tells you how fast.
  2. Diamond converting to graphite has \(\Delta G^\circ < 0\). Explain why jewellery survives.
  3. A catalyst changes \(E_a\) but does not change \(\Delta G^\circ\), \(\Delta H^\circ\), or \(K\).
  4. Can a catalyst make an unfavorable reaction favorable? Justify.
8. CED 9.4 A reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ = -200\) kJ and no observable rate at 298 K. A student concludes the value of \(\Delta G^\circ\) must be wrong. Explain why the conclusion does not follow, and give two ways to make the reaction proceed.
Spiral review • Unit 9 • blocks 1–2
  1. Sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) for \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> N2O4(g)}\): negative
  2. \(\Delta S^\circ\) for \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\): \(+161\) J/K
  3. \(S^\circ\) of an element in its standard state: not zero
  4. Second law, as an inequality: \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}} > 0\)
  5. The AP term replacing “spontaneous”: thermodynamically favored
