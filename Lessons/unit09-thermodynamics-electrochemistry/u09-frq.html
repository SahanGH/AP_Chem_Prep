Unit 9 • Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry

Free-Response Set

CED 9.1–9.11 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 9.1 through CED 9.11, worth 7 %–9 % of the exam. This unit joins thermodynamics to electrochemistry through a single bridge, \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\), and the questions below are built to make that link explicit.

Topic CED 9.8 excludes labelling an electrode as positive or negative, since the convention differs between cell types. Anode and cathode are assessed, and the rule never changes: oxidation occurs at the anode in both galvanic and electrolytic cells.

\(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\), \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\). Standard reduction potentials: \(\ce{Ag+}\)/\(\ce{Ag}\) \(= +0.80\) V, \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(= +0.34\) V.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A reaction has \(\Delta H^\circ = +95.0\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) and \(\Delta S^\circ = +150\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\).

  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K and state whether the reaction is thermodynamically favourable there.
  2. Determine the temperature above which it becomes favourable.
  3. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 700 K and hence \(K\) at that temperature.
  4. Explain why a positive \(\Delta S^\circ\) is required for a reaction with positive \(\Delta H^\circ\) ever to become favourable.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A galvanic cell is built from a copper electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Cu(NO3)2}\) and a silver electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\).

  1. Write the half-reaction at each electrode, identify the anode, and give the balanced overall equation.
  2. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) and \(\Delta G^\circ\).
  3. Calculate the cell potential when \([\ce{Cu^2+}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) and \([\ce{Ag+}] = 0.010\,\mathrm{M}\).
  4. Silver is deposited from \(\ce{AgNO3}\) solution by electrolysis at 1.50 A for 20.0 min. Calculate the mass deposited.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

Diamond converting to graphite has \(\Delta G^\circ = -2.9\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) at 298 K, yet diamonds do not visibly change.

  1. Explain how both statements can be true.
  2. State what would have to change for the conversion to proceed at an observable rate.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. Sodium chloride dissolves readily in water with \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) close to zero. Explain what drives the process.
  2. Some ionic solids are insoluble despite a favourable entropy change. State what must be true of \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) in those cases.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

The decomposition of a mineral has \(\Delta G^\circ = +72\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\). Industrially it is coupled to a reaction with \(\Delta G^\circ = -105\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) that shares a common species.

  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the coupled process and state whether it is favourable.
  2. Explain the principle that makes coupling work, and state the condition the two reactions must satisfy.
