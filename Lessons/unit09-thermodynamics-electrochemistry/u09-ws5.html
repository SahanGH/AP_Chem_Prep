Worksheet 5 • Nonstandard Conditions, Electrolysis & FRQ

CED 9.10–9.11 • argue from $Q$ against $K$; no Nernst equation required

Directions & data
\(q = It\) • \(\text{mol e}^- = q/F\) •\ \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\) •\ \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}\) • \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\) • \(E^\circ\) (V): \(\ce{Ag+}\)/\(\ce{Ag}\) \(+0.80\) •\ \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(+0.34\) • \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)/\(\ce{Zn}\) \(-0.76\) • molar masses: \(\ce{Ag}\) 107.87, \(\ce{Cu}\) 63.55, \(\ce{Al}\) 26.98.
1. CED 9.10 A cell's voltage as it runs.
  1. As a galvanic cell operates, \(Q\) rises toward \(K\) and \(E_{\text{cell}}\) falls.
  2. The cell is dead when \(Q = K\), at which point \(E_{\text{cell}} = \) 0.
  3. Explain in one sentence what a cell potential physically measures.
2. CED 9.10 For \(\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}\), predict the effect on \(E_{\text{cell}}\) and justify each with \(Q\). Do not calculate.
  1. \([\ce{Cu^2+}]\) is increased. increases — \(Q\) falls
  2. \([\ce{Zn^2+}]\) is increased. decreases — \(Q\) rises
  3. More solid \(\ce{Zn}\) is added. no change — solids omitted from \(Q\)
  4. Write the reasoning behind (a) in full, as an FRQ answer would require.
3. CED 9.10 Concentration cells. Two \(\ce{Cu}\) electrodes sit in 0.10 M and 1.0 M \(\ce{Cu^2+}\).
  1. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) for this cell is 0, because both half-cells are identical.
  2. Yet it produces a voltage. Which half-cell is the anode, and why?
  3. When does it stop? when the concentrations are equal
4. CED 9.11 Faraday's law — the chain. Put the four conversions in order:
  1. First: current and time to charge
  2. Then: charge to moles of electrons
  3. Then: moles of electrons to moles of substance
  4. Finally: moles to grams
  5. Which step needs the half-reaction, and why?
5. CED 9.11 A current of 2.00 A flows for 30.0 min through \(\ce{AgNO3}\). Calculate the mass of silver deposited.
\(t = 1800\) s; \(q = (2.00)(1800) = 3600\) C; mol e\(^- = 3600/96485 = 0.0373\); \(\ce{Ag+ + e^- -> Ag}\) is one electron per atom, so \(m = (0.0373)(107.87) = \mathbf{4.03}\) g
6. CED 9.11 The same charge is passed through \(\ce{CuSO4}\) instead — 1.50 A for 1.00 h.
  1. Calculate the mass of copper deposited.
    \(q = (1.50)(3600) = 5400\) C; mol e\(^- = 5400/96485 = 0.0560\); \(\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e^- -> Cu}\) needs two per atom, so mol Cu \(= 0.0280\) and \(m = (0.0280)(63.55) = \mathbf{1.78}\) g
  2. A student reports 3.56 g. Identify the single omitted step. did not divide by \(n = 2\)
7. CED 9.11 How long, in hours, to deposit 5.00 g of copper at 2.00 A?
mol Cu \(= 5.00/63.55 = 0.0787\); mol e\(^- = 2(0.0787) = 0.157\); \(q = (0.157)(96485) = 15{,}183\) C; \(t = 15{,}183/2.00 = 7591\) s \(= \mathbf{2.11}\) h
8. CED 9.11 Producing aluminium requires three electrons per atom and \(\ce{Al^3+}\)/\(\ce{Al}\) sits at \(-1.66\) V. Explain why aluminium smelters are built beside power stations, and why recycling saves so much energy.
9. FRQ (10 points). CED 9.8 CED 9.9 CED 9.10 A galvanic cell is constructed from a zinc electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Zn(NO3)2}\) and a silver electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\), joined by a salt bridge.
  1. Identify the anode and the cathode, and state the direction of electron flow through the external wire.
  2. Write the balanced overall cell reaction and give \(n\).
  3. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\).
    \(E^\circ = 0.80 - (-0.76) = \mathbf{+1.56}\) V
  4. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) in kJ.
    \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ = -(2)(96485)(1.56) = -301{,}033~\text{J} = \mathbf{-301}~\text{kJ}\)
  5. The \(\ce{AgNO3}\) solution is diluted to 0.10 M. Predict the effect on \(E_{\text{cell}}\) and justify without calculating.
  6. State the value of \(E_{\text{cell}}\) when the cell is exhausted, and the relationship between \(Q\) and \(K\) at that moment. \(E_{\text{cell}} = 0\) and \(Q = K\)
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 for both electrodes correctly assigned, 1 for electron direction stated as anode \(\to\) cathode • (b) 2 pts 1 balanced equation with \(\ce{Ag+}\) doubled, 1 for \(n = 2\) — an unbalanced equation earns 0 for both • (c) 1 pt \(+1.56\) V; using \(2(0.80)\) earns 0 • (d) 2 pts 1 correct substitution into \(-nFE^\circ\) with \(n = 2\), 1 answer converted to kJ — reporting joules as kilojoules earns 0 for that point • (e) 2 pts 1 states that \(Q\) increases because \(\ce{Ag+}\) is a reactant, 1 concludes \(E_{\text{cell}}\) decreases — a bare “decreases” with no \(Q\) reasoning earns 0 of 2 • (f) 1 pt both \(E = 0\) and \(Q = K\) required. Total: \(2 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 10\).
