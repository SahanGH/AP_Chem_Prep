AP Chemistry lessons

Unit 9 • Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry

Unit Exam

CED 9.1–9.11 • Fri Mar 19 • 69 minutes • 39 points

Format and scope

Section I: 25 multiple choice, no calculator, 38 minutes. Section II: one long and one short free response, calculator permitted, 31 minutes.

Per the CED, topic 9.10 is assessed without the Nernst equation — predict the direction of a change and justify it with \(Q\) against \(K\). Topic 9.8 excludes labelling electrodes as positive or negative; identify them as anode and cathode.

Write thermodynamically favored, not “spontaneous”.

Section I • Multiple Choice 25 questions • 38 minutes • 1 point each

1. Entropy is best described as a measure of
  1. the dispersal of energy and matter among available arrangements
  2. the disorder of the objects in a system
  3. the heat content of a system
  4. the rate at which a reaction proceeds
2. Which change has the most positive \(\Delta S^\circ\)?
  1. \(\ce{H2O(g) -> H2O(l)}\)
  2. \(\ce{CO2(s) -> CO2(g)}\)
  3. \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> N2O4(g)}\)
  4. \(\ce{Ag+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) -> AgCl(s)}\)
3. For \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) -> 2NH3(g)}\), \(\Delta S^\circ\) is negative principally because
  1. four moles of gas become two
  2. the reaction is exothermic
  3. ammonia is a polar molecule
  4. the reaction requires a catalyst
4. Which statement about \(S^\circ\) is correct?
  1. \(S^\circ\) for \(\ce{O2(g)}\) is a positive, nonzero value
  2. \(S^\circ\) for any element in its standard state is zero
  3. \(S^\circ\) is negative for all stable compounds
  4. \(S^\circ\) cannot be measured, only changes in it
5. A process has \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}} < 0\) and is thermodynamically favored. It follows that
  1. \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}}\) is positive and larger in magnitude
  2. the second law has been violated
  3. \(\Delta H\) must be positive
  4. \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}}\) is negative
6. \(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\). A reaction with \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\) and \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) is favored
  1. at all temperatures
  2. only at high temperature
  3. only at low temperature
  4. at no temperature
7. A reaction with \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\) and \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) becomes favored
  1. above \(T = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ\)
  2. below \(T = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ\)
  3. at all temperatures
  4. only if a catalyst is added
8. A student computes \(\Delta G^\circ = 178.5 - 298(161)\) and obtains \(-47{,}800\) kJ. The error is that
  1. \(\Delta S^\circ\) was not converted from J/K to kJ/K
  2. the temperature should be in °C
  3. the sign of \(\Delta H^\circ\) is wrong
  4. \(\Delta S^\circ\) should have been divided by \(T\)
9. For a reaction at equilibrium,
  1. \(\Delta G = 0\)
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\)
  3. \(K = 0\)
  4. \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}} > 0\)
10. If \(\Delta G^\circ\) for a reaction is positive, then
  1. \(K < 1\)
  2. \(K > 1\)
  3. \(K = 0\)
  4. the reaction cannot occur under any conditions
11. Reaction A has \(\Delta G^\circ = -5\) kJ and reaction B has \(\Delta G^\circ = -50\) kJ. Compared with \(K_A\), \(K_B\) is
  1. very much larger
  2. ten times larger
  3. ten times smaller
  4. the same
12. A reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ = -180\) kJ but no measurable rate at 298 K. The best explanation is
  1. a large activation energy
  2. an error in the value of \(\Delta G^\circ\)
  3. that \(K\) must be less than 1
  4. that the reaction is endothermic
13. Adding a catalyst to a reaction changes
  1. the activation energy only
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ\) and \(K\)
  3. \(\Delta H^\circ\) only
  4. \(K\) but not the rate
14. \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) dissolves endothermically yet is highly soluble. This is because
  1. \(T\Delta S^\circ\) exceeds \(\Delta H^\circ\)
  2. the lattice energy is negligible
  3. \(\Delta S^\circ\) is negative
  4. dissolving is always favored
15. When an ionic solid dissolves, the reorganization of solvent molecules into hydration shells contributes
  1. a negative \(\Delta S\)
  2. a positive \(\Delta S\)
  3. a positive \(\Delta H\)
  4. nothing to either term
16. An unfavorable reaction is coupled to a favorable one. For the coupled process to be favored, the two reactions must share a common intermediate and
  1. the sum of the two \(\Delta G^\circ\) values must be negative
  2. both reactions must be exothermic
  3. the favorable reaction must be faster
  4. the two \(\Delta G^\circ\) values must be equal in magnitude
17. In any electrochemical cell, oxidation occurs at the
  1. anode
  2. cathode
  3. salt bridge
  4. negative electrode in every case
18. The purpose of a salt bridge is to
  1. carry ions so both solutions stay electrically neutral
  2. carry electrons between the half-cells
  3. prevent the two solutions from reacting
  4. increase the cell potential
19. An electrolytic cell has
  1. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} < 0\) and \(\Delta G^\circ > 0\)
  2. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} > 0\) and \(\Delta G^\circ < 0\)
  3. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} > 0\) and \(\Delta G^\circ > 0\)
  4. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0\) and \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\)
20. Given \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)/\(\ce{Zn}\) \(= -0.76\) V and \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(= +0.34\) V, \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) for \(\ce{Zn + Cu^2+ -> Zn^2+ + Cu}\) is
  1. \(+1.10\) V
  2. \(-1.10\) V
  3. \(-0.42\) V
  4. \(+0.42\) V
21. For \(\ce{Cu + 2Ag+ -> Cu^2+ + 2Ag}\), with \(\ce{Ag+}\)/\(\ce{Ag}\) \(= +0.80\) V and \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(= +0.34\) V, \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) is
  1. \(+0.46\) V
  2. \(+1.26\) V
  3. \(+1.94\) V
  4. \(-0.46\) V
22. Two cells have the same \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) but transfer 1 and 3 electrons respectively. Compared with the first, the second cell has
  1. the same voltage and three times the magnitude of \(\Delta G^\circ\)
  2. three times the voltage and the same \(\Delta G^\circ\)
  3. three times both
  4. the same value of both
23. A galvanic cell has been running for some time. Compared with its initial state,
  1. \(Q\) has increased and \(E_{\text{cell}}\) has decreased
  2. \(Q\) has decreased and \(E_{\text{cell}}\) has increased
  3. \(K\) has decreased
  4. \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) has decreased
24. For \(\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}\), increasing \([\ce{Cu^2+}]\) will
  1. increase \(E_{\text{cell}}\), because \(Q\) decreases
  2. decrease \(E_{\text{cell}}\), because \(Q\) decreases
  3. increase \(E_{\text{cell}}\), because \(E^\circ\) increases
  4. leave \(E_{\text{cell}}\) unchanged
25. Passing one mole of electrons through solutions of \(\ce{Ag+}\), \(\ce{Cu^2+}\), and \(\ce{Al^3+}\) deposits
  1. 1 mol \(\ce{Ag}\), 0.5 mol \(\ce{Cu}\), and \(1/3\) mol \(\ce{Al}\)
  2. one mole of each metal
  3. 1 mol \(\ce{Ag}\), 2 mol \(\ce{Cu}\), and 3 mol \(\ce{Al}\)
  4. amounts that depend on the current used

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 31 minutes • calculator permitted

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A galvanic cell is assembled from a nickel electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Ni(NO3)2}\) and a silver electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\), joined by a salt bridge.

\(E^\circ\): \(\ce{Ag+ + e^- -> Ag}\), \(+0.80\) V •\ \(\ce{Ni^2+ + 2e^- -> Ni}\), \(-0.23\) V •\ \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\)
  1. Identify the anode and the cathode, and give the direction of electron flow through the wire.
  2. Write the balanced overall cell reaction and state \(n\).
  3. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\).
  4. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the cell reaction, in kJ.
  5. The \(\ce{AgNO3}\) solution is diluted to 0.010 M while the nickel half-cell is unchanged. Predict the effect on \(E_{\text{cell}}\) and justify with \(Q\). Do not calculate.
  6. State the mass change at each electrode as the cell runs, and give \(E_{\text{cell}}\) when the cell is exhausted.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

For \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H^\circ = +178.5\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +161\) J/K.

  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K and state whether the reaction is thermodynamically favored.
  2. Calculate the temperature above which the reaction becomes favored.
  3. A student claims that adding a catalyst would allow the decomposition to proceed at 298 K. Evaluate the claim.
