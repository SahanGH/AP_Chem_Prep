Worksheet 4 • Cells and Cell Potential

CED 9.8–9.9 • scale the half-reaction, never its voltage

Directions & data
\(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}\) • \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\) • \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\) • \(E^\circ\) (V): \(\ce{Ag+}\)/\(\ce{Ag}\) \(+0.80\) • \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(+0.34\) • \(\ce{2H+}\)/\(\ce{H2}\) \(0.00\) • \(\ce{Pb^2+}\)/\(\ce{Pb}\) \(-0.13\) • \(\ce{Ni^2+}\)/\(\ce{Ni}\) \(-0.23\) •\ \(\ce{Fe^2+}\)/\(\ce{Fe}\) \(-0.44\) • \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)/\(\ce{Zn}\) \(-0.76\) • \(\ce{Mg^2+}\)/\(\ce{Mg}\) \(-2.37\).

  3. State what the salt bridge does and what happens without it.

2. CED 9.8 Galvanic against electrolytic. Fill in:
  1. Galvanic: \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) positive, \(\Delta G^\circ\) negative
  2. Electrolytic: \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) negative, \(\Delta G^\circ\) positive
  3. Which converts chemical energy to electrical? galvanic
  4. An electrolytic cell is which CED 9.7 strategy, in hardware? external energy supplied

  3. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\).
    \(E^\circ = 0.34 - (-0.76) = \mathbf{+1.10}\) V
  4. State what happens to the mass of each electrode as it runs.

5. CED 9.9 For \(\ce{Cu + 2Ag+ -> Cu^2+ + 2Ag}\):
  1. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\). \(+0.46\) V
  2. A student writes \(2(0.80) - 0.34 = 1.26\) V. Identify the error and state the principle.
  3. Give \(n\) for this reaction, and say where you read it from. \(n = 2\), from the balanced overall equation
6. CED 9.9 Will \(\ce{Cu(s)}\) react with 1 M \(\ce{HCl}\)? Support your answer with a cell potential.
The proposed reaction is \(\ce{Cu + 2H+ -> Cu^2+ + H2}\). Cathode \(\ce{2H+}\)/\(\ce{H2}\) \(= 0.00\); anode \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(= +0.34\). \(E^\circ = 0.00 - 0.34 = \mathbf{-0.34}\) V. Negative, so no — copper does not dissolve in hydrochloric acid.
7. CED 9.9 Reading the table as a ranking.
  1. Stronger oxidizing agent, \(\ce{Ag+}\) or \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)? Justify.
  2. Stronger reducing agent, \(\ce{Mg}\) or \(\ce{Cu}\)? Justify.
  3. Which metal in the strip cannot be oxidized by \(\ce{Ag+}\)? none — \(\ce{Ag+}\) oxidizes all
8. CED 9.9 \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\).
  1. For the Daniell cell (\(E^\circ = +1.10\) V, \(n = 2\)), calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) in kJ.
    \(-(2)(96485)(1.10) = -212{,}267\) J \(= \mathbf{-212}\) kJ
  2. For \(\ce{Cu + 2Ag+ -> Cu^2+ + 2Ag}\) (\(E^\circ = +0.46\) V):
    \(-(2)(96485)(0.46) = -88{,}766\) J \(= \mathbf{-89}\) kJ
  3. Why does the calculation produce joules rather than kilojoules? \(F\) in C/mol, and 1 V \(=\) 1 J/C
  4. Two cells share \(E^\circ = +0.50\) V but transfer 1 and 4 electrons. Compare their voltages and their \(\Delta G^\circ\) values.
9. CED 9.9 Complete the three-way correspondence:
  1. \(E^\circ > 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) \(< 0\), \(K\) \(> 1\), cell is galvanic
  2. \(E^\circ = 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) \(= 0\), \(K\) \(= 1\), cell is dead
  3. \(E^\circ < 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) \(> 0\), \(K\) \(< 1\), cell is electrolytic
  4. A battery is described as “dead”. Is it empty? Explain.
