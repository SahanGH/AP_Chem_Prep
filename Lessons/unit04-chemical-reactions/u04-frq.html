Unit 4 • Chemical Reactions

Free-Response Set

CED 4.1–4.9 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — two CED exclusions apply

Covers CED 4.1 through CED 4.9, worth 7 %–9 % of the exam.

Topic CED 4.7 excludes the terms “oxidizing agent” and “reducing agent”, and excludes rote memorization of solubility rules beyond the short list in 4.7.A.5. Topic CED 4.8 excludes Lewis acid–base concepts; Brønsted–Lowry is the model the exam uses.

You will be asked which species is oxidized and which is reduced — never to name an “agent”.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

40.0 mL of 0.150 M \(\ce{Pb(NO3)2}\) is mixed with 50.0 mL of 0.200 M \(\ce{KI}\). A bright yellow precipitate of \(\ce{PbI2}\) forms (\(M = 461.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)). Assume volumes are additive.

  1. Write the balanced net ionic equation.
  2. Determine the limiting reactant.
  3. Calculate the mass of \(\ce{PbI2}\) formed.
  4. Calculate the concentration of lead(II) ion remaining in solution.
  5. Identify the spectator ions and explain what makes them spectators.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Acidified dichromate oxidizes iron(II) in solution: \[ \ce{Cr2O7^2-(aq) + Fe^2+(aq) + H+(aq) -> Cr^3+(aq) + Fe^3+(aq) + H2O(l)} \quad \text{(unbalanced)} \]

  1. Assign the oxidation number of chromium on each side and state whether it is oxidized or reduced.
  2. Balance the equation in acidic solution.
  3. A 20.00 mL sample of the iron(II) solution required 24.50 mL of 0.0150 M \(\ce{K2Cr2O7}\) to reach the endpoint. Calculate \([\ce{Fe^2+}]\).
  4. State what would happen to the calculated \([\ce{Fe^2+}]\) if the student overshot the endpoint, and why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Classify each as physical or chemical, with a one-line reason: (i) ice melting; (ii) iron rusting; (iii) salt dissolving in water.
  2. Dissolving is sometimes argued to be chemical because ion–dipole bonds form. State the strongest argument that it is physical.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

A 25.00 mL sample of a monoprotic acid \(\ce{HA}\) is titrated with 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). The endpoint occurs at 28.75 mL.

  1. Calculate the concentration of the acid.
  2. Write the net ionic equation for the reaction, given that \(\ce{HA}\) is a weak acid.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethanol, \(\ce{C2H5OH}\).
  2. A particulate diagram of the reactants shows 4 ethanol molecules and 9 oxygen molecules. Determine how many molecules of each species the product diagram should show.
