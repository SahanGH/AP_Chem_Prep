Unit 4 • Chemical Reactions

Unit Exam

Mon Oct 26 • 52 minutes • periodic table and equations sheet provided

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Which process is a chemical change?
  1. ice melting
  2. sugar dissolving in water
  3. \(\ce{HCl}\) gas dissolving in water
  4. dry ice subliming
2. A student observes bubbling when a solid is added to a warm liquid. This observation alone
  1. proves a chemical reaction occurred
  2. is consistent with, but does not prove, a chemical reaction
  3. proves no reaction occurred
  4. indicates a physical change only
3. Melting a molecular solid requires overcoming
  1. covalent bonds within molecules
  2. intermolecular forces between molecules
  3. ionic bonds
  4. metallic bonds
4. A correct particulate diagram of aqueous \(\ce{Na2SO4}\) shows
  1. intact \(\ce{Na2SO4}\) units
  2. twice as many \(\ce{Na+}\) particles as \(\ce{SO4^2-}\) particles, all separated
  3. equal numbers of separated \(\ce{Na+}\) and \(\ce{SO4^2-}\)
  4. separated \(\ce{Na+}\), \(\ce{S^2-}\), and \(\ce{O^2-}\)
5. In a particulate “before and after” pair of diagrams, which quantity must be identical in both boxes?
  1. the number of molecules
  2. the number of atoms of each element
  3. the number of distinct substances
  4. the volume occupied
6. In a particulate diagram of a completed precipitation reaction, the precipitate should be drawn as
  1. separated ions dispersed in solution
  2. a cluster of ions held together
  3. neutral molecules
  4. omitted entirely
7. Which species should be written as separated ions in a complete ionic equation?
  1. \(\ce{H2O(l)}\)
  2. \(\ce{HC2H3O2(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{KNO3(aq)}\)
  4. \(\ce{CaCO3(s)}\)
8. The net ionic equation for mixing \(\ce{Na2CO3(aq)}\) and \(\ce{CaCl2(aq)}\) is
  1. \(\ce{Na+(aq) + Cl^-(aq) -> NaCl(s)}\)
  2. \(\ce{Ca^2+(aq) + CO3^2-(aq) -> CaCO3(s)}\)
  3. \(\ce{Na2CO3(aq) + CaCl2(aq) -> CaCO3(s) + 2 NaCl(aq)}\)
  4. no reaction occurs
9. Which pair of reactants gives the net ionic equation \(\ce{H+(aq) + OH^-(aq) -> H2O(l)}\)?
  1. \(\ce{HNO3(aq)}\) and \(\ce{KOH(aq)}\)
  2. \(\ce{HF(aq)}\) and \(\ce{NaOH(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{HCl(aq)}\) and \(\ce{NH3(aq)}\)
  4. \(\ce{HC2H3O2(aq)}\) and \(\ce{KOH(aq)}\)
10. Mixing which pair produces a precipitate?
  1. \(\ce{KNO3(aq)}\) and \(\ce{NaCl(aq)}\)
  2. \(\ce{AgNO3(aq)}\) and \(\ce{K2CrO4(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{NH4Cl(aq)}\) and \(\ce{NaNO3(aq)}\)
  4. \(\ce{LiBr(aq)}\) and \(\ce{KNO3(aq)}\)
11. The reaction \(\ce{Zn(s) + CuSO4(aq) -> ZnSO4(aq) + Cu(s)}\) is best classified as
  1. precipitation
  2. acid–base
  3. oxidation–reduction
  4. decomposition
12. Two clear solutions are mixed and no precipitate forms. The best conclusion is that
  1. no ions are present
  2. all possible ion combinations are soluble
  3. the solutions were too dilute to react
  4. a gas must have formed instead
13. Which is a weak acid?
  1. \(\ce{HCl}\)
  2. \(\ce{HNO3}\)
  3. \(\ce{HF}\)
  4. \(\ce{HClO4}\)
14. A Bronsted–Lowry base is defined as a substance that
  1. produces hydroxide ions in water
  2. accepts a proton
  3. donates a proton
  4. dissolves to give a conducting solution
15. In the net ionic equation for \(\ce{HF(aq)}\) reacting with \(\ce{NaOH(aq)}\), hydrofluoric acid appears as
  1. \(\ce{HF(aq)}\), an intact molecule
  2. \(\ce{H+(aq)}\) and \(\ce{F^-(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{H+(aq)}\) only
  4. a spectator
16. The oxidation state of nitrogen in \(\ce{NO2-}\) is
  1. \(+5\)
  2. \(+3\)
  3. \(-3\)
  4. \(+4\)
17. In \(\ce{2 Mg(s) + O2(g) -> 2 MgO(s)}\), magnesium is
  1. oxidized and acts as the reducing agent
  2. oxidized and acts as the oxidizing agent
  3. reduced and acts as the reducing agent
  4. reduced and acts as the oxidizing agent
18. Which reaction is NOT a redox reaction?
  1. \(\ce{2 Na + Cl2 -> 2 NaCl}\)
  2. \(\ce{CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O}\)
  3. \(\ce{AgNO3(aq) + NaCl(aq) -> AgCl(s) + NaNO3(aq)}\)
  4. \(\ce{Fe2O3 + 3 CO -> 2 Fe + 3 CO2}\)
19. 25.0 mL of \(\ce{HCl}\) is exactly neutralized by 25.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). The concentration of the acid is
  1. 0.0500 M
  2. 0.100 M
  3. 0.200 M
  4. cannot be determined
20. Titrating \(\ce{H2SO4}\) with \(\ce{NaOH}\) and assuming a 1:1 mole ratio will make the calculated acid concentration
  1. twice the true value
  2. half the true value
  3. unchanged
  4. four times the true value

Section II • Free Response 1 question • 20 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A student mixes 40.0 mL of 0.150 M \(\ce{BaCl2(aq)}\) with 40.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{Na2SO4(aq)}\). A white solid forms.

  1. Write the balanced formula equation, including states.
  2. Write the net ionic equation and identify the spectator ions.
  3. Determine the limiting reactant. Show the comparison.
  4. Calculate the mass of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) (\(M = 233.39\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) produced.
  5. Calculate the concentration of the excess \(\ce{Ba^2+}\) remaining after the reaction. Assume volumes are additive.
  6. Explain why the formation of a white solid is stronger evidence of a chemical reaction than a temperature change would be.
