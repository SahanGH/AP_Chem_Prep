Unit 4 • Chemical Reactions

Worksheet 3 • Redox and Solution Stoichiometry

CED 4.5, 4.9 • assign states before naming agents

Directions & data
Write oxidation states above the symbols before you conclude anything. For stoichiometry, convert volume \(\times\) molarity to moles as the very first step.
1. Assign the oxidation state of the named element:
  1. Mn in \(\ce{MnO2}\): \(+4\)
  2. Cr in \(\ce{Cr2O3}\): \(+3\)
  3. S in \(\ce{SO3^2-}\): \(+4\)
  4. N in \(\ce{NH4+}\): \(-3\)
  5. Cl in \(\ce{ClO2-}\): \(+3\)
  6. O in \(\ce{H2O2}\): \(-1\)
2. For each reaction: is it redox? If so, name what is oxidized, what is reduced, and both agents.
  1. \(\ce{Cu(s) + 2 AgNO3(aq) -> Cu(NO3)2(aq) + 2 Ag(s)}\)
  2. \(\ce{BaCl2(aq) + Na2SO4(aq) -> BaSO4(s) + 2 NaCl(aq)}\)
  3. \(\ce{CH4(g) + 2 O2(g) -> CO2(g) + 2 H2O(g)}\)
3. A student writes: “Zinc is oxidized, so zinc is the oxidizing agent.” Correct the statement and explain the naming convention.
4. Solution stoichiometry:
  1. Moles of \(\ce{Ba^2+}\) in 35.0 mL of 0.200 M \(\ce{BaCl2}\): 7.00e-3 mol
  2. Mass of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) (\(M = 233.39\)) if that all precipitates: 1.63 g
  3. Volume of 0.150 M \(\ce{Na2SO4}\) needed to react with it exactly: 46.7 mL
5. 40.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\) is mixed with 40.0 mL of 0.150 M \(\ce{NaCl}\).
  1. Moles of each reactant: \(\ce{Ag+}\) 4.00e-3 mol; \(\ce{Cl-}\) 6.00e-3 mol
  2. Limiting reactant, with reasoning:
    1:1 ratio, so \(\ce{Ag+}\) (fewer moles) is limiting.
  3. Mass of \(\ce{AgCl}\) formed (\(M = 143.32\)): 0.573 g
  4. Moles of excess reactant remaining: 2.00e-3 mol \(\ce{Cl-}\)
6. Titration calculations:
  1. 25.0 mL of \(\ce{HCl}\) needs 28.6 mL of 0.125 M \(\ce{NaOH}\). Find \([\ce{HCl}]\). 0.143 M
  2. 20.0 mL of 0.200 M \(\ce{H2SO4}\) is titrated with 0.250 M \(\ce{KOH}\). Volume needed?
    \(n(\ce{H2SO4}) = 4.00e-3\,\mathrm{mol}\); diprotic \(\to n(\ce{OH-}) = 8.00e-3\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(V = 8.00\times10^{-3}/0.250 = 32.0\,\mathrm{mL}\)

  2. Weak acid written how in a net ionic? intact
  3. Physical or chemical: iron rusting? chemical
  4. Reaction type: \(\ce{HNO3 + KOH}\): acid–base
