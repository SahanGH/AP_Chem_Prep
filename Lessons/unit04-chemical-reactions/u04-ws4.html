Unit 4 • Chemical Reactions

Worksheet 4 • FRQ Practice

Type C • one long FRQ, the exam's format • answer, then self-score

Directions & data
The unit exam's free-response question is this shape and length. Write everything before looking at the rubric, then grade yourself the way a reader would — no credit for anything not written down.
1. Long FRQ (10 points). A student investigates the reaction between aqueous lead(II) nitrate and aqueous potassium iodide. She mixes 50.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{Pb(NO3)2}\) with 50.0 mL of 0.100 M \(\ce{KI}\) and observes a bright yellow solid.
  1. Write the balanced formula equation, including states.
  2. Write the net ionic equation and identify the spectator ions.
  3. Determine the limiting reactant, showing the comparison that justifies your answer.
    \(n(\ce{Pb^2+}) = (0.0500)(0.100) = 5.00e-3\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(n(\ce{I-}) = 5.00e-3\,\mathrm{mol}\). The ratio required is 1 \(\ce{Pb^2+}\) : 2 \(\ce{I-}\), so iodide runs out first — \(\ce{I-}\) is limiting.
  4. Calculate the mass of \(\ce{PbI2}\) (\(M = 461.0\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) produced.
  5. In the box, draw a particulate representation of the solution after the reaction is complete. Show at least six particles, label your symbols, and omit water.
    A cluster of \(\ce{PbI2}\) solid; separated \(\ce{K+}\) and \(\ce{NO3-}\) ions in solution; and leftover \(\ce{Pb^2+}\) ions (half the original amount) still dissolved, since iodide was limiting.
  6. The student claims the yellow colour alone proves a chemical reaction occurred. Evaluate this claim.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 1 pt balanced equation with correct states •\ (b) 2 pts 1 net ionic with the 2:1 coefficient, 1 both spectators • (c) 2 pts 1 both mole values, 1 comparison that uses the 1:2 ratio and names \(\ce{I-}\) — comparing raw moles earns 0 •\ (d) 2 pts 1 applies the 2:1 ratio to get 2.50e-3 mol, 1 mass with units • (e) 2 pts 1 precipitate drawn as a cluster with spectators separated, 1 leftover \(\ce{Pb^2+}\) shown •\ (f) 1 pt identifies precipitate formation as the stronger evidence.
2. Short redox practice. For \(\ce{2 KMnO4 + 5 H2C2O4 + 3 H2SO4 -> 2 MnSO4 + 10 CO2 + K2SO4 + 8 H2O}\):
  1. Oxidation state of Mn on each side: \(+7 \to +2\)
  2. Oxidation state of C in \(\ce{H2C2O4}\) and in \(\ce{CO2}\): \(+3 \to +4\)
  3. Oxidizing agent and reducing agent: \(\ce{KMnO4}\) oxidizing; \(\ce{H2C2O4}\) reducing
