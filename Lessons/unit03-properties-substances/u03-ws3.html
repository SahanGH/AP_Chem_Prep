Directions & data
When a problem involves an ionic solute, state how many moles of each ion form before computing concentrations.
1. Molarity calculations:
  1. 0.400 mol in 500. mL: 0.800 M
  2. 25.0 g \(\ce{NaOH}\) in 2.00 L: 0.313 M
  3. Moles in 150. mL of 0.250 M: 0.0375 mol
  4. Mass of \(\ce{KNO3}\) in 250. mL of 0.100 M: 2.53 g
2. Dilutions:
  1. 25.0 mL of 12.0 M diluted to 500. mL: 0.600 M
  2. Volume of 6.00 M needed to make 250. mL of 1.50 M: 62.5 mL
  3. Explain why \(M_1V_1 = M_2V_2\) works, in one sentence.
3. Ion concentrations:
  1. \([\ce{Na+}]\) in 0.20 M \(\ce{Na3PO4}\): 0.60 M
  2. \([\ce{SO4^2-}]\) in 0.15 M \(\ce{Al2(SO4)3}\): 0.45 M
  3. Total ion concentration in 0.10 M \(\ce{CaCl2}\): 0.30 M
4. In the box, draw a particulate representation of 0.10 M \(\ce{MgBr2}\). Use filled circles for \(\ce{Mg^2+}\) and open circles for \(\ce{Br-}\), show at least 3 magnesium ions, and omit water molecules (note that you have done so).
At least 3 filled and 6 open circles (\(1{:}2\) ratio), all separate and randomly distributed — never drawn as bonded \(\ce{MgBr2}\) units. Caption: “water omitted for clarity.”
5. A student draws 0.1 M \(\ce{KCl}\) as eight intact “\(\ce{KCl}\)” pairs floating in water. Identify the error and its consequence.
6. Describe how to prepare 500. mL of 0.200 M \(\ce{CuSO4}\) from the solid, including the glassware.
