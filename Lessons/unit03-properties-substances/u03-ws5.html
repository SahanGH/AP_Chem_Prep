Worksheet 5 • Spectroscopy & FRQ Practice

Directions & data
Constants: \(c = 3.00e8\,\mathrm{m/s}\), \(h = 6.626e-34\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\). The equations sheet gives \(E = h\nu\) but not \(\nu = c/\lambda\) — supply that step yourself.
1. Match each spectroscopic region to the molecular transition it probes:
  1. Microwave: molecular rotation
  2. Infrared: bond vibration
  3. Ultraviolet–visible: electronic transitions
2. Photon calculations:
  1. Frequency of 400. nm light: \(7.50\times10^{14}\) s\(^{-1}\)
  2. Energy of one photon at that wavelength: \(4.97\times10^{-19}\) J
  3. Energy of one mole of those photons: 299 kJ/mol
  4. A photon of 200 nm light has how many times the energy of one at 600 nm? Answer without a calculator. 3 times
3. Beer–Lambert basics. \(A = \epsilon bc\).
  1. A solution has \(A = 0.480\) in a 1.00 cm cell with \(\epsilon = 240\,\mathrm{/M/cm}\). Find \(c\). 0.00200 M
  2. If the same solution is measured in a 2.00 cm cell, what is \(A\)? 0.960
  3. Why does absorbance have no units?
4. Long FRQ (10 points). A student determines the concentration of a copper(II) solution by visible spectroscopy. Standards are measured at 635 nm in a 1.00 cm cell:
\(c\) (M)0.01000.02000.03000.0400
\(A\)0.1250.2500.3750.500
  1. On the axes below, plot \(A\) versus \(c\) and draw the best-fit line.
    Straight line through the origin, points collinear.
  2. Determine the slope of the line, including units.
  3. Calculate \(\epsilon\), stating its units.
  4. An unknown gives \(A = 0.300\). Determine its concentration.
  5. The student dilutes the unknown by half and re-measures. Predict the new absorbance and justify.
  6. Explain why the calibration must be performed at a single fixed wavelength.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 correctly plotted points, 1 straight best-fit line through the origin • (b) 2 pts 1 slope value, 1 units • (c) 1 pt \(\epsilon\) with \(b\) divided out • (d) 2 pts 1 divides by slope (not multiplies), 1 correct value • (e) 1 pt 0.150 with proportionality stated • (f) 1 pt names \(\epsilon\)'s wavelength dependence • 1 pt overall: all concentrations carry units and sig figs.
