Unit 3 • Properties of Substances and Mixtures

Worksheet 2 • Gases

CED 3.4–3.6 • kelvin every time

Directions & data
Convert every temperature to kelvin before substituting. Show the rearranged equation before plugging in numbers.
1. Ideal gas calculations. \(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\).
  1. Volume of 3.00 mol at 2.00 atm and 127 °C:
    \(T = 400.\,\mathrm{K}\); \(V = (3.00)(0.08206)(400.)/2.00 = 49.2\,\mathrm{L}\)
  2. Moles in 5.00 L at 1.20 atm and 27 °C: 0.244 mol
  3. Pressure of 0.100 mol in 2.00 L at 300. K: 1.23 atm
2. A gas occupies 6.0 L at 300 K and 1.0 atm.
  1. Volume at 600 K, constant pressure: 12 L
  2. Volume at 3.0 atm, constant temperature: 2.0 L
  3. A student converts 300 K to 27 °C and uses 27 in the ratio. Explain the error's effect.
3. A 10.0 L vessel holds 0.30 mol \(\ce{N2}\), 0.50 mol \(\ce{O2}\), and 0.20 mol \(\ce{Ar}\) at 300. K.
  1. Total pressure: 2.46 atm
  2. Mole fraction of \(\ce{O2}\): 0.50
  3. Partial pressure of \(\ce{O2}\): 1.23 atm
  4. If all the \(\ce{O2}\) were removed, what happens to the partial pressure of \(\ce{N2}\)?
4. State which KMT assumption each observation depends on:
  1. Gas pressure rises when the container shrinks. constant random motion / collisions
  2. All gases at the same \(T\) have equal average kinetic energy. \(KE \propto T\)
  3. Gas particles don't slow down over time in a sealed flask. elastic collisions
5. \(\ce{He}\) and \(\ce{CO2}\) are held at the same temperature.
  1. Compare their average kinetic energies. equal — KE depends only on \(T\)
  2. Compare their average speeds, with reasoning.
  3. Sketch both Maxwell–Boltzmann curves on one set of axes and label them.
    He: broad, flat, peak far right. \(\ce{CO2}\): narrow, tall, peak left. Equal areas.
6. Under which conditions does a real gas behave most ideally, and why? Name both failed assumptions in your answer.
7. For \(\ce{CH4}\) at 200 K, a plot of \(PV/nRT\) vs \(P\) first dips below 1, then rises above 1.
  1. Cause of the dip: attractions reduce collision force
  2. Cause of the rise: particle volume becomes significant
  3. Would \(\ce{He}\) show a larger or smaller dip? Why?
Spiral review • Unit 3 • blocks 1–2
  1. Strongest IMF in \(\ce{CH3CH2OH}\): hydrogen bonding
  2. Solid type of diamond: covalent network
  3. Higher vapor pressure: water or acetone (bp 56 °C)? acetone
  4. Melting a molecular solid breaks: IMFs, not bonds
  5. Higher bp: \(\ce{Ar}\) or \(\ce{Ne}\)? \(\ce{Ar}\)
