Unit Exam

Section I • Multiple Choice 30 questions • 45 minutes • 1 point each

1. Which intermolecular force is present in all molecular substances?
  1. dipole–dipole
  2. hydrogen bonding
  3. London dispersion
  4. ion–dipole
2. Which substance exhibits hydrogen bonding?
  1. \(\ce{CH3F}\)
  2. \(\ce{CH3OH}\)
  3. \(\ce{CH3OCH3}\)
  4. \(\ce{H2S}\)
3. \(\ce{BI3}\) (nonpolar) boils at 210 °C while \(\ce{NH3}\) (hydrogen bonding) boils at -33 °C. The best explanation is that
  1. \(\ce{BI3}\) has far more electrons, giving very strong dispersion forces
  2. \(\ce{NH3}\) does not actually hydrogen bond
  3. \(\ce{BI3}\) is ionic
  4. boiling point does not depend on intermolecular forces
4. Which property decreases as intermolecular forces get stronger?
  1. boiling point
  2. viscosity
  3. vapor pressure
  4. surface tension
5. A solid melts at 1600 °C, is very hard, and does not conduct electricity in any phase. It is best classified as
  1. ionic
  2. metallic
  3. covalent network
  4. molecular
6. Melting solid \(\ce{CO2}\) requires overcoming
  1. covalent C=O bonds
  2. London dispersion forces between molecules
  3. ionic attractions
  4. hydrogen bonds
7. During the melting of a pure substance at constant pressure, the added heat causes
  1. the temperature to rise steadily
  2. intermolecular forces to be overcome at constant temperature
  3. covalent bonds to break

8. A liquid boils when its vapor pressure
  1. reaches zero
  2. exceeds its critical pressure
  3. equals the external pressure
  4. equals its surface tension
9. A gas occupies 4.0 L at 300 K. At constant pressure, its volume at 600 K is
  1. 2.0 L
  2. 8.0 L
  3. 4.0 L
  4. 16 L
10. Which quantity must be expressed in kelvin in \(PV = nRT\)?
  1. pressure
  2. volume
  3. temperature
  4. moles
11. A 1.0 L flask contains 0.20 mol \(\ce{He}\) and 0.30 mol \(\ce{Ne}\). The mole fraction of helium is
  1. 0.20
  2. 0.40
  3. 0.50
  4. 0.67
12. In a mixture of gases, the partial pressure of one component depends on
  1. the identity of the other gases
  2. the molar mass of that gas
  3. its mole fraction and the total pressure
  4. the sizes of the gas molecules
13. Two identical flasks at the same temperature and pressure contain \(\ce{H2}\) and \(\ce{CO2}\). Which statement is correct?
  1. They contain the same number of molecules.
  2. They have the same mass.
  3. The \(\ce{H2}\) flask has more molecules.
  4. The \(\ce{CO2}\) flask has higher pressure.
14. According to kinetic molecular theory, the average kinetic energy of gas particles is directly proportional to
  1. pressure
  2. molar mass
  3. absolute temperature
  4. container volume
15. At the same temperature, which gas has the highest average molecular speed?
  1. \(\ce{H2}\)
  2. \(\ce{O2}\)

16. As the temperature of a gas increases, its Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution
  1. narrows and shifts left

17. Which KMT assumption explains why gas pressure increases when the container volume decreases at constant temperature?

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 31 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Answer all parts. The table gives data for three substances.

SubstanceMolar mass (g/mol)Boiling point (deg;C)Polarity
\(\ce{CH4}\)16.0\(-164\)nonpolar
\(\ce{CH3OH}\)32.0\(65\)polar
\(\ce{CH3SH}\)48.1\(6\)polar
  1. Identify all intermolecular forces present in each of the three substances.
  2. \(\ce{CH3SH}\) has the largest molar mass yet boils 59 °C below \(\ce{CH3OH}\). Explain.
  3. Predict which of the three has the highest vapor pressure at 25 °C and justify.
  4. A 2.00 L vessel holds 0.150 mol of \(\ce{CH4}\) gas at 27 °C. Calculate the pressure in atm.
  5. The vessel is cooled to -100 °C and the measured pressure is found to be lower than \(PV = nRT\) predicts. Explain in terms of the failed assumption.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

A student measures the absorbance of four standard solutions of a blue dye at 630 nm in a 1.00 cm cell and obtains a straight line through the origin with slope 45.0 /M.

  1. Determine the molar absorptivity \(\epsilon\), with units.
  2. An unknown solution gives \(A = 0.630\). Calculate its concentration.
  3. The student repeats the measurement at 500 nm and obtains a much smaller absorbance. Explain why the original calibration line cannot be used at this new wavelength.
