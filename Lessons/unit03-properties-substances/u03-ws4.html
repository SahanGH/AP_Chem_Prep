Unit 3 • Properties of Substances and Mixtures

Worksheet 4 • Solubility and Separation

CED 3.9, 3.10 • every separation exploits one physical difference

Directions & data
For solubility questions, cite the structural feature (a polar bond, an O–H, a long carbon chain) — not just the word “polar.”
1. Predict which member of each pair is more soluble in water and give the structural reason:
  1. \(\ce{CH3CH2OH}\) or \(\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH2CH2OH}\)
  2. \(\ce{I2}\) or \(\ce{NaI}\)
  3. \(\ce{CH4}\) or \(\ce{NH3}\)
2. Vitamin A is composed almost entirely of carbon and hydrogen; vitamin C contains several O–H and C–O bonds.
  1. Which is water-soluble? Justify from structure.
  2. Explain why excess vitamin A can accumulate in the body while excess vitamin C does not.
3. Iodine is far more soluble in \(\ce{CCl4}\) than in water, even though water is the more common solvent. Explain using “like dissolves like.”
4. Name the technique and the property it exploits:
  1. Separating sand from water: filtration — particle size
  2. Separating ethanol from water: distillation — boiling point
  3. Separating leaf pigments: chromatography — relative attraction
  4. Recovering dissolved salt from seawater: evaporation/distillation — volatility
5. A paper chromatogram is run with a polar stationary phase (paper) and a nonpolar mobile phase. Two dyes are applied; dye X travels 7.2 cm and dye Y travels 2.1 cm while the solvent front travels 9.0 cm.
  1. Which dye is more polar? Justify.
  2. Compute both retention factors (\(R_f = \) distance moved by substance \(\div\) distance moved by solvent). X: 0.80   Y: 0.23
  3. Predict what happens to dye Y's \(R_f\) if a polar mobile phase is used instead.
6. A mixture contains sand, salt, and water. Design a full separation, giving the order and the reason for each step.
