Unit 3 • Properties of Substances and Mixtures

Free-Response Set

CED 3.1–3.13 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — two CED exclusions apply

Covers CED 3.1 through CED 3.13. This is the heaviest unit on the exam, 18 %–22 %. Grade it hardest.

Topic CED 3.3 excludes phase diagrams. Topic CED 3.8 excludes colligative properties and, with them, calculations of molality, percent by mass and percent by volume. Concentration on this exam means molarity; no question below uses anything else.

\(R = 0.08206\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm/mol/K}\); the vapour pressure of water at 25 °C is 23.8 torr.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Zinc reacts with excess hydrochloric acid: \[ \ce{Zn(s) + 2HCl(aq) -> ZnCl2(aq) + H2(g)} \] A 3.27 g sample of zinc reacts completely and the hydrogen is collected over water at 25 °C. The total pressure in the collection tube is 745 torr.

  1. Calculate the moles of hydrogen produced.
  2. Determine the partial pressure of the dry hydrogen, in atm.
  3. Calculate the volume the hydrogen occupies.
  4. The tube is warmed. Explain, using kinetic molecular theory, what happens to the pressure exerted by the hydrogen if the volume is held fixed.
  5. Helium is added to the tube at constant volume and temperature. State the effect on the partial pressure of the hydrogen and on the total pressure.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

  1. Rank \(\ce{CH4}\), \(\ce{CH3OH}\) and \(\ce{CH3F}\) by boiling point, lowest first, and name the strongest intermolecular force in each.
  2. Explain why \(\ce{CH3F}\) boils lower than \(\ce{CH3OH}\) even though fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen.
  3. Methanol and water mix in all proportions; methane is almost insoluble in water. Account for the difference.
  4. During boiling, energy is supplied but the temperature does not rise. Explain.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. State the two assumptions of the ideal gas model that fail in a real gas.
  2. At high pressure the measured pressure of a real gas falls below the ideal prediction, but at very high pressure it rises above it. Explain both.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

A coloured solution has an absorbance of 0.435 in a 1.00 cm cell. The molar absorptivity of the absorbing species is 1.25e3  /M/cm.

  1. Calculate the concentration.
  2. A student dilutes the sample to half its concentration. State the new absorbance and explain.
  3. Explain why a molecule absorbs only certain wavelengths of light.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. A student draws a particulate diagram of aqueous \(\ce{K2SO4}\) showing intact \(\ce{K2SO4}\) units. Identify the error and describe the correct drawing.
  2. Name a technique that separates a dissolved salt from its solvent, and state the property it exploits.
