The seven tools

IMF identificationLDF in everything; dipole–dipole if polar; H-bonding only if H is on N, O, or F; ion–dipole for salts in polar solvent. Compare size first when molecules differ greatly
IMF consequencesstronger IMF \(\to\) higher bp, viscosity, surface tension; lower vapor pressure
Solid classificationionic / metallic / covalent network / molecular — predict mp, hardness, conductivity from the particle type
Gas laws\(PV = nRT\) (\(R = 0.08206\)), kelvin always; \(P_A = \chi_A P_{\text{tot}}\)
KMT\(KE_{\text{avg}} \propto T\) only; \(u \propto 1/\sqrt{M}\); hotter \(=\) broader, flatter, right-shifted, same area
Solutions\(M = \) mol solute / L solution; \(M_1V_1 = M_2V_2\); ionic solutes drawn fully dissociated in ratio
Beer–Lambert\(A = \epsilon bc\); calibration slope \(= \epsilon b\); unknown \(c = A \div \text{slope}\)

Two deviation stories, kept straight

  • \(PV/nRT < 1\) — attractions dominate (low \(T\), moderate \(P\)). Molecules pull on each other, softening wall collisions, so measured \(P\) is low.
  • \(PV/nRT > 1\) — particle volume dominates (high \(P\)). The gas cannot compress as far as the ideal law predicts.
  • Most ideal: high \(T\), low \(P\). Most deviant: strong IMFs (\(\ce{H2O}\), \(\ce{NH3}\)) at low \(T\), high \(P\).

Separation: name the property, not just the technique

filtration \(\to\) particle size • distillation \(\to\) boiling point • chromatography \(\to\) relative attraction to stationary vs mobile phase

The traps, one line each

  • Ranking by force type alone — \(\ce{BI3}\) outboils \(\ce{NH3}\) because dispersion scales with electron count.
  • Celsius in a gas law. Convert first, every time.
  • “Melting breaks the covalent bonds” — melting a molecular solid breaks only IMFs.
  • “Real gases deviate because they're real” — name volume or attractions, and say which direction.
  • Drawing ionic solutions as intact units instead of separated ions in the correct ratio.
  • Multiplying by the Beer–Lambert slope instead of dividing.
  • Forgetting \(\nu = c/\lambda\) — the equations sheet gives only \(E = h\nu\).

How to study for this exam

This is a double block and the largest unit — budget two study sessions, not one. Session 1: IMFs, solids, phases (WS 1). Session 2: gases, KMT, deviations (WS 2), then solutions and spectroscopy (WS 3–5). Redo WS 5's FRQ cold; the exam's short FRQ is its twin. For every “explain” question, name the particles and the force before naming the property.
