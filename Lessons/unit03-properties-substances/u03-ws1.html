Unit 3 • Properties of Substances and Mixtures

Worksheet 1 • Forces, Solids, Phases

CED 3.1–3.3 • every ranking needs its reason

Directions & data
Name the force AND the structural feature that produces it. “It has stronger IMFs” restates the question — say which force and why it is present.
1. List every intermolecular force present in each substance:
  1. \(\ce{CO2}\): LDF only (nonpolar)
  2. \(\ce{CH3F}\): LDF + dipole–dipole
  3. \(\ce{NH3}\): LDF + dipole–dipole + H-bonding
  4. \(\ce{KBr}\) dissolved in water: ion–dipole
2. \(\ce{HF}\) boils at 19.5 °C while \(\ce{HCl}\) boils at -85 °C, even though \(\ce{HCl}\) has the greater molar mass. Explain.
3. Zumdahl reports these boiling points: \(\ce{BI3}\) 209.5 °C (LDF only), \(\ce{PCl3}\) 76.1 °C (LDF + dipole–dipole), \(\ce{NH3}\) -33.0 °C (LDF + H-bonding).
  1. This ordering appears to contradict “hydrogen bonding is strongest.” Resolve the apparent contradiction.
  2. State the rule this illustrates in one sentence.
4. Classify each solid and predict whether it conducts electricity as a solid:
  1. \(\ce{SiC}\), mp 2730 °C, extremely hard: covalent network; no
  2. \(\ce{Ag}\), mp 962 °C, malleable: metallic; yes
  3. \(\ce{K2S}\), mp 840 °C, brittle: ionic; no (yes when molten)
  4. \(\ce{I2}\), mp 114 °C, soft: molecular; no
5. A student claims that melting ice breaks the O–H bonds in water. Evaluate this claim and give the correct account.
6. Rank by vapor pressure at 25 °C, highest first, with a one-line reason: \(\ce{H2O}\), \(\ce{CH3OH}\), \(\ce{C6H14}\) (hexane).
