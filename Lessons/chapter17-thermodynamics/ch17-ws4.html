Chapter 17 • Entropy and Free Energy

Worksheet 4 • Dissolution, Coupling & FRQ

Zumdahl §17.5, 17.10 • free energies add, which is what makes coupling work

Directions & data
\(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\) •\ \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\) • \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\). ATP hydrolysis: \(\Delta G^\circ = -30.5\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\). \(S^\circ\) (J/K\(\cdot\)mol): \(\ce{H2}\) 131 • \(\ce{O2}\) 205 • \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) 70. \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) (kJ/mol): \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) \(-237\).
1. Dissolving a salt has three free-energy contributions. Name each and give the sign of its enthalpy or entropy contribution:
  1. Breaking up the crystal lattice: \(\Delta H\) positive, \(\Delta S\) positive
  2. Reorganizing solvent around the ions: \(\Delta S\) negative (hydration shells order)
  3. Attraction between ions and solvent: \(\Delta H\) negative
2. The CED says predicting the overall \(\Delta G^\circ\) of dissolution “can be challenging.” Explain why, and say what you are expected to do.
3. \(\ce{NH4NO3}\) dissolves endothermically — the beaker gets noticeably cold — yet it is extremely soluble.
  1. What does the cold beaker tell you about \(\Delta H^\circ\)? it is positive
  2. Since the salt dissolves readily, what must be true of \(\Delta G^\circ\), and therefore of \(\Delta S^\circ\)?
  3. Explain the entropy increase in physical terms.
  4. What does this example prove about the claim “exothermic reactions are the favored ones”?
4. \(\ce{NaCl}\) dissolves with \(\Delta H^\circ\) very close to zero, yet readily. Explain what must be driving it and write the reduced form of the free-energy expression that applies.
5. Two ways to drive an unfavorable process.
  1. Name them.
  2. Give the CED's two examples of an external source.
  3. What must two coupled reactions share for the coupling to be real rather than bookkeeping? a common intermediate
6. Coupled reaction arithmetic.
  1. Reaction X has \(\Delta G^\circ = +9.2\) kJ/mol and is coupled to ATP hydrolysis. Find the overall \(\Delta G^\circ\) and say whether it is favored. \(-21.3\) kJ/mol; favored
  2. Reaction Y has \(\Delta G^\circ = +50\) kJ/mol. Show that coupling to a single ATP hydrolysis is not enough, then find how many are needed.
    One ATP: \(+50 + (-30.5) = +19.5\) kJ — still unfavorable. Two ATP: \(+50 + 2(-30.5) = \mathbf{-11.0}~\text{kJ}\) — favored. So two are required.
  3. Explain what “ATP is the cell's energy currency” actually means, thermodynamically.
7. Photosynthesis converts \(\ce{CO2}\) and water to glucose and oxygen with \(\Delta G^\circ \approx +2870\) kJ per mole of glucose.
  1. Is the reaction thermodynamically favored? no
  2. Explain how it occurs anyway, and why no law is broken.
8. FRQ (10 points). Consider the electrolysis of water, \(\ce{2H2O(l) -> 2H2(g) + O2(g)}\).
  1. Predict the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) and justify from the equation alone.
  2. Calculate \(\Delta S^\circ\).
    \([2(131) + 205] - 2(70) = 467 - 140 = \mathbf{+327}~\mathrm{J/K}\)
  3. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K from \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) values, and state whether the reaction is thermodynamically favored.
    \(\Delta G^\circ = 0 - 2(-237) = \mathbf{+474}~\text{kJ}\) — strongly not favored
  4. Given \(\Delta H^\circ = +572\) kJ, calculate the temperature above which the reaction becomes favored, and comment on whether that is a practical route to hydrogen.
    \(T = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ = 572/0.327 = \mathbf{1749}~\mathrm{K}\) (\(\approx1480\,\mathrm{{}^\circ C}\)). Impractical — maintaining that temperature is far harder than supplying the energy electrically.
  5. Explain how hydrogen is obtained from water in practice, in terms of this unit's vocabulary.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 positive, 1 justification citing 3 mol of gas formed from a liquid • (b) 2 pts 1 correct setup with coefficients, 1 answer \(+327\) J/K • (c) 2 pts 1 \(\Delta G^\circ = +474\) kJ, 1 states not favored •\ (d) 3 pts 1 recognizes \(T = \Delta H^\circ/\Delta S^\circ\), 1 unit conversion to kJ/K and answer \(1749\) K, 1 comment on impracticality — an answer that forgets to convert J to kJ earns at most 1 •\ (e) 1 pt electrolysis identified as an external energy source driving an unfavorable process.
Spiral review • Chapter 17 • blocks 1–4
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\); if \(K > 1\) then \(\Delta G^\circ\) is negative
  2. Favored but immeasurably slow means: kinetic control
  3. Crossover temperature \(=\) \(\Delta H/\Delta S\)
  4. A catalyst changes the rate but not: \(K\)
  5. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\) with \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) is favored at: all temperatures
