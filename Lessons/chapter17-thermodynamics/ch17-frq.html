Chapter 17 • Spontaneity, Entropy, and Free Energy

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §17.1–§17.10 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope

Covers CED 9.1 (§17.1), 9.2 (§17.2, §17.5–17.6), 9.3 (§17.3–17.4), 9.4 (§17.7), 9.5 (§17.9), 9.6 (§17.5) and 9.7 (coupled reactions, §17.10).

A note on wording: the CED prefers “thermodynamically favourable” to “spontaneous”, precisely because the latter suggests something about speed. It does not — a thermodynamically favourable reaction may be immeasurably slow. Both terms appear below; the distinction is examined in Question 1(e).

\(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\).

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A reaction has \(\Delta H^\circ = +30.0\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) and \(\Delta S^\circ = +100\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\).

  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K.
  2. Determine the temperature above which the reaction becomes thermodynamically favourable.
  3. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 400 K.
  4. State the general condition, in terms of the signs of \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta S\), for a reaction that is favourable only at high temperature.
  5. At 400 K this reaction is thermodynamically favourable, yet no product is detected after an hour. Explain how both can be true.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

  1. Predict the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) for each process, with a one-line reason.
    1. \(\ce{H2O(l) -> H2O(g)}\)
    2. \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> N2O4(g)}\)
    3. \(\ce{NaCl(s) -> Na+(aq) + Cl^-(aq)}\)
  2. State the single most reliable indicator of the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) for a reaction involving gases.
  3. A reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ = -10.0\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) at 400 K. Calculate \(K\).
  4. State the value of \(K\) when \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\), and explain what that means physically.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

Ammonium nitrate dissolves readily in water although the process is endothermic.

  1. State the signs of \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta S\) for the dissolution.
  2. Explain how \(\Delta G\) can still be negative, and state what would happen at a sufficiently low temperature.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

For \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) -> 2NH3(g)}\), standard molar entropies in J/mol/K are \(\ce{N2}\) 191.6, \(\ce{H2}\) 130.7, \(\ce{NH3}\) 192.5.

  1. Calculate \(\Delta S^\circ\) for the reaction.
  2. State whether the sign agrees with what you would predict by inspection, and why.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

The extraction of a metal from its oxide, \(\ce{MO(s) -> M(s) + 1/2O2(g)}\), has \(\Delta G^\circ = +150\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\). Industrially it is carried out with carbon, using \(\ce{C(s) + 1/2O2(g) -> CO(g)}\), \(\Delta G^\circ = -137\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\).

  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the coupled reaction \(\ce{MO(s) + C(s) -> M(s) + CO(g)}\).
  2. State whether coupling makes the extraction favourable at 298 K, and explain the principle behind coupling.
