Worksheet 3 • Temperature, $K$, and Kinetic Control

Zumdahl §17.3, 17.9 • favored says where it ends up, never how fast

Directions & data
\(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\) •\ \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\) • \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\) • \(RT \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) at 298 K. Remember to convert \(\Delta G^\circ\) to joules before using \(R\).
1. Complete the four-case table:
  1. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\): favored at
  2. \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ < 0\): favored at
  3. \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\): favored at
  4. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\), \(\Delta S^\circ < 0\): favored at
  5. In which two cases is no calculation needed at all, and why?
2. The crossover temperature is where \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\).
  1. Derive the expression for it from \(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\).
  2. A reaction has \(\Delta H^\circ = +30\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +80\) J/K. Find the crossover temperature and say on which side it is favored.
  3. A reaction has \(\Delta H^\circ = -40\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = -120\) J/K. Find the crossover temperature and the favored side.
3. Vaporization of water has \(\Delta H^\circ = +44\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +119\) J/K.
  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K and interpret it.
  2. Calculate the crossover temperature and identify what it physically is.
  3. Water actually boils at 373 K. Account for the discrepancy.
4. Convert between \(\Delta G^\circ\) and \(K\) at 298 K:
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ = -20\) kJ. Find \(K\).
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ = +20\) kJ. Find \(K\).
  3. \(K = 25\). Find \(\Delta G^\circ\).
  4. \(K = 1.0\times10^{-5}\). Find \(\Delta G^\circ\).
5. Estimation without a calculator. Fill in “greater than 1,” “close to 1,” or “less than 1”:
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ = -45\) kJ: \(K\) is
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ = +45\) kJ: \(K\) is
  3. \(\Delta G^\circ \approx 0\): \(K\) is
  4. Why does a change of only a few kJ in \(\Delta G^\circ\) swing \(K\) so violently?
6. For \(\ce{N2O4(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\), \(\Delta H^\circ = +58\) kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ = +176\) J/K.
  1. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K.
  2. Calculate \(K\) at 298 K.
  3. Find the crossover temperature and explain what a student would observe on warming a sealed tube of this mixture.
7. Kinetic control.
  1. Define kinetic control and give the usual cause.
  2. The conversion of diamond to graphite has \(\Delta G^\circ < 0\) at 298 K. Explain why diamonds persist.
  3. A mixture of \(\ce{H2}\) and \(\ce{O2}\) sits unchanged for years, yet the combustion has \(\Delta G^\circ = -474\) kJ. What does a spark change, and what does it not?
  4. A catalyst is added to a favored but slow reaction. State the effect on the rate and on \(K\).
8. A student writes: “The reaction has a large negative \(\Delta G^\circ\), so it must go quickly and go to completion.” Identify both errors.
