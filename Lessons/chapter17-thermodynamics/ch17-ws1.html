Worksheet 1 • Entropy and the Second Law

Zumdahl §17.1–17.2 • count moles of gas before you do anything else

Directions & data
Write thermodynamically favored, not “spontaneous.” \(S^\circ\) in J/K/mol: \(\ce{H2}\) 131 •\ \(\ce{O2}\) 205 • \(\ce{N2}\) 192 • \(\ce{H2O(l)}\) 70 • \(\ce{H2O(g)}\) 189 • \(\ce{CO2}\) 214 •\ \(\ce{CO}\) 198 • \(\ce{C(gr)}\) 6 • \(\ce{NH3}\) 193.
1. Entropy measures dispersal. Name the two things that disperse, and give an example of each.
2. Give the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) for each change and a one-phrase reason:
  1. \(\ce{H2O(s) -> H2O(l)}\) positive — solid to liquid
  2. \(\ce{2SO2(g) + O2(g) -> 2SO3(g)}\) negative — 3 mol gas to 2
  3. \(\ce{NH4NO3(s) -> NH4+(aq) + NO3^-(aq)}\) positive — ordered lattice to free ions
  4. A gas is compressed into half its volume negative — less space to occupy
  5. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\) positive — gas produced from a solid
  6. A sample of argon is warmed from 300 to 400 K positive — energy spread over more states
  7. \(\ce{H2(g) + Cl2(g) -> 2HCl(g)}\)
3. Explain why counting moles of gas is the first move, using water as evidence.
4. State the second law and explain why it refers to the universe rather than the system.
5. \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}} = -\Delta H/T\).
  1. For an exothermic reaction, the sign of \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}}\) is positive
  2. Why does the same quantity of heat raise the surroundings' entropy more at low temperature than at high?
  3. Use that to explain why water freezes below 0 °C but melts above it, given that \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta S\) for freezing barely change over that range.
6. For the combustion of carbon, \(\ce{C(gr) + O2(g) -> CO2(g)}\):
  1. Predict the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) before calculating, and say why the prediction is hard here.
  2. Calculate \(\Delta S^\circ\).
    \(214 - (6 + 205) = \mathbf{+3}~\mathrm{J/K}\) — very small, as predicted
  3. The reaction is strongly exothermic. What does that tell you about \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}}\)?
7. A classmate says: “Life creates order out of disorder, so living things break the second law.” Refute this.
8. Rank by molar entropy, lowest first: \(\ce{H2O(s)}\), \(\ce{H2O(l)}\), \(\ce{H2O(g)}\). Then explain what would change if the comparison were made at 500 K instead of 298 K.
