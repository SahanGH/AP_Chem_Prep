AP Chemistry lessons

Chapter 17 • Entropy and Free Energy

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §17.1–17.10 • 60 minutes • thermodynamic data provided with Section II

Scope and format

Covers CED 9.1–9.7. Electrochemistry (9.8–9.11) is Chapter 18 and does not appear.

Write thermodynamically favored, not “spontaneous.” The textbook uses the older word; the exam does not.

This test carries one long and one short free response rather than two short ones. A complete thermodynamic analysis — \(\Delta S^\circ\), \(\Delta G^\circ\) by two routes, crossover temperature, \(K\) — is a standard AP long-response task and does not fit in four points.

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Entropy is best described as a measure of
  1. the dispersal of matter and energy
  2. the total energy of a system
  3. how quickly a reaction proceeds
  4. the heat released by a reaction
2. Which change has a positive \(\Delta S^\circ\)?
  1. \(\ce{2H2(g) + O2(g) -> 2H2O(l)}\)
  2. \(\ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\)
  3. \(\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) -> 2NH3(g)}\)
  4. \(\ce{2NO2(g) -> N2O4(g)}\)
3. When predicting the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) for a reaction, the first thing to examine is
  1. the change in the number of moles of gas
  2. whether the reaction is exothermic
  3. the number of atoms in each molecule
  4. the temperature
4. Raising the temperature of a gas increases its entropy because
  1. the distribution of molecular kinetic energies broadens
  2. the molecules become larger
  3. the number of molecules increases
  4. the pressure necessarily falls
5. For the same substance, which ordering of \(S^\circ\) is correct?
  1. gas \(<\) liquid \(<\) solid
  2. solid \(<\) liquid \(<\) gas
  3. liquid \(<\) solid \(<\) gas
  4. all three are equal
6. The second law of thermodynamics requires that, for a thermodynamically favored process,
  1. \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}} > 0\)
  2. \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0\)
  3. \(\Delta S_{\text{univ}} > 0\)
  4. \(\Delta H < 0\)
7. \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}}\) is given by
  1. \(-\Delta H/T\)
  2. \(+\Delta H/T\)
  3. \(-T\Delta H\)
  4. \(\Delta H - T\Delta S\)
8. Exothermicity is most effective as a driving force
  1. at low temperature
  2. at high temperature
  3. equally at all temperatures
  4. only above the crossover temperature
9. Water freezes below 0 °C even though \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}} < 0\) because
  1. the surroundings gain more entropy than the system loses
  2. the second law does not apply to phase changes
  3. \(\Delta S_{\text{sys}}\) becomes positive below 0 °C
  4. freezing is endothermic
10. \(\Delta S^\circ\) for a reaction is calculated as
  1. \(\sum nS^\circ_{\text{products}} - \sum nS^\circ_{\text{reactants}}\)
  2. \(\sum nS^\circ_{\text{reactants}} - \sum nS^\circ_{\text{products}}\)
  3. \(\sum nS^\circ_{\text{products}} + \sum nS^\circ_{\text{reactants}}\)
  4. \(-\Delta H/T\)
11. The standard entropy \(S^\circ\) of \(\ce{O2(g)}\) at 298 K is
  1. exactly zero, since it is an element
  2. a positive number, about 205 J/K/mol
  3. negative
  4. undefined
12. A student computes \(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\) using \(\Delta H^\circ\) in kJ and \(\Delta S^\circ\) in J/K without converting. Their answer will be wrong by a factor of roughly
  1. 10
  2. 100
  3. 1000
  4. they will get the right answer
13. A process is thermodynamically favored when
  1. \(\Delta G^\circ < 0\)
  2. \(\Delta G^\circ > 0\)
  3. \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\) only
  4. \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) only
14. A reaction with \(\Delta H^\circ < 0\) and \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) is favored
  1. at all temperatures
  2. at no temperature
  3. only at high temperature
  4. only at low temperature
15. A reaction with \(\Delta H^\circ > 0\) and \(\Delta S^\circ > 0\) is favored
  1. at all temperatures
  2. at no temperature
  3. above the crossover temperature
  4. below the crossover temperature
16. The crossover temperature at which \(\Delta G^\circ = 0\) equals
  1. \(\Delta H^\circ / \Delta S^\circ\)
  2. \(\Delta S^\circ / \Delta H^\circ\)
  3. \(-\Delta H^\circ \Delta S^\circ\)
  4. \(\Delta H^\circ - \Delta S^\circ\)
17. If \(\Delta G^\circ\) is negative, then
  1. \(K > 1\) and products are favored at equilibrium
  2. \(K < 1\) and reactants are favored
  3. \(K = 1\)
  4. \(K\) cannot be determined
18. A reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ\) very close to zero. Then \(K\) is
  1. very large
  2. very small
  3. close to 1
  4. negative
19. A thermodynamically favored reaction that proceeds at no measurable rate is best described as
  1. under kinetic control
  2. at equilibrium
  3. thermodynamically unfavored after all
  4. endothermic
20. Adding a catalyst to a thermodynamically favored reaction
  1. increases the rate but leaves \(K\) and \(\Delta G^\circ\) unchanged
  2. increases both the rate and \(K\)
  3. makes \(\Delta G^\circ\) more negative
  4. has no effect on anything

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 30 minutes

Data
\(\Delta H_f^\circ\) (kJ/mol), \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) (kJ/mol), \(S^\circ\) (J/K\(\cdot\)mol): \(\ce{CO(g)}\) \(-110.5\), \(-137\), 198 •\ \(\ce{CO2(g)}\) \(-393.5\), \(-394\), 214 • \(\ce{O2(g)}\) 0, 0, 205 • \(\ce{CaCO3(s)}\) \(-1207\), \(-1129\), 93 • \(\ce{CaO(s)}\) \(-635\), \(-604\), 40.
\(\Delta G^\circ = \Delta H^\circ - T\Delta S^\circ\) •\ \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\) •\ \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\). ATP hydrolysis: \(\Delta G^\circ = -30.5\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\).

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Limestone decomposes according to \[ \ce{CaCO3(s) -> CaO(s) + CO2(g)} \]

  1. Predict the sign of \(\Delta S^\circ\) and justify from the equation.
  2. Calculate \(\Delta S^\circ\) and \(\Delta H^\circ\).
  3. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) at 298 K and state whether the decomposition is thermodynamically favored.
  4. Verify your answer to (c) using the \(\Delta G_f^\circ\) values, and comment on any difference.
  5. Calculate the temperature above which the decomposition becomes thermodynamically favored.
  6. Calculate \(K\) at 298 K, and explain what its magnitude means physically.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

  1. The oxidation \(\ce{2CO(g) + O2(g) -> 2CO2(g)}\) has \(\Delta G^\circ = -514\) kJ, yet carbon monoxide persists in the atmosphere for months. Explain.
  2. A biochemical reaction has \(\Delta G^\circ = +21\) kJ/mol. Show whether coupling it to one ATP hydrolysis makes it favored.
  3. State what the two reactions must share for the coupling to be chemically real.
