Directions & data
\(K = {}^\circ\)C \(+\ 273.15\) • \(d = m/V\) •\ \(1~\text{in} \equiv 2.54~\text{cm}\) (exact). Multiply or divide: fewest significant figures. Add or subtract: fewest decimal places.
1. Counting practice. Give the number of significant figures:
  1. \(0.0105\) 3
  2. \(0.050080\) 5
  3. \(8.050\times10^{-3}\) 4
  4. \(100\) 1
  5. \(100.\) 3
  6. \(1.00\times10^{2}\) 3
  7. \(1200\) 2
  8. \(1.20\times10^{3}\) 3
  9. \(0.00300\) 3
  10. \(45.20\) 4
2. For each of the three zero types, state the rule and give one example from problem 1.
3. Why is \(1.20\times10^{3}\) unambiguous where \(1200\) is not?
4. Multiplication and division. Report each to the correct number of significant figures:
  1. \(4.56 \times 1.4\) 6.4
  2. \(2.5 \times 3.42\) 8.6
  3. \(12.4 \times 0.0025\) 0.031
  4. \(0.0250 \times 4.00\) 0.100
  5. \(8.315 \div 298\)
    \(= 0.027903\ldots\); three significant figures in both, so \(\mathbf{0.0279}\)
  6. \((6.022\times10^{23})(2.0)\) \(1.2\times10^{24}\)
5. Addition and subtraction. Watch the decimal places:
  1. \(12.11 + 18.0 + 1.013\)
    \(= 31.123\); \(18.0\) limits to one decimal place, so \(\mathbf{31.1}\)
  2. \(3.14159 - 0.2\) 2.9
  3. \(100.0 + 0.005\) 100.0
  4. \(1.0 + 2.02 + 3.003\) 6.0
  5. \(25.64 - 25.6\) 0.0
6. Look again at 5(c) and 5(e).
  1. In 5(c), how many significant figures does the answer have, and how many does the least precise term have?
  2. In 5(e), two numbers with three or four significant figures produced an answer with essentially none. Explain, and name a laboratory situation where this matters.
7. Dimensional analysis. Show your conversion factors.
  1. 1.50 kg to grams 1500 g
  2. 2.50 L to millilitres 2500 mL
  3. 750 mL to litres 0.750 L
  4. 12.0 inches to centimetres
    \((12.0)(2.54) = \mathbf{30.5}\) cm — the 2.54 is exact, so three significant figures come from the \(12.0\)
  5. 65.0 miles per hour to metres per second.
    \(65.0 \times 5280 \times 12 \times 2.54 \div 100 = 104607\,\mathrm{m/h}\); \(\div 3600 = 29.057\ldots\), so \(\mathbf{29.1}~\mathrm{m/s}\)
8. Why do the conversion factors in problem 7(d) and 7(e) not limit the significant figures?
9. Density.
  1. Mercury has \(d = 13.6\,\mathrm{g/mL}\). What volume does 100.0 g occupy?
    \(100.0/13.6 = \mathbf{7.35}\) mL — three significant figures, limited by the \(13.6\)
  2. A 25.0 g sample occupies 9.26 cm³. Find its density and suggest the metal.
    \(25.0/9.26 = \mathbf{2.70}~\mathrm{g/cm^3}\) — aluminium
  3. Explain why density is better thought of as a conversion factor than as a formula.
10. Precision, accuracy, and a decision.
  1. Four students measure the same 10.00 g standard. Their results:
    A 9.99, 10.01, 10.00B 9.2, 10.8, 9.7C 9.45, 9.46, 9.44D 8.1, 11.4, 10.2
    Classify each as precise, accurate, both, or neither.
  2. Which student's problem is fixed by more careful technique, and which by recalibrating the instrument?
