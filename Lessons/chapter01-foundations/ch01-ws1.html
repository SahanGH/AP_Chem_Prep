Day 1 Diagnostic • 25 marks

Zumdahl §1.3–1.10 • 30 minutes • this is scored, but it is not a test grade

Directions & data
\(K = {}^\circ\)C \(+\ 273.15\) • \(d = m/V\) •\ \(1~\text{in} \equiv 2.54~\text{cm}\) (exact) •\ \(1~\text{mi} = 5280~\text{ft}\) • \(1~\text{ft} = 12~\text{in}\). Show your work — the point of this is to find out where you are, not to sort you.
What this is for

This diagnostic tells your teacher which skills to reteach and which to leave alone. It does not go in the gradebook as a test. Answer what you can, mark anything you are guessing at, and leave blank anything you have genuinely never seen — a blank is far more useful information than a guess.

1. [6 marks] Give the number of significant figures:
  1. \(0.00470\)
  2. \(6500\)
  3. \(6.500\times10^{3}\)
  4. \(2.0080\)
  5. \(150.\)
  6. \(0.0090\)
2. [4 marks] Carry out each calculation and report it to the correct number of significant figures:
  1. \(3.24 \times 0.0150\)
  2. \(88.4 \div 2.0\)
  3. \(14.31 + 2.7\)
  4. \(9.00 - 8.9\)
3. [4 marks] Unit conversions. Show the conversion factors you use.
  1. Convert 2.50 L to millilitres.
  2. Convert 350 mg to grams.
  3. Convert 0.0450 m to millimetres.
  4. Convert 5.00 miles to kilometres.
4. [3 marks] Temperature.
  1. 37.0 °C in kelvin:
  2. 373.15 K in degrees Celsius:
  3. Why is the kelvin scale the one used in gas-law and thermodynamic equations?
5. [4 marks] Density.
  1. A block of aluminium (\(d = 2.70\,\mathrm{g/cm^3}\)) has a volume of 15.0 cm³. Find its mass.
  2. Ethanol has \(d = 0.789\,\mathrm{g/mL}\). Find the mass of 25.0 mL.
  3. A 45.2 g metal sample raises the water level in a graduated cylinder from 25.0 mL to 41.7 mL. Calculate its density.
6. [2 marks] Precision and accuracy. A student weighs the same object four times and records 12.47, 12.48, 12.46 and 12.48 g. The object's true mass is 13.02 g.
  1. Are the measurements precise? Accurate?
  2. What kind of error does this indicate, and what would you do about it?
7. [2 marks] Classify each as an element, a compound, a homogeneous mixture, or a heterogeneous mixture:
  1. Pure copper wire
  2. Table salt, \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  3. Salt water
  4. Sand in water
Scoring guide — for the teacher
Total 25 marks. One mark per numbered part unless stated. Award the mark only when the significant figures are also correct on problems 2–5; that is the skill being measured. 22–25 — fluent. No reteaching needed; move straight into Unit 1.
17–21 — sound but leaky. Assign `ch01-ws2' as homework and move on.
12–16 — the significant-figure and conversion habits are not yet automatic. Assign `ch01-ws2', and expect to re-flag sig figs in the first two Unit 1 worksheets.
below 12 — these skills will block progress in Unit 3 (stoichiometry) and Unit 6 (thermochemistry). Worth a dedicated float block before Unit 1's exam. Watch the pattern, not just the total. Problems 1–2 are significant figures, 3 is conversions, 5 is density. A student who scores well overall but loses both marks on 2(c) and 2(d) has one specific, fixable misconception — applying the multiplication rule to sums — and does not need general remediation.
