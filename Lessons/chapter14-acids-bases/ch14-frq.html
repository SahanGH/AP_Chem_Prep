Chapter 14 • Acids and Bases

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §14.1–§14.9 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 8.1 (§14.1–14.2), 8.2 (§14.3–14.4, §14.6), 8.3 (§14.5–14.7), 8.6 (§14.9) and 8.7 (§14.8).

The CED exclusion on topic 4.8 removes Lewis acid–base concepts. Zumdahl develops the Lewis model in §14.11; it is not assessed and no question below uses it. Brønsted–Lowry — proton donor and acceptor — is the model the exam expects throughout.

\(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\) at 25 °C.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Acetic acid, \(\ce{CH3COOH}\), has \(K_a = 1.8\times10^{-5}\). Consider a 0.100 M solution.

  1. Write the equation for its ionization in water and the \(K_a\) expression.
  2. Calculate the pH of the solution.
  3. Justify the approximation you used, or explain why none was needed.
  4. Calculate the percent ionization, and state what happens to it if the solution is diluted.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

  1. Distinguish between a strong acid and a concentrated acid.
  2. Calculate the pH of 0.0100 M \(\ce{HCl}\) and of 0.0100 M \(\ce{NaOH}\).
  3. Explain why \(\ce{HF}\) is a weak acid while \(\ce{HCl}\) is strong, even though fluorine is the more electronegative element.
  4. Oxoacid strength increases along \(\ce{HClO}\), \(\ce{HClO2}\), \(\ce{HClO3}\), \(\ce{HClO4}\). Explain the trend.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A solution at 25 °C has \([\ce{OH-}] = 2.5e-4\,\mathrm{M}\).

  1. Calculate the pOH, the \([\ce{H3O+}]\) and the pH.
  2. State whether the solution is acidic or basic, and how you know.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. Identify the two conjugate acid–base pairs in \[ \ce{HCO3^-(aq) + H2O(l) <=> H2CO3(aq) + OH^-(aq)} \]
  2. \(\ce{HCO3-}\) can act as either an acid or a base. State the term for this and give the equation for it acting as an acid.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

Predict whether an aqueous solution of each salt is acidic, basic or neutral, and justify.

  1. \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  2. \(\ce{NH4Cl}\)
  3. \(\ce{NaCH3COO}\)
