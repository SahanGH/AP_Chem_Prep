Chapter 14 • Acids and Bases

Worksheet 3 • Salts, Structure & FRQ

Zumdahl §14.8–14.9 • trace every salt back to its parent acid and base

Directions & data
\(K_a\): \(\ce{HF}\) \(7.2\times10^{-4}\) • \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) • \(\ce{HCN}\) \(6.2\times10^{-10}\). \(K_b\): \(\ce{NH3}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
1. Classify each salt solution as acidic, basic, or neutral, and name the ion responsible:
  1. \(\ce{NaCl}\): neutral — neither ion hydrolyses
  2. \(\ce{NH4Cl}\): acidic — \(\ce{NH4+}\)
  3. \(\ce{NaCN}\): basic — \(\ce{CN-}\)
  4. \(\ce{KNO3}\): neutral
  5. \(\ce{NH4NO3}\): acidic — \(\ce{NH4+}\)
  6. \(\ce{KF}\): basic — \(\ce{F-}\)
2. Explain, in conjugate-pair terms, why \(\ce{NaCl}\) solutions are neutral but \(\ce{NaCN}\) solutions are basic.
3. Calculate the pH of 0.20 M \(\ce{NaC2H3O2}\).
  1. Write the hydrolysis equation.
  2. Calculate \(K_b\). \(5.6\times10^{-10}\)
  3. Calculate the pH.
    \(x^2/0.20 \approx 5.6\times10^{-10}\); \(x = 1.06\times10^{-5} = [\ce{OH-}]\); pOH \(= 4.98\); pH \(= 9.02\)
4. Calculate the pH of 0.30 M \(\ce{NaF}\).
\(K_b = 1.0\times10^{-14}/7.2\times10^{-4} = 1.4\times10^{-11}\); \(x^2/0.30 \approx 1.4\times10^{-11}\); \(x = 2.0\times10^{-6}\); pOH \(= 5.69\); pH \(= 8.31\)
5. Structure and acid strength:
  1. Rank \(\ce{HF}\), \(\ce{HCl}\), \(\ce{HBr}\), \(\ce{HI}\) by increasing strength and give the controlling factor.
  2. Explain why \(\ce{HF}\) is the weakest despite fluorine being the most electronegative element.
  3. Rank \(\ce{HClO}\), \(\ce{HClO2}\), \(\ce{HClO3}\), \(\ce{HClO4}\) and give both reasons.
  4. Rank \(\ce{HOI}\), \(\ce{HOBr}\), \(\ce{HOCl}\). \(\ce{HOI}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HOBr}\) \(<\) \(\ce{HOCl}\)
6. FRQ (10 points). Hypochlorous acid, \(\ce{HOCl}\), has \(K_a = 3.5\times10^{-8}\).
  1. Write the ionization equation and the \(K_a\) expression.
  2. Calculate the pH of a 0.150 M solution of \(\ce{HOCl}\), including the validity check on any approximation.
    \(x^2 \approx (3.5\times10^{-8})(0.150) = 5.25\times10^{-9}\); \(x = 7.25\times10^{-5}\); pH \(= 4.14\). Check: \(7.25\times10^{-5}/0.150 = 0.048\%\), far under 5% ✓
  3. Calculate the percent dissociation. 0.048%
  4. Sodium hypochlorite, \(\ce{NaOCl}\), is dissolved in water. Predict whether the solution is acidic, basic, or neutral, and justify.
  5. Calculate \(K_b\) for \(\ce{OCl-}\) and the pH of a 0.150 M \(\ce{NaOCl}\) solution.
    \(K_b = 1.0\times10^{-14}/3.5\times10^{-8} = 2.9\times10^{-7}\); \(x^2/0.150 \approx 2.9\times10^{-7}\); \(x = 2.08\times10^{-4} = [\ce{OH-}]\); pOH \(= 3.68\); pH \(= 10.32\)
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 equation, 1 expression • (b) 3 pts 1 correct ICE setup, 1 pH \(= 4.14\), 1 explicit 5% check with the number — asserting validity earns 0 • (c) 1 pt 0.048% • (d) 2 pts 1 basic, 1 identifies \(\ce{OCl-}\) as the conjugate base of a weak acid AND \(\ce{Na+}\) as inert •\ (e) 2 pts 1 \(K_b\) from \(K_w/K_a\), 1 pH via pOH — reporting \(-\log x\) as the pH earns 0.
