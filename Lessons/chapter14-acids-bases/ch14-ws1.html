Chapter 14 • Acids and Bases

Worksheet 1 • Conjugates, Strength, and the pH Scale

Zumdahl §14.1–14.4, 14.6 • strong is not the same word as concentrated

Directions & data
\(K_a\) values where needed: \(\ce{HF}\) \(7.2\times10^{-4}\) •\ \(\ce{HNO2}\) \(4.0\times10^{-4}\) • \(\ce{HC2H3O2}\) \(1.8\times10^{-5}\) • \(\ce{HOCl}\) \(3.5\times10^{-8}\) •\ \(\ce{HCN}\) \(6.2\times10^{-10}\). \(K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}\).
1. Identify the conjugate:
  1. Conjugate base of \(\ce{H2SO4}\): \(\ce{HSO4^-}\)
  2. Conjugate acid of \(\ce{HCO3^-}\): \(\ce{H2CO3}\)
  3. Conjugate base of \(\ce{HCO3^-}\): \(\ce{CO3^2-}\)
  4. Conjugate acid of \(\ce{H2O}\): \(\ce{H3O+}\)
2. For \(\ce{HNO2(aq) + H2O(l) <=> H3O+(aq) + NO2^-(aq)}\), label all four species as acid, base, conjugate acid, or conjugate base.
3. Explain why water can act as a Brønsted–Lowry base, referring to its structure.
4. Rank as bases, weakest to strongest, and justify: \(\ce{Cl-}\), \(\ce{F-}\), \(\ce{CN-}\), \(\ce{C2H3O2^-}\).
5. pK\(_a\) practice:
  1. pK\(_a\) of acetic acid: 4.74
  2. pK\(_a\) of \(\ce{HCN}\): 9.21
  3. Which is the stronger acid, and how does pK\(_a\) show it?
6. pH and pOH conversions at 25 °C:
  1. \([\ce{H+}] = 2.5\times10^{-3}\): pH \(=\) 2.60
  2. pH \(= 8.40\): \([\ce{H+}] =\) \(4.0\times10^{-9}\)
  3. \([\ce{OH-}] = 5.0\times10^{-5}\): pH \(=\) 9.70
  4. pH \(= 11.20\): pOH \(=\) 2.80
7. Explain why pure water at 50 °C has a pH slightly below 7 yet is still neutral.
8. Strong acid and base pH:
  1. 0.020 M \(\ce{HCl}\): 1.70
  2. 0.0010 M \(\ce{HNO3}\): 3.00
  3. 0.025 M \(\ce{NaOH}\): 12.40
  4. 0.015 M \(\ce{Ba(OH)2}\):
    \([\ce{OH-}] = 2(0.015) = 0.030\); pOH \(= 1.52\); pH \(= 12.48\)
9. A student calculates the pH of 0.010 M \(\ce{Sr(OH)2}\) as 12.00. Identify the error and give the correct value.
10. 0.10 M \(\ce{HCl}\) has pH 1.00; 0.10 M acetic acid has pH 2.87. Explain the difference and state what it illustrates about the words “strong” and “concentrated.”
