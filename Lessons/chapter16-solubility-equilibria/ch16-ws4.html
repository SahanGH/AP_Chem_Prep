Chapter 16 • Solubility Equilibria

Worksheet 4 • pH, Precipitation & FRQ

Zumdahl §16.1–16.2 • the test is whether the anion is a base

Directions & data
\(K_{sp}\): \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.6\times10^{-10}\) • \(\ce{BaSO4}\) \(1.5\times10^{-9}\) • \(\ce{PbCl2}\) \(1.6\times10^{-5}\) • \(\ce{CaF2}\) \(4.0\times10^{-11}\) • \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\) \(8.9\times10^{-12}\). CED 8.11 is qualitative only — predict the direction, justify with Le Ch\^atelier, never compute solubility from pH.
1. State the single test that decides whether a salt's solubility depends on pH, and explain why it works.
2. For each salt, state whether it is more soluble in acidic solution than in pure water, and name the conjugate acid of its anion:
  1. \(\ce{CaCO3}\): yes — \(\ce{H2CO3}\), weak
  2. \(\ce{AgCl}\): no — \(\ce{HCl}\), strong
  3. \(\ce{CaF2}\): yes — \(\ce{HF}\), weak
  4. \(\ce{PbI2}\): no — \(\ce{HI}\), strong
  5. \(\ce{ZnS}\): yes — \(\ce{H2S}\), weak
  6. \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\): yes — \(\ce{H2O}\), very weak
  7. \(\ce{Ag3PO4}\): yes — \(\ce{HPO4^2-}\), weak
  8. \(\ce{Ba(NO3)2}\): no — \(\ce{HNO3}\), strong
3. Milk of magnesia is a suspension of solid \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\).
  1. Write the dissolution equilibrium.
  2. Explain, with an equation, why it dissolves in the stomach.
  3. What would adding \(\ce{NaOH}\) do instead?
4. Refute this claim: “Acids are very reactive, so every salt is more soluble in acid than in water.” Use a specific counterexample.
5. Limestone caves.
  1. Rainwater dissolves \(\ce{CO2}\), making it slightly acidic.
  2. Explain how that hollows out caverns in limestone, \(\ce{CaCO3}\).
  3. Where the water drips into open air, \(\ce{CO2}\) escapes and stalactites form. Explain the reversal.
6. Enrichment — \(Q\) versus \(K_{sp}\). State the rule:
  1. \(Q > K_{sp}\): a precipitate forms
  2. \(Q < K_{sp}\): no precipitate; unsaturated
  3. What must you always do to the concentrations before computing \(Q\) for two mixed solutions? correct them for dilution
7. Enrichment. 50.0 mL of 0.0020 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\) is mixed with 50.0 mL of 0.0020 M \(\ce{NaCl}\).
  1. Concentrations after mixing: both \(1.0\times10^{-3}\) M
  2. Calculate \(Q\) and decide.
    \(Q = (1.0\times10^{-3})(1.0\times10^{-3}) = 1.0\times10^{-6}\). Since \(1.0\times10^{-6} > 1.6\times10^{-10} = K_{sp}\), a precipitate forms.
  3. What would the answer have been had you forgotten the dilution?
8. Enrichment. 100.0 mL of 0.0010 M \(\ce{Pb(NO3)2}\) is mixed with 100.0 mL of 0.020 M \(\ce{NaCl}\). Will \(\ce{PbCl2}\) precipitate?
After mixing, \([\ce{Pb^2+}] = 5.0\times10^{-4}\) and \([\ce{Cl-}] = 1.0\times10^{-2}\). \(Q = (5.0\times10^{-4})(1.0\times10^{-2})^2 = 5.0\times10^{-8}\). Since \(5.0\times10^{-8} < 1.6\times10^{-5} = K_{sp}\), no precipitate forms.
9. FRQ (10 points). Calcium fluoride has \(K_{sp} = 4.0\times10^{-11}\) at 25 °C.
  1. Write the dissolution equation and the \(K_{sp}\) expression.
  2. Calculate the molar solubility in pure water.
    \([\ce{Ca^2+}] = s\), \([\ce{F-}] = 2s\); \(K_{sp} = (s)(2s)^2 = 4s^3 = 4.0\times10^{-11}\); \(s^3 = 1.0\times10^{-11}\); \(s = \mathbf{2.2\times10^{-4}}\) M
  3. Calculate the molar solubility in 0.025 M \(\ce{NaF}\), and state the assumption you made.
    \([\ce{F-}] = 0.025 + 2s \approx 0.025\), assuming \(2s\) is negligible because \(K_{sp}\) is very small. \(4.0\times10^{-11} = (s)(0.025)^2\); \(s = \mathbf{6.4\times10^{-8}}\) M. The assumption is justified: \(2s = 1.3\times10^{-7}\), utterly negligible beside 0.025.
  4. State what happens to \(K_{sp}\) between parts (b) and (c), and why.
  5. Predict whether \(\ce{CaF2}\) is more soluble in 0.10 M \(\ce{HCl}\) than in pure water, and justify. Do not calculate.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 balanced equation with states, 1 expression with the fluoride squared • (b) 3 pts 1 recognizing \([\ce{F-}] = 2s\), 1 setting up \(4s^3\), 1 answer \(2.2\times10^{-4}\) M — using \(s^3\) instead of \(4s^3\) caps this part at 1 •\ (c) 2 pts 1 correct substitution of 0.025 for \([\ce{F-}]\), 1 answer \(6.4\times10^{-8}\) M with the approximation stated •\ (d) 1 pt unchanged, because \(K_{sp}\) depends only on temperature — saying “it decreases” earns 0 • (e) 2 pts 1 more soluble, 1 justification naming \(\ce{F-}\) as the conjugate base of a weak acid.
