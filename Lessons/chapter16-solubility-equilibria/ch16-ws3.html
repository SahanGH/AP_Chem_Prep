Zumdahl §16.1 • solubility moves; $K_sp$ does not

Directions & data
\(K_{sp}\): \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.6\times10^{-10}\) • \(\ce{AgBr}\) \(5.0\times10^{-13}\) • \(\ce{BaSO4}\) \(1.5\times10^{-9}\) • \(\ce{PbI2}\) \(1.4\times10^{-8}\) • \(\ce{PbCl2}\) \(1.6\times10^{-5}\) • \(\ce{CaF2}\) \(4.0\times10^{-11}\) • \(\ce{MgF2}\) \(6.4\times10^{-9}\) • \(\ce{Ag2CrO4}\) \(9.0\times10^{-12}\).

2. Predict, without calculating, whether each addition decreases the solubility of \(\ce{AgCl}\). Answer yes or no and say why:
  1. \(\ce{NaCl}\): yes — \(\ce{Cl-}\) is a common ion
  2. \(\ce{AgNO3}\): yes — \(\ce{Ag+}\) is a common ion
  3. \(\ce{KNO3}\): no — neither ion participates
  4. \(\ce{NaBr}\):
3. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{AgCl}\):
  1. in pure water: \(1.3\times10^{-5}\) M
  2. in 0.10 M \(\ce{NaCl}\):
    \([\ce{Cl-}] \approx 0.10\); \(1.6\times10^{-10} = (s)(0.10)\); \(s = \mathbf{1.6\times10^{-9}}\) M
  3. in 0.020 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\):
    \([\ce{Ag+}] \approx 0.020\); \(1.6\times10^{-10} = (0.020)(s)\); \(s = \mathbf{8.0\times10^{-9}}\) M
  4. By what factor did the 0.10 M \(\ce{NaCl}\) suppress the solubility? about 7900
4. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) in 0.010 M \(\ce{Na2SO4}\), and compare with pure water.
\([\ce{SO4^2-}] \approx 0.010\); \(1.5\times10^{-9} = (s)(0.010)\); \(s = \mathbf{1.5\times10^{-7}}\) M. In pure water \(s = 3.9\times10^{-5}\) M, so the sulfate suppressed it by a factor of about 260.
5. Now a salt with a squared ion. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{PbI2}\):
  1. in pure water:
    \(4s^3 = 1.4\times10^{-8}\); \(s^3 = 3.5\times10^{-9}\); \(s = \mathbf{1.5\times10^{-3}}\) M
  2. in 0.10 M \(\ce{NaI}\):
    \([\ce{I-}] \approx 0.10\); \(1.4\times10^{-8} = (s)(0.10)^2\); \(s = \mathbf{1.4\times10^{-6}}\) M
  3. By roughly what factor did the iodide suppress it? about 1100
6. The same salt, the other ion. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{CaF2}\):
  1. in 0.10 M \(\ce{NaF}\):
    \([\ce{F-}] \approx 0.10\); \(4.0\times10^{-11} = (s)(0.10)^2\); \(s = \mathbf{4.0\times10^{-9}}\) M
  2. in 0.10 M \(\ce{Ca(NO3)2}\):
    \([\ce{Ca^2+}] \approx 0.10\) and \([\ce{F-}] = 2s\): \(4.0\times10^{-11} = (0.10)(2s)^2 = 0.40s^2\); \(s^2 = 1.0\times10^{-10}\); \(s = \mathbf{1.0\times10^{-5}}\) M
  3. The two solutions contain the same concentration of common ion, yet the effects differ enormously. Explain.
  4. In part (b), why does a factor of 4 appear even though calcium is the added ion?
7. Calculate the solubility of \(\ce{Ag2CrO4}\) in 0.10 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\). Be careful which ion is squared.
\(\ce{Ag2CrO4(s) <=> 2Ag+(aq) + CrO4^2-(aq)}\), \(K_{sp} = [\ce{Ag+}]^2[\ce{CrO4^2-}]\). The silver is pre-loaded: \([\ce{Ag+}] \approx 0.10\), \([\ce{CrO4^2-}] = s\). \(9.0\times10^{-12} = (0.10)^2(s)\); \(s = \mathbf{9.0\times10^{-10}}\) M. In pure water it is \(1.3\times10^{-4}\) M.
8. A student writes: “Adding \(\ce{NaCl}\) lowers \(K_{sp}\) for \(\ce{AgCl}\), which is why less dissolves.” Rewrite the sentence correctly and say what is still true of the ion product.
9. Design question. You want to wash a precipitate of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) with as little loss as possible. Would you rinse with pure water or with dilute \(\ce{Na2SO4}\) solution? Justify.
