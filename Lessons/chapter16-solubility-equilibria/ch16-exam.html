Chapter 16 • Solubility Equilibria

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §16.1 • 50 minutes • $K_sp$ values provided with Section II

Scope

Covers §16.1 only — CED topics 7.11, 7.12 and 8.11. Nothing from §16.2 (precipitation, qualitative analysis) or §16.3 (complex ions) appears; both are off the framework, and the \(Q\)-versus-\(K_{sp}\) material in Block 4 was marked enrichment.

Per the CED exclusion on topic 8.11, pH and solubility is assessed qualitatively only. You will be asked to predict a direction and justify it, never to compute a solubility from a pH.

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 25 minutes • 1 point each

1. The \(K_{sp}\) expression for \(\ce{Ag2CrO4}\) is
  1. \([\ce{Ag+}][\ce{CrO4^2-}]\)
  2. \([\ce{Ag+}]^2[\ce{CrO4^2-}]\)
  3. \([\ce{Ag+}]^2[\ce{CrO4^2-}]^2\)
  4. \([\ce{Ag2CrO4}]/[\ce{Ag+}]^2[\ce{CrO4^2-}]\)
2. A solid is omitted from a \(K_{sp}\) expression because
  1. it is a pure solid and has no variable concentration
  2. its concentration is zero
  3. it does not participate in the reaction
  4. \(K_{sp}\) applies only to ions
3. Adding more undissolved solid \(\ce{AgCl}\) to a saturated solution of \(\ce{AgCl}\) causes \([\ce{Ag+}]\) to
  1. stay the same
  2. increase
  3. decrease
  4. increase, then decrease
4. Which statement is correct?
  1. \(K_{sp}\) is a constant; solubility is a position that varies with the solution
  2. \(K_{sp}\) and solubility are two names for the same quantity
  3. solubility is constant; \(K_{sp}\) varies with the solution
  4. both vary with the amount of excess solid
5. For a salt \(\ce{MX}\) with molar solubility \(s\), \(K_{sp}\) equals
  1. \(s^2\)
  2. \(2s\)
  3. \(4s^3\)
  4. \(s\)
6. For a salt \(\ce{MX2}\) with molar solubility \(s\), \(K_{sp}\) equals
  1. \(s^3\)
  2. \(2s^3\)
  3. \(4s^3\)
  4. \(27s^4\)
7. A salt \(\ce{MX}\) has \(K_{sp} = 4.0\times10^{-10}\). Its molar solubility is
  1. \(4.0\times10^{-10}\)
  2. \(2.0\times10^{-10}\)
  3. \(2.0\times10^{-5}\)
  4. \(1.6\times10^{-19}\)

  1. too small by a factor of 4
  2. too large by a factor of 4
  3. too small by a factor of 2

9. \(\ce{Ag3PO4}\) dissolves to give three \(\ce{Ag+}\) and one \(\ce{PO4^3-}\). In terms of \(s\), \(K_{sp}\) equals
  1. \(3s^2\)

  4. are all insoluble by the Unit 4 rules
11. \(\ce{BaSO4}\) (\(K_{sp} = 1.5\times10^{-9}\)), \(\ce{PbSO4}\) (\(1.3\times10^{-8}\)) and \(\ce{SrSO4}\) (\(3.2\times10^{-7}\)). The most soluble is

  2. \(\ce{PbSO4}\)
  3. \(\ce{SrSO4}\)

13. According to the CED, salts the Unit 4 rules call “soluble” correspond to \(K_{sp}\) values that are
  1. negative
  2. exactly 1

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 25 minutes

Data
\(K_{sp}\) at 25 °C: \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\) \(8.9\times10^{-12}\) • \(\ce{AgCl}\) \(1.6\times10^{-10}\) • \(\ce{BaSO4}\) \(1.5\times10^{-9}\) • \(\ce{CaCO3}\) \(8.7\times10^{-9}\).

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

Magnesium hydroxide, \(\ce{Mg(OH)2}\), is a sparingly soluble solid.

  1. Write the dissolution equation and the \(K_{sp}\) expression.
  2. Calculate the molar solubility in pure water.
  3. State what happens to the solubility if \(\ce{NaOH}\) is added, and what happens to \(K_{sp}\).

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

A saturated solution of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) is prepared.

  1. Calculate the molar solubility of \(\ce{BaSO4}\) in pure water.
  2. Calculate the molar solubility in 0.010 M \(\ce{Na2SO4}\).
  3. \(\ce{BaSO4}\) and \(\ce{CaCO3}\) are each treated with dilute \(\ce{HNO3}\). Predict the effect on each solubility and justify the difference. Do not calculate.
