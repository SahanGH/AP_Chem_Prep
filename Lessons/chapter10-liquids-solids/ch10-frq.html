Chapter 10 • Liquids and Solids

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §10.1–§10.8 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 3.1 (intermolecular forces, §10.1), 3.2 (properties of solids, §10.3–10.7), 3.3 (§10.2, §10.8), 2.3 (ionic solids, §10.7), 2.4 (metals and alloys, §10.4) and 6.5 (energy of phase changes, §10.8).

Phase diagrams (§10.9) are excluded by the CED statement on topic 3.3 and are not examined here. Unit-cell geometry (§10.7) is enrichment depth under the exclusion on 2.3: the types of solid and their properties are assessed, specific crystal structures are not.

Take \(c(\ce{H2O},\ell) = 4.18\,\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 334\,\mathrm{J/g}\) and \(\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 2260\,\mathrm{J/g}\).

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

The normal boiling points of four substances are:
SubstanceBoiling point (deg;C)
\(\ce{CH4}\)\(-164\)
\(\ce{HCl}\)\(-85\)
\(\ce{HF}\)\(+20\)
\(\ce{H2O}\)\(+100\)
  1. Identify the strongest intermolecular force present in each substance.
  2. Explain why \(\ce{HF}\) boils higher than \(\ce{HCl}\) even though \(\ce{HCl}\) has more electrons.
  3. Explain why \(\ce{H2O}\) boils higher than \(\ce{HF}\), even though fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen.
  4. State what would happen to the boiling point of \(\ce{CH4}\) if it were replaced by \(\ce{CCl4}\), and justify.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A 50.0 g sample of ice at 0 °C is heated until it has all become steam at 100 °C.

  1. Calculate the energy needed to melt the ice.
  2. Calculate the energy needed to raise the resulting water from 0 °C to 100 °C.
  3. Calculate the energy needed to vaporize the water at 100 °C, and hence the total energy for the whole process.
  4. Vaporization requires far more energy than fusion. Explain why, in terms of what happens to the intermolecular forces.
  5. State what happens to the temperature during melting, and explain why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

Classify each solid and give one property that follows from your classification.

  1. \(\ce{SiO2}\) (quartz)
  2. \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  3. \(\ce{Cu}\)

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. Explain why metals conduct electricity and are malleable, using the electron sea model.
  2. Bronze is harder than pure copper. Give the structural reason.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Define the vapour pressure of a liquid.
  2. Diethyl ether has a much higher vapour pressure than water at 25 °C. State what this implies about its intermolecular forces and its boiling point.
