Chapter 10 • Liquids and Solids

Worksheet 1 • Forces and Liquids

Zumdahl §10.1–10.2 • compare size before comparing force type

Directions & data
Name the force AND the structural feature producing it. “Stronger IMFs” restates the question — say which force and why it is present.
1. List every intermolecular force present:
  1. \(\ce{CH3CH3}\): dispersion only
  2. \(\ce{CH3Cl}\): dispersion \(+\) dipole–dipole
  3. \(\ce{CH3OH}\): dispersion, dipole–dipole, H-bonding
  4. \(\ce{HF}\): dispersion, dipole–dipole, H-bonding
2. \(\ce{C2H5OH}\) boils at 78 °C while \(\ce{CH3OCH3}\) boils at -24 °C. Both have the formula \(\ce{C2H6O}\). Explain the 100 °C gap.
3. Zumdahl reports \(\ce{BI3}\) (dispersion only) boiling at 209.5 °C and \(\ce{NH3}\) (hydrogen bonding) at -33.0 °C.
  1. This appears to contradict “hydrogen bonding is the strongest IMF.” Resolve it.
  2. State the working rule this illustrates.
4. Rank each set by increasing boiling point with a one-line reason:
  1. \(\ce{Ne}\), \(\ce{Ar}\), \(\ce{Kr}\)
  2. \(\ce{CH4}\), \(\ce{CH3F}\), \(\ce{CH3OH}\)
5. Surface tension, viscosity, capillary action:
  1. Explain surface tension at the particle level.
  2. Water forms a concave meniscus in glass; mercury forms a convex one. State which force dominates in each case.
  3. Give two reasons glycerol (\(\ce{C3H8O3}\)) is more viscous than ethanol.
6. Which liquid in each pair has the higher vapour pressure at 25 °C? Justify.
  1. Water or acetone (bp 56 °C): acetone — weaker IMFs
  2. Hexane or octane: hexane — fewer electrons, weaker dispersion
