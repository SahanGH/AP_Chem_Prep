Chapter 10 • Liquids and Solids

Worksheet 3 • Vapour Pressure and Phase Changes

Zumdahl §10.8 • also CED 6.5 — Unit 6 borrows this topic

Directions & data
Water data for all problems: \(c_{\text{ice}} = 2.09\), \(c_{\text{water}} = 4.184\), \(c_{\text{steam}} = 2.0\) J/g/°C; \(\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 6.02\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\); \(\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 40.7\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\); \(M = 18.02\,\mathrm{g/mol}\).
1. Vapour pressure concepts:
  1. Define vapour pressure in terms of equilibrium.
  2. Does vapour pressure depend on how much liquid is present? Explain.
  3. State the condition for boiling.
  4. Explain why water boils below 100 °C at high altitude.
2. Heating-curve segments. State whether each uses \(q = mc\Delta T\) or \(q = n\Delta H\), and explain what the energy does:
  1. Warming ice from -20 °C to 0 °C: \(mc\Delta T\) — raises kinetic energy
  2. Melting ice at 0 °C: \(n\Delta H_{\text{fus}}\) — overcomes IMFs
  3. Boiling water at 100 °C: \(n\Delta H_{\text{vap}}\) — separates molecules
3. Calculations:
  1. Energy to melt 36.04 g of ice at 0 °C: 12.0 kJ
  2. Energy to vaporize 36.04 g of water at 100 °C: 81.4 kJ
  3. Energy to warm 36.04 g of liquid water from 0 °C to 100 °C:
    \(q = (36.04)(4.184)(100.0) = 15.1\,\mathrm{kJ}\)
  4. Which of these three steps requires the most energy, and why?
4. Explain why \(\Delta H_{\text{vap}}\) (40.7 kJ/mol) is nearly seven times \(\Delta H_{\text{fus}}\) (6.02 kJ/mol) for water.
5. FRQ (10 points). A 90.1 g sample of ice at -15.0 °C is heated until it becomes steam at 115.0 °C.
  1. Determine the number of moles of water.
  2. Calculate the energy required for each of the five stages.
    Warm ice: \((90.1)(2.09)(15.0) = 2.82\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Melt: \((5.00)(6.02) = 30.1\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Warm water: \((90.1)(4.184)(100.0) = 37.7\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Vaporize: \((5.00)(40.7) = 204\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Warm steam: \((90.1)(2.0)(15.0) = 2.7\,\mathrm{kJ}\).
  3. Determine the total energy required. 277 kJ
  4. What percentage of the total is spent on vaporization? Comment on the result.
  5. Sketch the heating curve, labelling both plateaus and stating why the temperature is constant along them.
    Curve rises, plateaus at 0 °C, rises, plateaus (longer) at 100 °C, rises. Temperature is constant because the energy is overcoming intermolecular forces rather than increasing average kinetic energy.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 1 pt 5.00 mol • (b) 4 pts 1 per correct sloped-segment pair (ice and steam), 1 fusion, 1 vaporization, 1 liquid warming — using mass in \(n\Delta H\) or moles in \(mc\Delta T\) loses the point • (c) 1 pt 277 kJ •\ (d) 2 pts 1 percentage, 1 comment noting no temperature change • (e) 2 pts 1 correct shape with the longer plateau at 100 °C, 1 explanation citing IMFs versus kinetic energy.
6. Explain why steam at 100 °C produces far more severe burns than liquid water at 100 °C, using a quantitative comparison.
