  1. dipole–dipole
  2. hydrogen bonding
  3. London dispersion
  4. ion–dipole

  1. \(\ce{CH3F}\)

  2. \(\ce{NH3}\) does not really hydrogen bond
  3. \(\ce{BI3}\) is ionic
  4. boiling point is unrelated to intermolecular forces
4. Which property decreases as intermolecular forces strengthen?
  1. boiling point
  2. surface tension
  3. vapour pressure
  4. viscosity

  1. its adhesive forces to glass exceed its cohesive forces
  2. its cohesive forces exceed its adhesive forces

  4. water has low surface tension
6. A liquid droplet forms a sphere in free fall because
  1. gravity compresses it
  2. surface tension minimizes the surface area
  3. air pressure is uniform
  4. the liquid is nonpolar
7. Viscosity is best described as a liquid's
  1. tendency to evaporate
  2. resistance to flow
  3. ability to dissolve solids
  4. surface curvature
8. A solid melts at 2800 °C, is extremely hard, and does not conduct in any phase. It is
  1. ionic
  2. metallic
  3. network covalent
  4. molecular
9. Which solid conducts electricity in the solid state?
  1. \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  2. Cu
  3. \(\ce{SiO2}\)
  4. ice

  1. the electron sea flows with metal layers, but ionic layers bring like charges into contact
  2. metals have weaker bonds
  3. ionic solids contain no charged particles

11. Steel is harder than pure iron because carbon atoms

  2. occupy interstitial holes and impede layer sliding
  3. bond ionically to iron

  2. each sp\(^2\) carbon has a delocalized \(p\) electron

13. Melting solid \(\ce{CO2}\) requires overcoming
  1. covalent C=O bonds
  2. dispersion forces between molecules
  3. ionic attractions
  4. hydrogen bonds

  1. holds molecules in an open lattice with more empty space
  2. makes the molecules heavier
  3. is weaker in the solid
  4. converts water to a network covalent solid
15. Vapour pressure depends on
  1. the amount of liquid present
  2. the temperature and the strength of intermolecular forces
  3. the volume of the container
  4. the surface area of the liquid
16. A liquid boils when its vapour pressure

17. During a phase change at constant pressure, the added energy

  3. breaks covalent bonds

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

A 54.06 g sample of ice at 0 °C is heated until it is entirely liquid water at 50.0 °C.

  1. Calculate the energy needed to melt the ice.
  2. Calculate the energy needed to warm the resulting water to 50.0 °C.
  3. State the total energy required, and explain why the temperature did not rise during the first stage.
