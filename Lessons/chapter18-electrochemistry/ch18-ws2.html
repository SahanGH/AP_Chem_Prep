Chapter 18 • Electrochemistry

Worksheet 2 • E°cell, ΔG° and K

Zumdahl §18.2–18.3 • scale the half-reaction, never scale the volts

Directions & data
\(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}}\) • \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\) • \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\) •\ \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\).
\(E^\circ\) (V): \(\ce{F2/F-}\) \(+2.87\) • \(\ce{Cl2/Cl-}\) \(+1.36\) • \(\ce{Ag+/Ag}\) \(+0.80\) • \(\ce{Fe^3+/Fe^2+}\) \(+0.77\) • \(\ce{I2/I-}\) \(+0.54\) • \(\ce{Cu^2+/Cu}\) \(+0.34\) • \(\ce{H+/H2}\) \(0.00\) • \(\ce{Pb^2+/Pb}\) \(-0.13\) • \(\ce{Ni^2+/Ni}\) \(-0.23\) • \(\ce{Fe^2+/Fe}\) \(-0.44\) • \(\ce{Zn^2+/Zn}\) \(-0.76\) • \(\ce{Al^3+/Al}\) \(-1.66\) • \(\ce{Mg^2+/Mg}\) \(-2.37\).
1. The standard hydrogen electrode.
  1. What \(E^\circ\) is assigned to \(\ce{2H+ + 2e^- -> H2}\)? exactly 0.00 V
  2. Why must some half-reaction be assigned a value by convention?
  3. State the three standard conditions. 1 M solutes, 1 atm gases, 25 °C
2. Two rules about manipulating half-reactions.
  1. Reversing a half-reaction does what to \(E^\circ\)? flips the sign
  2. Multiplying a half-reaction by 3 does what to \(E^\circ\)? nothing at all
  3. Explain the second rule.
3. For each pair, identify which species is reduced, write the overall equation, and calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\):
  1. \(\ce{Zn}\) and \(\ce{Ag+}\)
    \(\ce{Ag+}\) is reduced (more positive). \(\ce{Zn + 2Ag+ -> Zn^2+ + 2Ag}\); \(E^\circ = 0.80 - (-0.76) = \mathbf{+1.56}~\text{V}\)
  2. \(\ce{Ni}\) and \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)
    \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) is reduced. \(\ce{Ni + Cu^2+ -> Ni^2+ + Cu}\); \(E^\circ = 0.34 - (-0.23) = \mathbf{+0.57}~\text{V}\)
  3. \(\ce{Al}\) and \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)
    \(\ce{Zn^2+}\) is reduced. \(\ce{2Al + 3Zn^2+ -> 2Al^3+ + 3Zn}\); \(E^\circ = -0.76 - (-1.66) = \mathbf{+0.90}~\text{V}\)
  4. \(\ce{I-}\) and \(\ce{Cl2}\)
    \(\ce{Cl2}\) is reduced. \(\ce{2I- + Cl2 -> I2 + 2Cl-}\); \(E^\circ = 1.36 - 0.54 = \mathbf{+0.82}~\text{V}\)
4. In problem 3(c), the aluminium half-reaction was doubled and the zinc half-reaction tripled. Show that \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) is unaffected, and state what is affected.
5. Reading the table as a ranking.
  1. The strongest oxidizing agent in the data strip is \(\ce{F2}\), because it has the most positive \(E^\circ\)
  2. The strongest reducing agent is \(\ce{Mg}\), because its reduction potential is the most negative
  3. Will \(\ce{Ag(s)}\) reduce \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)? Justify with a potential.
  4. Which metals in the strip would dissolve in 1 M \(\ce{HCl}\)? Explain the criterion.
6. \(\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ\).
  1. For the Daniell cell (\(E^\circ = +1.10\) V, \(n = 2\)), calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) in kJ.
    \(\Delta G^\circ = -(2)(96485)(1.10) = -212{,}267~\text{J} = \mathbf{-212}~\text{kJ}\)
  2. For \(\ce{Cu + 2Ag+ -> Cu^2+ + 2Ag}\), calculate \(E^\circ\) and then \(\Delta G^\circ\).
    \(E^\circ = 0.80 - 0.34 = +0.46\) V; \(n = 2\); \(\Delta G^\circ = -(2)(96485)(0.46) = -88{,}766~\text{J} = \mathbf{-89}~\text{kJ}\)
  3. Why does the answer come out in joules rather than kilojoules?
7. Two cells each have \(E^\circ = +0.60\) V. Cell A transfers 1 electron; cell B transfers 3.
  1. Which has the larger voltage? neither — equal
  2. Which has the more negative \(\Delta G^\circ\), and by what factor? B, by a factor of 3
  3. Explain the difference in one sentence.
8. Connecting to \(K\). Using \(\ln K = nFE^\circ/RT\):
  1. For the Daniell cell, calculate \(\ln K\) and \(K\) at 298 K.
    \(\ln K = (2)(96485)(1.10)/[(8.314)(298)] = 85.7\); \(K = e^{85.7} \approx \mathbf{2\times10^{37}}\)
  2. Complete the table:
    1. \(E^\circ > 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) negative, \(K\) greater than 1
    2. \(E^\circ = 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) zero, \(K\) equal to 1
    3. \(E^\circ < 0\): \(\Delta G^\circ\) positive, \(K\) less than 1
  3. Explain why a cell voltage of only 1.10 V corresponds to an equilibrium constant as enormous as \(10^{37}\).
Spiral review • Chapter 18 • block 1

  2. Electrons flow from anode to: cathode
  3. Anions in the salt bridge move toward: the anode
