AP Chemistry lessons

Chapter 18 • Electrochemistry

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §18.1–18.4, 18.7–18.8 • 60 minutes • potentials provided with Section II

Scope and format

Covers CED 9.8–9.11. With Chapter 17 this completes Unit 9 and the whole framework.

Not assessed: labelling an electrode positive or negative (excluded by the CED), the Nernst equation (topic 9.10 asks for reasoning about distance from equilibrium, not calculation), and §18.5–18.6 (batteries and corrosion).

This test carries one long and one short free response. A full electrochemical analysis — half-reactions, \(E^\circ\), \(\Delta G^\circ\) and a Faraday calculation — is a standard AP long-response task.

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A galvanic cell is constructed from a zinc electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Zn(NO3)2}\) and a silver electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\), joined by a salt bridge.

  1. Write the two half-reactions, labelled as anode and cathode, and the balanced overall equation.
  2. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\).
  3. The silver half-reaction was doubled in part (a). Explain why \(E^\circ\) for it was not doubled.
  4. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the cell reaction.
  5. State what happens to the mass of each electrode as the cell runs, and the direction anions move in the salt bridge.
  6. Some of the 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\) is replaced with water, diluting it. Predict the effect on the cell potential and justify without using Le Ch\^atelier's principle.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

Copper is electroplated onto a metal object from \(\ce{CuSO4}\) solution using a current of 2.50 A for 40.0 min.

  1. Write the half-reaction occurring at the cathode and explain why the object must be the cathode.
  2. Calculate the mass of copper deposited.
