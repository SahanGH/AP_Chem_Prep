Chapter 18 • Electrochemistry

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §18.1–§18.4, §18.7 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope

Covers CED 9.8 (galvanic and electrolytic cells, §18.1, §18.7), 9.9 (cell potential and free energy, §18.2–18.3), 9.10 (nonstandard conditions, §18.4) and 9.11 (electrolysis and Faraday's law, §18.7). §18.5–18.6 (batteries, corrosion) are applications and are not separately assessed.

The CED exclusion on topic 9.8 concerns the \(+\)/\(-\) labelling of electrodes, which differs between cell types and is not assessed. Anode and cathode are assessed, and the rule never changes: oxidation occurs at the anode in both cell types.

\(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\). Standard reduction potentials: \(\ce{Cu^2+}\)/\(\ce{Cu}\) \(= +0.34\) V, \(\ce{Zn^2+}\)/\(\ce{Zn}\) \(= -0.76\) V, \(\ce{Ag+}\)/\(\ce{Ag}\) \(= +0.80\) V.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

A galvanic cell is built from a zinc electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Zn(NO3)2}\) and a copper electrode in 1.0 M \(\ce{Cu(NO3)2}\), joined by a salt bridge.

  1. Write the half-reaction at each electrode, identifying which is the anode.
  2. Calculate the standard cell potential.
  3. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the cell reaction.
  4. State the direction of electron flow in the external circuit and the direction of anion migration in the salt bridge.
  5. Explain what happens to the cell potential as the cell operates, and what its value is when the cell is dead.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

  1. For the zinc–copper cell above, calculate the cell potential when \([\ce{Cu^2+}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) and \([\ce{Zn^2+}] = 1.0\,\mathrm{M}\).
  2. State the effect on \(E\) of increasing \([\ce{Cu^2+}]\), and justify using Le Ch\^atelier's principle.
  3. Silver is plated from \(\ce{Ag+}\) solution using a current of 2.00 A for 30.0 min. Calculate the mass of silver deposited.
  4. If \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) were plated instead of \(\ce{Ag+}\) under the same current and time, state whether more or fewer moles of metal would deposit, and why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Determine whether copper metal will dissolve in 1.0 M \(\ce{Zn(NO3)2}\).
  2. State the general rule connecting the sign of \(E^\circ\) to favourability.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. State two differences between a galvanic and an electrolytic cell.
  2. State what is true of the anode in both types of cell.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

Both half-cells of a cell contain copper electrodes in \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) solution, one at 1.0 M and one at 0.010 M.

  1. State the value of \(E^\circ\) for this cell, with a reason.
  2. Explain why the cell nevertheless produces a measurable voltage, and state which half-cell is the cathode.
