Chapter 18 • Electrochemistry

Worksheet 3 • Nonstandard Conditions

Zumdahl §18.4 • reason from distance to equilibrium, not from Le Ch^atelier

Directions & data
CED 9.10 is reasoning, not calculation — the Nernst equation is not required. Standard conditions are \(Q = 1\); at equilibrium \(Q = K\) and \(E = 0\). \(E^\circ\) (V): \(\ce{Ag+/Ag}\) \(+0.80\) •\ \(\ce{Cu^2+/Cu}\) \(+0.34\) • \(\ce{Zn^2+/Zn}\) \(-0.76\).
1. State the central principle of this block in one sentence, then give the two consequences.
2. Fill in:
  1. Standard conditions correspond to \(Q = \) 1
  2. At that point, \(E\) equals \(E^\circ\)
  3. At equilibrium, \(Q = \) \(K\) and \(E = \) 0
  4. A “dead” battery is one that has reached equilibrium
3. Explain why a dead battery is not an empty one.
4. For the Daniell cell, \(\ce{Zn(s) + Cu^2+(aq) -> Zn^2+(aq) + Cu(s)}\):
  1. Write \(Q\) for this reaction. \([\ce{Zn^2+}]/[\ce{Cu^2+}]\)
  2. Why do \(\ce{Zn(s)}\) and \(\ce{Cu(s)}\) not appear? they are pure solids
  3. Increasing \([\ce{Cu^2+}]\) changes \(Q\) how, and \(E\) how?
  4. Increasing \([\ce{Zn^2+}]\) changes \(Q\) how, and \(E\) how?
  5. Adding more solid zinc to the anode changes \(E\) how? not at all — it is a pure solid
5. For each change to a running Daniell cell, state whether \(E\) rises, falls, or is unchanged:
  1. Water is added to the cathode compartment, diluting \(\ce{Cu^2+}\) falls
  2. \(\ce{Na2S}\) is added to the cathode compartment, precipitating \(\ce{CuS}\) falls
  3. Solid \(\ce{CuSO4}\) is dissolved in the cathode compartment rises
  4. The zinc electrode is replaced with a larger zinc electrode unchanged
  5. The cell is allowed to run for several hours falls
6. Explain your answer to 5(b) fully — why does precipitating the cathode ion have a larger effect than simple dilution?
7. A student justifies an answer by writing “by Le Ch\^atelier's principle, removing a product shifts the equilibrium forward, so the voltage rises.” Explain why this reasoning is rejected here and give the correct version.
8. Concentration cells. Consider \(\ce{Cu} \mid \ce{Cu^2+}~(0.010\,\mathrm{M}) \parallel \ce{Cu^2+}~(1.0\,\mathrm{M}) \mid \ce{Cu}\).
  1. Calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\). \(0.00\) V
  2. Explain why the cell nevertheless produces a voltage.
  3. Which half-cell is the anode? Justify by asking what must happen to reach equilibrium.
  4. In which direction do electrons flow? from the dilute to the concentrated half-cell
  5. What happens to \(E\) as the cell runs, and what is its final value?
9. A concentration cell is built from silver electrodes in 0.10 M and 2.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\).
  1. Identify the cathode. the 2.0 M side
  2. Predict what happens to the mass of each electrode.
  3. Would a cell built from 1.0 M and 1.0 M \(\ce{AgNO3}\) produce any voltage? Explain.
