Worksheet 4 • Electrolysis, Faraday's Law & FRQ

Zumdahl §18.7–18.8 • the electron is a reagent; count it

Directions & data
\(I = q/t\) • \(F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}\) e\(^-\) • time in seconds. Molar masses (g/mol): \(\ce{Ag}\) 107.87 • \(\ce{Cu}\) 63.55 • \(\ce{Cr}\) 52.00 • \(\ce{Al}\) 26.98 •\ \(\ce{Ni}\) 58.69 • \(\ce{Au}\) 196.97.
1. Write the conversion chain from a measured current to a mass of plated metal, naming each step.
2. How many moles of electrons are needed to deposit one mole of each metal?
  1. \(\ce{Ag}\) from \(\ce{Ag+}\): 1
  2. \(\ce{Cu}\) from \(\ce{Cu^2+}\): 2
  3. \(\ce{Al}\) from \(\ce{Al^3+}\): 3
  4. \(\ce{Cr}\) from \(\ce{Cr^3+}\): 3
  5. Where does that number come from? the charge on the ion, via the half-reaction
3. A current of 2.00 A passes through \(\ce{AgNO3}\) solution for 30.0 min.
  1. Convert the time to seconds. 1800 s
  2. Calculate the charge passed. 3600 C
  3. Calculate the moles of electrons. 0.0373 mol
  4. Calculate the mass of silver deposited.
    \(\ce{Ag+ + e^- -> Ag}\), so mol Ag \(= 0.0373\); \(m = (0.0373)(107.87) = \mathbf{4.03}~\text{g}\)
4. The same charge is now passed through \(\ce{CuSO4}\) solution instead — 1.50 A for 1.00 h.
  1. Calculate the mass of copper deposited.
    \(q = (1.50)(3600) = 5400\) C; mol e\(^- = 5400/96485 = 0.0560\); \(\ce{Cu^2+ + 2e^- -> Cu}\) so mol Cu \(= 0.0280\); \(m = (0.0280)(63.55) = \mathbf{1.78}~\text{g}\)
  2. A student forgets to divide by 2 and reports 3.56 g. By what factor is that wrong, and what step did they skip?
5. Working backwards.
  1. How long, in hours, does it take to deposit 5.00 g of copper at 2.00 A?
    mol Cu \(= 5.00/63.55 = 0.0787\); mol e\(^- = 0.157\); \(q = (0.157)(96485) = 15{,}183\) C; \(t = 15183/2.00 = 7591\) s \(= \mathbf{2.11}\) h
  2. What current is needed to deposit 10.0 g of silver in exactly 1.00 h?
    mol Ag \(= 10.0/107.87 = 0.0927\); mol e\(^- = 0.0927\); \(q = 8945\) C; \(I = 8945/3600 = \mathbf{2.48}~\text{A}\)
  3. Chromium is plated from \(\ce{Cr^3+}\) at 5.00 A for 2.00 h. Find the mass.
    \(q = (5.00)(7200) = 36{,}000\) C; mol e\(^- = 0.373\); mol Cr \(= 0.373/3 = 0.124\); \(m = (0.124)(52.00) = \mathbf{6.47}~\text{g}\)
6. Two electrolytic cells are wired in series, so the same current passes through both for the same time. One contains \(\ce{AgNO3}\), the other \(\ce{CuSO4}\).
  1. Which receives more charge? the same — they are in series
  2. Which deposits more moles of metal, and why?
  3. Does that mean the silver electrode gains more mass? Explain.
7. Industrial scale.
  1. How much charge is required to produce 1.00 kg of aluminium from \(\ce{Al^3+}\)?
    mol Al \(= 1000/26.98 = 37.1\); mol e\(^- = 3(37.1) = 111\); \(q = (111)(96485) = \mathbf{1.07\times10^{7}}~\text{C}\)
  2. Explain, using two features of aluminium, why smelting it is so energy-intensive.
  3. Why does recycling aluminium save so much energy compared with producing it?
8. FRQ (10 points). An electrolytic cell is used to plate nickel onto a steel part from a solution of \(\ce{NiSO4}\). A current of 3.00 A is passed for 45.0 min.
  1. Write the half-reaction occurring at the cathode. \(\ce{Ni^2+ + 2e^- -> Ni}\)
  2. Explain why the part being plated must be the cathode.
  3. Calculate the mass of nickel deposited.
    \(t = 45.0 \times 60 = 2700\) s; \(q = (3.00)(2700) = 8100\) C; mol e\(^- = 8100/96485 = 0.0840\); mol Ni \(= 0.0840/2 = 0.0420\); \(m = (0.0420)(58.69) = \mathbf{2.46}~\text{g}\)
  4. Given \(E^\circ\) for \(\ce{Ni^2+/Ni}\) is \(-0.23\) V and for the anode reaction \(+1.23\) V, calculate \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) and state what it tells you about the cell.
    \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = -0.23 - 1.23 = \mathbf{-1.46}~\text{V}\). Negative, so the reaction is thermodynamically unfavored and the cell is electrolytic — it requires an external applied potential of more than 1.46 V.
  5. Calculate \(\Delta G^\circ\) for the cell reaction, taking \(n = 2\).
    \(\Delta G^\circ = -(2)(96485)(-1.46) = +281{,}736~\text{J} = \mathbf{+282}~\text{kJ}\) — positive, as expected for an unfavored process
