Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 20 minutes

Kinetics equations
\(\ln[\ce{A}]_t - \ln[\ce{A}]_0 = -kt\) •\ \(\dfrac{1}{[\ce{A}]_t} - \dfrac{1}{[\ce{A}]_0} = kt\) •\ \(t_{1/2} = \dfrac{0.693}{k}\) (first order) •\ \(\ln k = -\dfrac{E_a}{R}\left(\dfrac{1}{T}\right) + \ln A\) •\ \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\)

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

The decomposition \(\ce{2 H2O2(aq) -> 2 H2O(l) + O2(g)}\) is first order in \(\ce{H2O2}\), with \(k = 7.30e-4\,\mathrm{/s}\) at 20 °C. A solution is prepared with \([\ce{H2O2}]_0 = 0.500\,\mathrm{M}\).

  1. Calculate the half-life.
  2. Calculate \([\ce{H2O2}]\) after 1800 s.
  3. A student estimates the answer to (b) as 0.125 M by noting that 1800 s is close to two half-lives. Comment on this estimate.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

The overall reaction \(\ce{2 NO2 + F2 -> 2 NO2F}\) has the experimental rate law rate \(= k[\ce{NO2}][\ce{F2}]\). The proposed mechanism is \begin{align*} \ce{NO2 + F2 &-> NO2F + F} &&\text{slow}\\ \ce{NO2 + F &-> NO2F} &&\text{fast} \end{align*}

  1. Show that the mechanism is consistent with both the overall equation and the rate law.
  2. Identify the intermediate and explain how you know.
  3. Explain why the rate law contains \([\ce{NO2}]\) to the first power even though the balanced equation has a coefficient of 2.
