Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §12.1–§12.7 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 5.1 (§12.1), 5.2 (§12.2–12.3), 5.3 (§12.4), 5.4 and 5.7–5.9 (§12.5), 5.5, 5.6 and 5.10 (§12.6), and 5.11 (§12.7).

The CED exclusion on topic 5.6 removes calculations involving the Arrhenius equation. You will not be asked to compute an activation energy from two rate constants, or to use \(\ln k\) against \(1/T\) to extract \(E_a\). Reading an energy profile and reasoning qualitatively about how temperature and catalysts change a rate both remain fully examinable, and both appear below.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

For the reaction \(\ce{A + B -> C}\), the following initial rates were measured at constant temperature.

Experiment\([\ce{A}]_0\) (M)\([\ce{B}]_0\) (M)Initial rate (M/s)
10.100.10\(2.0\times10^{-3}\)
20.200.10\(8.0\times10^{-3}\)
30.100.20\(4.0\times10^{-3}\)
  1. Determine the order with respect to \(\ce{A}\).
  2. Determine the order with respect to \(\ce{B}\).
  3. Write the rate law and state the overall order.
  4. Calculate the value of \(k\), with units.
  5. Explain why the orders cannot be read off the balanced equation.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A proposed two-step mechanism for \(\ce{2NO2 + F2 -> 2NO2F}\) is \begin{align*} \text{Step 1:}\quad & \ce{NO2 + F2 -> NO2F + F} \qquad \text{(slow)}\\ \text{Step 2:}\quad & \ce{NO2 + F -> NO2F} \qquad\qquad \text{(fast)} \end{align*}

  1. Show that the mechanism sums to the overall equation.
  2. Identify any intermediate, and state how you recognized it.
  3. Write the rate law predicted by this mechanism, and justify it.
  4. Sketch in words how the energy profile for this two-step mechanism differs from that of a single-step reaction.
  5. Explain how a catalyst increases the rate, and state what it does not change.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A first-order decomposition has \(k = 0.0250\,\mathrm{/s}\).

  1. Calculate the half-life.
  2. Starting from 1.00 M, state the concentration remaining after three half-lives, and explain why the half-life does not change as the reaction proceeds.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

  1. State the two requirements for a collision to result in reaction.
  2. A modest rise in temperature can double a reaction rate, even though it raises the average molecular speed only slightly. Explain.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

For \(\ce{2N2O5 -> 4NO2 + O2}\), oxygen is produced at 0.0125 M/s.

  1. Determine the rate at which \(\ce{NO2}\) is formed and the rate at which \(\ce{N2O5}\) is consumed.
  2. State why a rate expressed as \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta[\ce{N2O5}]/\Delta t\) gives the same value as \(\tfrac{1}{4}\Delta[\ce{NO2}]/\Delta t\).
