Chapter 13 • Chemical Equilibrium

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §13.1–§13.7 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 7.1–7.2 (§13.1), 7.3 and 7.5 (§13.2, §13.5), 7.4 (§13.2–13.4), 7.6 (§13.3–13.4), 7.7 (§13.6), 7.8 (§13.1) and 7.9–7.10 (§13.7). Equilibrium sits in CED Unit 7 and is one of the most heavily tested ideas on the exam.

The CED exclusion on topic 7.3 removes converting between \(K_c\) and \(K_p\). Zumdahl derives \(K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}\) in §13.3; that conversion is not assessed and is not asked for here. Writing and using \(K_p\) directly from partial pressures remains examinable.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

At a certain temperature, \(K_c = 50.0\) for \[ \ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)} \] A vessel is charged with 1.00 M \(\ce{H2}\) and 1.00 M \(\ce{I2}\) and no \(\ce{HI}\).

  1. Write the equilibrium constant expression.
  2. Construct an ICE table for the reaction.
  3. Calculate the equilibrium concentration of \(\ce{HI}\).
  4. Explain why the approximation \(1.00 - x \approx 1.00\) would be invalid here.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

For the exothermic equilibrium \[ \ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)} \qquad \Delta H < 0 \] state and justify the effect of each change on the amount of \(\ce{NH3}\) at equilibrium.

  1. The volume of the container is decreased.
  2. The temperature is increased.
  3. A catalyst is added.
  4. At one moment the mixture contains \([\ce{N2}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\), \([\ce{H2}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) and \([\ce{NH3}] = 0.50\,\mathrm{M}\), and \(K_c = 0.50\). Determine the direction of net change.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Write the equilibrium constant expression for \(\ce{CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}\).
  2. Explain why the two solids are omitted.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

Two reactions at the same temperature have \(K = 1\times10^{12}\) and \(K = 1\times10^{-8}\).

  1. State what each value indicates about the position of equilibrium.
  2. State whether a large \(K\) means the reaction is fast, and explain.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. Explain what is meant by describing chemical equilibrium as dynamic.
  2. State what a graph of concentration against time looks like once equilibrium is reached, and what it would look like if the reaction had simply stopped.
