Chapter 13 • Chemical Equilibrium

Worksheet 2 • $Q$, $K$, and ICE Tables

Zumdahl §13.4–13.6 • always check the approximation

Directions & data
Show the full ICE table for every equilibrium calculation. When you use the small-\(x\) approximation, state the 5% check explicitly — an unchecked approximation earns no credit.
1. Write \(K\) for each heterogeneous equilibrium:
  1. \(\ce{Fe3O4(s) + 4 H2(g) <=> 3 Fe(s) + 4 H2O(g)}\): \([\ce{H2O}]^4/[\ce{H2}]^4\)
  2. \(\ce{2 NaHCO3(s) <=> Na2CO3(s) + CO2(g) + H2O(g)}\): \([\ce{CO2}][\ce{H2O}]\)
  3. Explain why pure solids and liquids are excluded.
2. Interpreting the magnitude of \(K\):
  1. \(K = 3\times10^{12}\): the equilibrium mixture is mostly products
  2. \(K = 2\times10^{-9}\): mostly reactants
  3. Does a large \(K\) imply a fast reaction? Explain.
3. For \(\ce{N2(g) + 3 H2(g) <=> 2 NH3(g)}\), \(K_c = 0.500\). A flask contains \([\ce{N2}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}\), \([\ce{H2}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}\), \([\ce{NH3}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}\).
  1. Calculate \(Q\).
    \(Q = (1.00)^2/[(1.00)(1.00)^3] = 1.00\)
  2. Compare \(Q\) with \(K\) and predict the direction of shift.
  3. What would \(Q\) have to equal for no shift to occur? 0.500
4. ICE table setup. Write the equilibrium row for each:
  1. \(\ce{2 HI <=> H2 + I2}\), starting with 0.400 M \(\ce{HI}\): \([\ce{HI}] = 0.400-2x\); \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = x\)
  2. \(\ce{PCl5 <=> PCl3 + Cl2}\), starting with 1.50 M \(\ce{PCl5}\): \(1.50-x\); \(x\); \(x\)
5. \(\ce{N2O4(g) <=> 2 NO2(g)}\) has \(K_c = 4.6\times10^{-3}\). A flask initially contains 1.00 M \(\ce{N2O4}\).
  1. Write the ICE table.
  2. Solve using the small-\(x\) approximation.
    \(4x^2/1.00 = 4.6\times10^{-3}\); \(x^2 = 1.15\times10^{-3}\); \(x = 0.0339\)
  3. Perform the 5% check and state whether the approximation is valid.
  4. Give both equilibrium concentrations. \([\ce{NO2}] = 0.068\) M; \([\ce{N2O4}] = 0.966\) M
6. \(\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2 HI(g)}\) has \(K = 50.0\). A flask initially contains 1.00 M of each reactant and no \(\ce{HI}\).
  1. Explain why the small-\(x\) approximation cannot be used here.
  2. Solve for \(x\). (The expression is a perfect square.)
    \((2x)^2/(1.00-x)^2 = 50.0\); take roots: \(2x/(1.00-x) = 7.071\); \(9.071x = 7.071\); \(x = 0.779\)
  3. Give all three equilibrium concentrations and verify against \(K\).
    \([\ce{H2}] = [\ce{I2}] = 0.221\) M; \([\ce{HI}] = 1.558\) M. Check: \((1.558)^2/(0.221)^2 = 49.7 \approx 50.0\) ✓
7. A student solving an equilibrium problem obtains the roots \(x = 0.45\) and \(x = 3.20\) from a quadratic, where the initial reactant concentration was 1.00 M. Which root is correct, and how do you know?
