Worksheet 3 • Shapes, Angles, Polarity

CED 2.7 • the full chain: Lewis $$ domains $$ shape $$ angle $$ polarity

Directions & data
Run the whole chain for every molecule — shape answers without the domain count shown earn half credit. Angle answers must name the lone-pair effect when there is one.
1. Complete the chain for each species (Lewis on scratch, then fill):
  1. \(\ce{SiF4}\): domains 4, shape tetrahedral, angle 109.5\(^\circ\), hybridization of Si sp\(^3\)
  2. \(\ce{H2S}\): domains 4 (2 LP), shape bent, angle \(<109.5^\circ\), polar? yes
  3. \(\ce{CS2}\): domains 2, shape linear, angle 180\(^\circ\), polar? no
  4. \(\ce{PCl3}\): domains 4 (1 LP), shape trigonal pyramidal, angle \(\approx107^\circ\), polar? yes
2. \(\ce{CH4}\), \(\ce{NH3}\), and \(\ce{H2O}\) all have four electron domains, yet their bond angles run 109.5\(^\circ\), 107\(^\circ\), 104.5\(^\circ\). Write the three-sentence grader answer: count, distinguish, conclude.
3. \(\sigma\)/\(\pi\) bookkeeping for acetylene, \(\ce{HC#CH}\):
  1. Total \(\sigma\): 3   Total \(\pi\): 2
  2. Hybridization of each C: sp
  3. Every atom lies on one line. Connect that to the domain count.
4. Polarity verdicts — geometry argument required:
  1. \(\ce{CCl4}\): nonpolar — 4 equal dipoles, tetrahedral, cancel
  2. \(\ce{CH2Cl2}\): polar — unequal corners, no cancellation
  3. \(\ce{XeF2}\) (linear, 3 LP on Xe): nonpolar — two opposite dipoles cancel
5. A molecule \(\ce{AB3}\) is measured to be nonpolar with 120\(^\circ\) angles; another \(\ce{AB3}\) molecule is polar with angles near 107\(^\circ\). Deduce the electron-domain picture of each central atom.
