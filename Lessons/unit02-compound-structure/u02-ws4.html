Directions & data
The unit exam's FRQ is this shape and length. Write everything first; the rubric is printed after the last part. Score like a reader: no point for anything not on the page.
1. Long FRQ (10 points). Answer all parts. Nitrogen and oxygen form several species; this question examines \(\ce{NO2-}\) and \(\ce{NO2+}\).
  1. Give the total valence electron count for each species.
  2. Draw a complete Lewis diagram for \(\ce{NO2+}\).
    O=N=O, linear skeleton, 2 lone pairs per O, no lone pair on N; brackets, \(+\).
  3. Draw BOTH resonance forms of \(\ce{NO2-}\).
    O=N–O \(\leftrightarrow\) O–N=O, lone pair ON N, brackets, \(-\).
  4. Predict the O–N–O bond angle in each species and justify the difference.
  5. In \(\ce{NO2-}\), the two N–O bonds are equal in length. Explain using your diagrams from (c).
  6. Predict which species has the shorter N–O bonds, and justify with bond order.
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 1 pt both counts correct, charge handled both directions. (b) 2 pts 16 e\(^-\) structure: 1 for correct connectivity/multiple bonds, 1 for lone pairs + charge bracket. (c) 2 pts both forms, lone pair on N, 18 e\(^-\) each. (d) 2 pts 1 for each angle WITH domain-count justification; bare angles earn 1 max. (e) 1 pt hybrid/average language with equal order — “flipping between forms” earns 0. (f) 2 pts 1 for \(\ce{NO2+}\), 1 for the order comparison \(2 > 3/2\).
