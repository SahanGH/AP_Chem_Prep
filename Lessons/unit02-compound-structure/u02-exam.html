Unit Exam

Section I • Multiple Choice 20 questions • 30 minutes • 1 point each

1. Which bond is the most polar?
  1. \(\ce{C-H}\)
  2. \(\ce{N-H}\)
  3. \(\ce{O-H}\)
  4. \(\ce{O-F}\)
2. Solid magnesium conducts electricity, but solid \(\ce{MgCl2}\) does not. The best explanation is that
  1. Mg has delocalized valence electrons; \(\ce{MgCl2}\) has ions fixed in a lattice
  2. \(\ce{MgCl2}\) contains no charged particles
  3. Mg atoms are smaller than \(\ce{Mg^2+}\) ions
  4. \(\ce{MgCl2}\) has covalent bonds that trap electrons
3. A compound melts at 801\(^\circ\)C, dissolves in water, conducts only when molten or dissolved, and shatters when struck. Its bonding is best described as
  1. metallic
  2. nonpolar covalent
  3. ionic
  4. polar covalent molecular
4. On a potential-energy versus internuclear-distance curve for two atoms, the equilibrium bond length is found at
  1. the distance where the energy equals zero
  2. the distance at the minimum of the curve
  3. the distance where attraction disappears
  4. the largest distance plotted
5. \(\ce{H2}\) has bond energy 432 kJ/mol and length 74 pm; \(\ce{F2}\) has bond energy 155 kJ/mol and length 142 pm. Compared with \(\ce{H2}\), the PE curve for \(\ce{F2}\) has a minimum that is
  1. deeper and at shorter distance
  2. deeper and at longer distance
  3. shallower and at shorter distance
  4. shallower and at longer distance
6. Which compound has the largest lattice energy magnitude?
  1. \(\ce{NaCl}\)
  2. \(\ce{KCl}\)
  3. \(\ce{MgO}\)
  4. \(\ce{CaS}\)
7. Ionic solids are brittle because an applied stress
  1. breaks the covalent bonds within each molecule
  2. slides layers so that like charges align and repel
  3. releases electrons from the lattice
  4. converts the solid to its molten form
8. \(\ce{LiF}\) and \(\ce{KBr}\) have ions of the same charges. \(\ce{LiF}\)'s melting point is much higher because
  1. its smaller ions sit closer, strengthening the attraction
  2. fluorine is more electronegative than bromine
  3. \(\ce{LiF}\) has stronger covalent character
  4. K and Br have more electrons to share
9. Adding a small percentage of carbon to iron makes steel harder than pure iron because the carbon atoms
  1. replace iron atoms in the lattice
  2. form ionic bonds with iron
  3. occupy interstitial holes and hinder layers from sliding
  4. increase the number of delocalized electrons
10. Which property is common to BOTH metallic solids and ionic solids?
  1. electrical conductivity in the solid state
  2. malleability
  3. attraction arising from electrostatic forces
  4. discrete molecules in the lattice
11. The total number of valence electrons in the sulfite ion, \(\ce{SO3^2-}\), is
  1. 24
  2. 26
  3. 25
  4. 32
12. In the best Lewis diagram for \(\ce{HCN}\) (C central), the number of lone pairs on the nitrogen atom is
  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 0
13. Which molecule has a central atom with LESS than an octet in its accepted Lewis diagram?
  1. \(\ce{NH3}\)

  4. \(\ce{CO2}\)
14. In a Lewis diagram of \(\ce{CO2}\) drawn with one single bond and one triple bond, the formal charge on the single-bonded oxygen is
  1. \(-1\)
  2. \(0\)
  3. \(+1\)
  4. \(-2\)
15. Experimental evidence that \(\ce{NO3-}\) is a resonance hybrid is that

16. The carbonate ion \(\ce{CO3^2-}\) has three resonance forms. The carbon–oxygen bond order is

Section II • Free Response 1 question • 20 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Carbon dioxide (\(\ce{CO2}\)) and sulfur dioxide (\(\ce{SO2}\)) are both triatomic oxides, yet they differ in shape and polarity. Answer all parts.

  1. Draw the complete Lewis diagram of \(\ce{CO2}\), and one complete resonance form of \(\ce{SO2}\). Show all lone pairs.
  2. Identify the molecular geometry and approximate bond angle of each molecule.
  3. Explain why the two geometries differ, in terms of electron domains.
  4. One of these molecules is polar. Identify it and justify using bond dipoles and geometry.
  5. The two S–O bonds in \(\ce{SO2}\) are found to be equal in length. Explain this observation.
  6. Solid \(\ce{CO2}\) (dry ice) sublimes at \(-78^\circ\)C, while \(\ce{MgO}\) melts near 2850\(^\circ\)C. Identify the particles occupying each solid's lattice and explain the enormous difference.
