Unit 2 • Compound Structure and Properties

Worksheet 1 • Bonds, Wells, and Lattices

CED 2.1–2.4 • classify, rank, and justify

Directions & data
Every ranking needs its reason — “charges first, then radii” is a full sentence here. One-word answers earn half credit at best.
1. Classify the bonding in each (nonpolar covalent / polar covalent / ionic / metallic) with a one-phrase reason:
  1. \(\ce{N2}\): nonpolar covalent — identical atoms
  2. \(\ce{SrCl2}\): ionic — metal + nonmetal
  3. \(\ce{ICl}\): polar covalent — nonmetals, small \(\Delta\)EN
  4. bronze (Cu/Sn): metallic — delocalized e\(^-\) sea
2. Rank by increasing bond polarity, then state the trend that decides it: \(\ce{O-F}\), \(\ce{O-H}\), \(\ce{O-O}\).
3. A potential-energy curve for \(\ce{Cl2}\) has its minimum at 199 pm and \(-243\) kJ/mol.
  1. Bond length: 199 pm   Bond energy: 243 kJ/mol
  2. At distances shorter than 199 pm, the curve rises steeply. What interaction dominates there? nucleus–nucleus repulsion
  3. \(\ce{Br2}\)'s well: 228 pm, \(-193\) kJ/mol. On shared axes, sketch both curves, labeled.
    \(\ce{Br2}\) minimum farther right (228) and shallower (\(-193\)); \(\ce{Cl2}\) deeper, shorter.
  4. Which bond is easier to break, and what number says so? \(\ce{Br2}\) — smaller well depth (193 kJ/mol)
4. Rank by lattice energy magnitude, largest first: \(\ce{KBr}\), \(\ce{CaO}\), \(\ce{KCl}\), \(\ce{SrO}\).
5. Solid \(\ce{MgO}\) melts at 2852\(^\circ\)C; solid \(\ce{NaF}\) at 993\(^\circ\)C. Both lattices have similar ion sizes. Explain the gap with Coulomb's law.
6. A jeweler's gold alloy substitutes Cu atoms (similar radius) into the Au lattice; steel inserts small C atoms into holes in the Fe lattice.
  1. Name each alloy type: Au/Cu substitutional; steel interstitial
  2. Both alloys are harder than the pure metal. Give the particle-level reason.
  3. Why does the alloy still conduct electricity?

  3. PES: which peak has the greatest area for Si, and why? 2p — 6 electrons, most in any subshell
