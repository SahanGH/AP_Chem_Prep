By the end of this unit you can…

  • Classify bonds as ionic, polar covalent, nonpolar covalent, or metallic from electronegativity and position — and say why the categories blur. CED 2.1
  • Read a potential-energy vs. distance curve: bond length at the minimum, bond energy as the well depth. CED 2.2
  • Rank lattice energies with Coulomb's law (charges first, then radii). CED 2.3
  • Explain metallic bonding and predict how interstitial and substitutional alloys change properties. CED 2.4
  • Draw Lewis diagrams fast and correctly, including the octet exceptions worth knowing. CED 2.5
  • Use formal charge to choose between candidate structures; draw resonance sets and state what they mean for bond length. CED 2.6
  • Predict electron-domain and molecular geometry, bond angles, hybridization (sp, sp\(^2\), sp\(^3\)), and molecular polarity. CED 2.7
Where students lose points in this unit

Drawing one resonance form and calling it “the” structure; skipping the formal-charge check that picks the best skeleton; claiming a molecule is polar because it “has polar bonds” (symmetric \(\ce{CO2}\) says otherwise); quoting 109.5\(^\circ\) for bent molecules without the lone-pair compression argument; and explaining lattice energy by size when the charges differ — charges dominate.
