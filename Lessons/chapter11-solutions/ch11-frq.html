Chapter 11 • Properties of Solutions

Free-Response Set

Zumdahl §11.1–§11.3 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — most of this chapter is excluded

Only §11.1–11.3 are examinable, as CED 3.7 (solutions and mixtures) and 3.10 (solubility). Read this before assigning:

The CED exclusion on topic 3.8 removes colligative properties entirely, and with them calculations of molality, percent by mass and percent by volume. That takes §11.4–11.8 — vapour-pressure lowering, boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression, osmotic pressure, the van't Hoff factor and colloids — off the exam completely. None of it appears below.

Concentration on the AP exam means molarity. Every calculation here uses it.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

Dissolving an ionic solid in water can be broken into steps: separating the ions from the lattice, and hydrating them.

  1. State the sign of the enthalpy change for each step, with a reason.
  2. Explain how the relative sizes of those two terms determine whether dissolving is exothermic or endothermic overall.
  3. Ammonium nitrate dissolves readily in water even though the solution becomes noticeably colder. Explain how a process that absorbs energy can still occur spontaneously.
  4. Describe how water molecules arrange themselves around a dissolved \(\ce{Na+}\) ion and around a dissolved \(\ce{Cl-}\) ion.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

  1. Explain, using “like dissolves like”, why sodium chloride dissolves readily in water but not in hexane, \(\ce{C6H14}\).
  2. The solubility of most solid solutes rises with temperature, but the solubility of a gas in water falls. Explain the gas behaviour.
  3. A sealed bottle of carbonated water fizzes when opened. Explain in terms of the pressure dependence of gas solubility.
  4. State whether pressure has a comparable effect on the solubility of a solid, and why.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

  1. Calculate the mass of \(\ce{NaCl}\) needed to prepare 250.0 mL of a 0.150 M solution.
  2. Describe why the solid is dissolved in less than 250 mL of water and the solution then made up to the mark, rather than being added to 250 mL of water.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

Ethanol (\(\ce{CH3CH2OH}\)) is miscible with water in all proportions. Hexane is not.

  1. Explain the difference in terms of intermolecular forces.
  2. Predict, with a reason, whether 1-octanol (\(\ce{CH3(CH2)7OH}\)) is as soluble in water as ethanol.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

A 0.200 M solution of \(\ce{Al2(SO4)3}\) is prepared and is fully dissociated.

  1. Calculate the concentration of aluminium ions and of sulfate ions.
  2. Calculate the total concentration of ions in the solution.
