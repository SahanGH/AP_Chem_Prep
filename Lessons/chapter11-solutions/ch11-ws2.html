Worksheet 2 • The Energetics of Dissolving

Zumdahl §11.2 • three steps, two endothermic, one exothermic

Directions & data
Every explanation on this sheet should name which of the three steps is responsible. “Like dissolves like” is a conclusion, not a reason.
1. The three-step model of solution formation:
  1. Name each step and give the sign of its \(\Delta H\).
  2. Write the expression for \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\).
  3. For an ionic solute, what are steps 1 and 3 usually called?
2. Predict the temperature change and identify a use:
  1. \(\ce{NH4NO3}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = +25.7\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\):
  2. \(\ce{CaCl2}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = -82.8\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\):
  3. \(\ce{NaCl}\), \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = +3.9\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\):
3. \(\ce{NaCl}\) has a very large lattice energy, yet it dissolves readily in water with \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) of only +3.9 kJ/mol. Explain how such a small net value arises from such large individual terms.
4. Use the three-step model to explain each observation:
  1. Oil does not dissolve in water.
  2. Ethanol mixes with water in all proportions.
  3. \(\ce{NaCl}\) does not dissolve in hexane.
5. A student states: “Dissolving is always exothermic because new attractions form.” Evaluate this claim.
6. Some substances dissolve even though \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) is positive. What does this reveal about the driving force for dissolving?
7. Explain, energetically, why “like dissolves like” works — without simply restating the phrase.
