Chapter 11 • Properties of Solutions

Chapter Test

Zumdahl §11.1–11.3 only • 50 minutes • see scope note

Scope

This test covers §11.1–11.3 only. Nothing from §11.4–11.8 — vapour pressure lowering, boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression, osmotic pressure, or colloids — appears. Those were marked enrichment in the notes and are not on the AP framework.

Section II • Free Response 2 questions • 20 minutes

Free-response 1 • short • 4 points

A solution is prepared by dissolving 18.0 g of glucose (\(\ce{C6H12O6}\), \(M = 180.16\,\mathrm{g/mol}\)) in 182.0 g of water. The resulting solution has a volume of 190. mL.

  1. Calculate the molality of the solution.
  2. Calculate the molarity of the solution.
  3. The solution is warmed to 60 °C. State what happens to each of your two answers, with a reason.

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

Two salts are dissolved separately in water. Salt X causes the solution to warm; salt Y causes it to cool.

  1. State the sign of \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) for each and justify from the observation.
  2. Using the three-step model, explain what must be true of the hydration energy for salt Y.
  3. A student claims salt Y cannot dissolve because dissolving it requires energy. Evaluate this claim.
