Directions & data
Problems 1–4 are exam-relevant. Problem 5 is marked OPTIONAL — colligative properties are not on the AP exam.
1. Predict which is more soluble in water and give the structural reason:
  1. \(\ce{CH3CH2OH}\) or \(\ce{CH3(CH2)6CH2OH}\)
  2. \(\ce{NH3}\) or \(\ce{PH3}\)
  3. \(\ce{KNO3}\) or \(\ce{I2}\)
2. Vitamins A and C.
  1. Vitamin A is largely carbon and hydrogen; vitamin C carries several O–H and C–O bonds. Classify each as hydrophobic or hydrophilic.
  2. Explain why excess vitamin A can cause hypervitaminosis while excess vitamin C generally does not.
3. Pressure and temperature effects. Complete the table:
Solids in waterGases in water
Increasing pressure

4. Explain each observation:
  1. An opened bottle of soda goes flat.
  2. A warm soda fizzes more violently than a cold one when opened.
  3. A power plant discharging warm water can suffocate fish.
  4. A student claims heating always dissolves more solute. Correct them precisely.
5. FRQ (10 points). A student investigates the dissolving of two ionic salts in water. Salt A causes the solution temperature to fall by 8.2 °C; salt B causes it to rise by 11.5 °C.
  1. State the sign of \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) for each salt and name the process.
  2. Using the three-step model, explain how salt A can dissolve at all despite absorbing energy.
  3. For salt B, state which energy term must be unusually large, and identify it by name.
  4. Which salt would be suitable for an instant cold pack, and which for a hand warmer?
  5. The student dissolves 0.150 mol of salt A in 200.0 g of water. Calculate \(\Delta H_{\text{soln}}\) in kJ/mol, assuming the solution has the specific heat of water (4.184 J/g/°C).
Rubric — score yourself, 10 points
(a) 2 pts 1 per salt, sign AND term • (b) 3 pts 1 identifies steps 1+2 exceeding step 3, 1 states \(\Delta H > 0\), 1 invokes disorder/entropy as the driver • (c) 2 pts 1 names step 3, 1 calls it hydration energy • (d) 1 pt both assignments • (e) 2 pts 1 flips the sign from solution to system, 1 correct value per mole.
6. OPTIONAL — enrichment only. Colligative properties are not assessed on the AP exam.
  1. Define a colligative property.
  2. Rank by freezing-point depression at equal molality: glucose, \(\ce{NaCl}\), \(\ce{CaCl2}\). Explain.
  3. Explain why a red blood cell bursts in pure water.
