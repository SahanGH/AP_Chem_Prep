Chapter 11 • Properties of Solutions

Worksheet 1 • Solution Composition

Zumdahl §11.1 • litres of SOLUTION vs kilograms of SOLVENT

Directions & data
State which denominator each calculation uses before you compute. Most errors on this sheet are denominator errors, not arithmetic errors.
1. Write the defining formula for each:
  1. Molarity: mol solute / L solution
  2. Molality: mol solute / kg solvent
  3. Mass percent: (mass solute / mass solution)\(\times\)100
  4. Mole fraction: \(n_A / n_{\text{total}}\)
2. A solution is made by dissolving 20.0 g of \(\ce{KCl}\) (\(M = 74.55\)) in 180.0 g of water. The final volume is 190. mL.
  1. Moles of \(\ce{KCl}\): 0.268 mol
  2. Molarity: 1.41 M
  3. Molality: 1.49 mol/kg
  4. Mass percent: 10.0%
  5. Mole fraction of \(\ce{KCl}\):
    \(n(\ce{H2O}) = 180.0/18.02 = 9.989\,\mathrm{mol}\); \(\chi = 0.268/(0.268+9.989) = 0.0261\)
3. Explain why molarity changes with temperature but molality does not.
4. A student adds 0.500 mol of solute to exactly 1.000 L of water and labels the bottle “0.500 M.”
  1. Identify the error.
  2. What quantity is exactly 0.500 for this solution? the molality (per kg water)
  3. How should the solution have been prepared?
5. Which concentration measure is most appropriate, and why?
  1. Calculating the partial pressure of one gas in a mixture: mole fraction
  2. Titrating an acid of known volume: molarity
  3. Reporting the composition of a saline solution to a patient: mass percent
6. A 500.0 g sample of aqueous glucose (\(\ce{C6H12O6}\), \(M = 180.16\)) is 12.0% glucose by mass.
  1. Mass of glucose present: 60.0 g
  2. Moles of glucose: 0.333 mol
  3. Mass of water: 440.0 g
  4. Molality: 0.757 mol/kg
