How this paper runs
Three sessions, not one. Session 1 (Parts A–B) covers rate laws and integrated rate laws — the core of the unit and the largest session. Session 2 (Parts C–E) covers mechanisms, energetics, and catalysis. Session 3 (Part F) is a challenge set that goes past what the AP exam asks. Do not attempt more than one session in a single sitting.

Calculator, periodic table, and equations sheet are permitted throughout. Show every step. A bare final answer earns the answer mark only; the setup marks are for work you can point to. Carry units through each line and round only at the end.

Scope — read before Session 2

One CED boundary shapes this paper more than any other: CED 5.6 excludes calculations involving the Arrhenius equation (\(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\)) from the AP exam. You will still be asked to read an energy profile, identify \(E_a\), and reason about how temperature and catalysts change a rate — all core CED content. But solving \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\) numerically, whether for \(E_a\), \(A\), or \(k\) itself, is flagged [beyond CED] throughout Parts D, E, and F. It is here because Arrhenius calculations are standard chemistry and because Unit 9 electrochemistry leans on the same algebra.

For \(\ce{4PH3(g) -> P4(g) + 6H2(g)}\), phosphine is consumed at 0.0048 mol/s in a 2.0 L container.

Determine the rate of production of \(\ce{P4}\) and of \(\ce{H2}\).
Step 1 — convert to a concentration rate. \(-\dfrac{d[\ce{PH3}]}{dt} = \dfrac{0.0048\,\mathrm{mol/s}} {2.0\,\mathrm{L}} = 0.0024\,\mathrm{M/s}\) Step 2 — divide by \(\ce{PH3}\)'s coefficient to reach the rate of reaction: \(\text{rate} = 0.0024/4 = 6.0e-4\,\mathrm{M/s}\) Step 3 — multiply by each product's coefficient. \(\dfrac{d[\ce{P4}]}{dt} = 1 \times 6.0\times10^{-4} = \mathbf{6.0\times10^{-4}}\) M/s \(\dfrac{d[\ce{H2}]}{dt} = 6 \times 6.0\times10^{-4} = \mathbf{3.6\times10^{-3}}\) M/s

Free-response 2 • short • 4 points

For \(\ce{2H2O2(aq) -> 2H2O(l) + O2(g)}\) at 40 °C: \([\ce{H2O2}] = 1.000\,\mathrm{M}\) at \(t=0\), 0.500 M at \(t = 2.16e4\,\mathrm{s}\), 0.250 M at \(t = 4.32e4\,\mathrm{s}\).

Calculate the average rate of \(\ce{H2O2}\) decomposition, and the corresponding average rate of \(\ce{O2}\) production, over each interval.

Section B • Integrated Rate Laws and Half-Life 17 questions • 92 points

The three straight-line tests

Every item in this section reduces to one question: which quantity, plotted against time, gives a straight line? \([\ce{A}]\) linear \(\Rightarrow\) zero order. \(\ln[\ce{A}]\) linear \(\Rightarrow\) first order. \(1/[\ce{A}]\) linear \(\Rightarrow\) second order. Do not guess the order from how the curve looks — test a transform and check that it is actually straight.
