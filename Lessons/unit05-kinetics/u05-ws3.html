Worksheet 3 • Mechanisms and Energy Profiles

CED 5.4–5.8 • molecularity gives the order only for an elementary step

Directions & data
Elementary step: coefficients are orders •\ \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\) •\ \(\ln(k_2/k_1) = -\tfrac{E_a}{R}\left(\tfrac{1}{T_2}-\tfrac{1}{T_1}\right)\) • \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}\) •\ \(E_a(\text{fwd}) - E_a(\text{rev}) = \Delta H\).
1. CED 5.4 Write the rate law for each elementary step:
  1. \(\ce{A -> products}\) rate \(= k[\ce{A}]\)
  2. \(\ce{A + B -> products}\) rate \(= k[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]\)
  3. \(\ce{2A -> products}\) rate \(= k[\ce{A}]^2\)
  4. \(\ce{2NO + O2 -> 2NO2}\) as a single step rate \(= k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]\)
  5. Why is (d) an unlikely elementary step?
2. CED 5.4 Explain precisely why coefficients give orders for an elementary step but not for an overall equation.
3. CED 5.7–CED 5.8 Overall: \(\ce{2NO2 + F2 -> 2NO2F}\), experimental rate \(= k[\ce{NO2}][\ce{F2}]\). Proposed mechanism: step 1 (slow) \(\ce{NO2 + F2 -> NO2F + F}\); step 2 (fast) \(\ce{NO2 + F -> NO2F}\).
  1. Show that the steps sum to the overall equation.
  2. Give the predicted rate law and say why.
  3. Does the mechanism pass both tests? yes
  4. Identify the intermediate. \(\ce{F}\)
  5. Does passing both tests prove the mechanism is correct? Explain.
4. CED 5.8 For the mechanism \(\ce{A2 -> 2A}\) (slow), then \(\ce{A + B -> AB}\) (fast):
  1. Overall equation: \(\ce{A2 + 2B -> 2AB}\)
  2. Predicted rate law: rate \(= k[\ce{A2}]\)
  3. B is a reactant but absent from the rate law. Is that a problem?
5. CED 5.5 The collision model.
  1. List the three requirements for a reactive collision.
  2. Why is the fraction of successful collisions so small?
  3. Raising the temperature increases the rate far more than it increases collision frequency. Explain.
6. CED 5.5 Arrhenius calculations.
  1. A reaction with \(E_a = 50.0\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\) is warmed from 300 K to 310 K. By what factor does \(k\) change?
    \(\ln(k_2/k_1) = -(50000/8.314) (1/310 - 1/300) = 0.647\); \(k_2/k_1 = e^{0.647} = \mathbf{1.9}\) — roughly double
  2. Repeat for \(E_a = 100.0\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\). \(\times 3.6\)
  3. What general conclusion follows?
  4. A reaction has \(k = 2.0e-3\,\mathrm{/s}\) at 300 K and 8.0e-3 /s at 320 K. Find \(E_a\).
    \(\ln(8.0/2.0) = 1.386 = -(E_a/8.314)(1/320 - 1/300)\); \(E_a = \mathbf{5.5\times10^{4}}~\mathrm{J/mol} = 55\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}\)
7. CED 5.6 Energy profiles.
  1. \(E_a(\text{fwd}) = 80\,\mathrm{kJ}\), \(E_a(\text{rev}) = 120\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Find \(\Delta H\) and classify. \(-40\) kJ; exothermic
  2. \(E_a(\text{fwd}) = 150\,\mathrm{kJ}\), \(\Delta H = +60\,\mathrm{kJ}\). Find \(E_a(\text{rev})\). 90 kJ
  3. On a profile, \(E_a\) is measured from what to what? reactants up to the transition state
  4. Can a strongly exothermic reaction be very slow? Explain.
