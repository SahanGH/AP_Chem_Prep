Worksheet 4 • Pre-Equilibrium, Catalysis & FRQ

CED 5.9–5.11 • a final rate law never contains an intermediate

Directions & data
Fast first step: write the slow step's rate law, then use the equilibrium of step 1 to eliminate the intermediate • a catalyst lowers \(E_a\) for both directions and changes neither \(\Delta H\) nor \(K\).
1. CED 5.9 Overall \(\ce{2NO + O2 -> 2NO2}\), experimental rate \(= k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]\). Mechanism: step 1 (fast equilibrium) \(\ce{2NO <=> N2O2}\); step 2 (slow) \(\ce{N2O2 + O2 -> 2NO2}\).
  1. Write the rate law for the slow step as it stands. rate \(= k_2[\ce{N2O2}][\ce{O2}]\)
  2. Why is that not yet an acceptable rate law?
  3. Use step 1's equilibrium to express \([\ce{N2O2}]\). \([\ce{N2O2}] = K_1[\ce{NO}]^2\)
  4. Substitute and give the predicted rate law.
    rate \(= k_2K_1[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}] = \mathbf{k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]}\), matching experiment, with the observed \(k = k_2K_1\)
  5. What does it mean that the observed \(k\) equals \(k_2K_1\)?
2. CED 5.9 Derive the rate law for: \(\ce{A + B <=> C}\) (fast equilibrium), then \(\ce{C + B -> D}\) (slow).
Slow step: rate \(= k_2[\ce{C}][\ce{B}]\). From step 1, \(K_1 = [\ce{C}]/([\ce{A}][\ce{B}])\), so \([\ce{C}] = K_1[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]\). Substituting gives rate \(= k_2K_1[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]^2 = \mathbf{k[\ce{A}][\ce{B}]^2}\) — note B ends up second order.
3. CED 5.10 Multistep energy profiles.
  1. The number of humps equals the number of: elementary steps
  2. A valley between two humps is: an intermediate
  3. The rate-determining step is the one with: the highest activation barrier
  4. A profile shows a first barrier of 95 kJ and a second of 60 kJ measured from the intermediate. Which step is rate-determining? the first
  5. If instead the second barrier were the larger, what would change about the rate law?
4. CED 5.11 Catalysis.
  1. A catalyst increases the rate by providing: an alternative pathway with lower \(E_a\)
  2. List three quantities a catalyst does not change. \(\Delta H\), \(K\), \(\Delta G^\circ\)
  3. Explain why a catalyst cannot shift an equilibrium.
  4. Distinguish homogeneous from heterogeneous catalysis. same phase as reactants vs different phase
5. CED 5.11 For the mechanism \(\ce{H2O2 + I- -> H2O + IO-}\), then \(\ce{H2O2 + IO- -> H2O + O2 + I-}\):
  1. Overall equation: \(\ce{2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2}\)
  2. Catalyst: \(\ce{I-}\)
  3. Intermediate: \(\ce{IO-}\)
  4. State the test that distinguishes them.
6. FRQ (10 points). CED 5.2 CED 5.3 CED 5.8 The gas-phase reaction \(\ce{2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\) was studied at constant temperature.
Exp\([\ce{NO}]_0\) (M)\([\ce{O2}]_0\) (M)initial rate (M/s)
10.0100.010\(2.5\times10^{-5}\)
20.0200.010\(1.0\times10^{-4}\)
30.0100.020\(5.0\times10^{-5}\)
  1. Determine the order in \(\ce{NO}\) and in \(\ce{O2}\), showing the comparison used for each.
  2. Write the rate law and calculate \(k\) with units.
    rate \(= k[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]\); \(k = 2.5\times10^{-5}/[(0.010)^2(0.010)] = \mathbf{25}~\mathrm{/M^2/s}\)
  3. Predict the initial rate when \([\ce{NO}] = 0.030\) and \([\ce{O2}] = 0.015\).
    \((25)(0.030)^2(0.015) = (25)(9.0\times10^{-4})(0.015) = \mathbf{3.4\times10^{-4}}~\mathrm{M/s}\)
  4. A student proposes that the reaction occurs in a single termolecular step. State whether this is consistent with the rate law, and whether it is likely.
  5. Show that the mechanism \(\ce{2NO <=> N2O2}\) (fast equilibrium) followed by \(\ce{N2O2 + O2 -> 2NO2}\) (slow) predicts the same rate law.
    Slow step: rate \(= k_2[\ce{N2O2}][\ce{O2}]\). From the equilibrium, \([\ce{N2O2}] = K_1[\ce{NO}]^2\). Substituting: rate \(= k_2K_1[\ce{NO}]^2[\ce{O2}]\), which has the observed form.
