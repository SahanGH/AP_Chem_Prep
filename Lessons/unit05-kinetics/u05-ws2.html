Worksheet 2 • Concentration Over Time

CED 5.3 • say which plot is linear, not just ``the graph''

Directions & data
Zero: \([\ce{A}] = -kt + [\ce{A}]_0\), \(t_{1/2} = [\ce{A}]_0/2k\) • First: \(\ln[\ce{A}] = -kt + \ln[\ce{A}]_0\), \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\) • Second: \(1/[\ce{A}] = kt + 1/[\ce{A}]_0\), \(t_{1/2} = 1/(k[\ce{A}]_0)\).
1. CED 5.3 Complete the table from memory, then check it against the data strip:
  1. Zero order — linear plot: \([\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\), slope \(-k\)
  2. First order — linear plot: \(\ln[\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\), slope \(-k\)
  3. Second order — linear plot: \(1/[\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\), slope \(+k\)
2. CED 5.3 A first-order reaction has \(k = 0.0250\,\mathrm{/s}\) and \([\ce{A}]_0 = 0.500\,\mathrm{M}\).
  1. Find \([\ce{A}]\) after 60.0 s.
    \(\ln[\ce{A}] = -(0.0250)(60.0) + \ln(0.500) = -1.50 - 0.693 = -2.193\); \([\ce{A}] = e^{-2.193} = \mathbf{0.112}~\mathrm{M}\)
  2. Find the half-life. 27.7 s
  3. After two half-lives, what fraction remains, and does your answer to (a) look consistent?
3. CED 5.3 A second-order reaction has \(k = 0.15\,\mathrm{/M/s}\) and \([\ce{A}]_0 = 0.20\,\mathrm{M}\).
  1. Find \([\ce{A}]\) after 100 s.
    \(1/[\ce{A}] = (0.15)(100) + 1/0.20 = 15 + 5.0 = 20\); \([\ce{A}] = \mathbf{0.050}~\mathrm{M}\)
  2. Find the half-life. 33 s
  3. After the first half-life, will the second take the same time, less, or more? Justify.
4. CED 5.3 A zero-order reaction has \(k = 0.020\,\mathrm{M/s}\) and \([\ce{A}]_0 = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}\).
  1. Find \(t_{1/2}\). 25 s
  2. Find \([\ce{A}]\) after 30 s. 0.40 M
  3. At what time does the reactant run out completely? What does that tell you about zero-order behaviour?
5. CED 5.3 Identify the order from each observation:
  1. Half-life is 25.0 min at the start and 25.0 min again later: first order
  2. \(k\) for that reaction: 0.0277 /min
  3. A plot of \(1/[\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\) is a straight line with slope \(+0.42\): second order, \(k = 0.42\) /M/s
  4. A plot of \([\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\) is a straight line: zero order
6. CED 5.3 A student writes “the graph was linear, so the reaction is first order.” Explain why this earns no credit and write the sentence that would.
7. CED 5.3 Why is a constant half-life such strong evidence of first-order kinetics?
8. CED 5.3 A radioactive isotope has a half-life of 8.0 d. A sample starts at 40.0 mg.
  1. Mass remaining after 24 d: 5.0 mg
  2. How did you get it without using the integrated rate law?
  3. Radioactive decay is always first order. What does that imply about the time to go from 40.0 to 20.0 mg versus 20.0 to 10.0 mg? the same — 8.0 d each
Spiral review • Unit 5 • blocks 1–2
  1. Orders come from: experiment
  2. Units of \(k\), first order: /s
  3. Doubling [A] quadruples the rate; order in A? 2
  4. Rate for \(\ce{2A -> B}\) in terms of A: \(-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta[\ce{A}]/\Delta t\)
  5. Initial rate is measured at: \(t=0\)
