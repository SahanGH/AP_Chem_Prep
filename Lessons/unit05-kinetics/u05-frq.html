Unit 5 • Kinetics

Free-Response Set

CED 5.1–5.11 • 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Scope — one CED exclusion applies

Covers CED 5.1 through CED 5.11, worth 7 %–9 % of the exam.

Topic CED 5.6 excludes calculations involving the Arrhenius equation. You will not be asked to extract an activation energy from two rate constants or from a plot of \(\ln k\) against \(1/T\). Reading an energy profile and reasoning qualitatively about temperature and catalysts stay fully examinable, and both appear below.

Section II • Free Response 5 questions • 32 points • 50 minutes

Free-response 1 • long • 10 points

For \(\ce{2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\), the following initial rates were measured at constant temperature.

Experiment\([\ce{NO}]_0\) (M)\([\ce{O2}]_0\) (M)Initial rate (M/s)
10.0200.010\(2.5\times10^{-5}\)
20.0400.010\(1.0\times10^{-4}\)
30.0200.020\(5.0\times10^{-5}\)
  1. Determine the order with respect to each reactant, showing your reasoning.
  2. Write the rate law and state the overall order.
  3. Calculate \(k\), with units.
  4. Explain why the order with respect to \(\ce{NO}\) cannot be assumed from its coefficient of 2 in the balanced equation.

Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A proposed mechanism for \(\ce{2N2O5 -> 4NO2 + O2}\) is \begin{align*} \text{Step 1:}\quad & \ce{N2O5 -> NO2 + NO3} \qquad \text{(slow)}\\ \text{Step 2:}\quad & \ce{NO2 + NO3 -> NO + O2 + NO2} \qquad \text{(fast)}\\ \text{Step 3:}\quad & \ce{NO + N2O5 -> 3NO2} \qquad\quad \text{(fast)} \end{align*}

  1. Identify the intermediates and state how you recognized them.
  2. Write the rate law this mechanism predicts, with justification.
  3. Describe how the energy profile for this three-step mechanism differs from that of a single-step reaction, and state which barrier is highest.
  4. A catalyst is introduced. State its effect on the rate, on \(\Delta H\), and on the position of any equilibrium.

Free-response 3 • short • 4 points

A first-order decomposition has \(k = 1.5e-3\,\mathrm{/s}\) and an initial concentration of 0.80 M.

  1. Calculate the half-life.
  2. State the concentration remaining after three half-lives, and explain why each successive half-life takes the same time.

Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

In \(\ce{2NO(g) + O2(g) -> 2NO2(g)}\), oxygen is consumed at 0.0075 M/s.

  1. Determine the rate at which \(\ce{NO}\) is consumed and \(\ce{NO2}\) formed.
  2. Explain why dividing each concentration change by its coefficient gives the same number for every species.

Free-response 5 • short • 4 points

  1. State the two conditions a collision must meet to produce reaction.
  2. A rise of 10 °C can roughly double a reaction rate, although it raises the average molecular speed by only a few percent. Explain.
