The five tools

Rate definitionrate \(= -\tfrac{1}{a}\Delta[\ce{A}]/\Delta t = +\tfrac{1}{c}\Delta[\ce{C}]/\Delta t\). Always divide by the coefficient — otherwise “the rate” depends on which species you watched
Rate lawrate \(= k[\ce{A}]^m[\ce{B}]^n\), orders from experiment. Method of initial rates: change one concentration, see what the rate does. Doubling \(\to\) \(\times1\), \(\times2\), \(\times4\) means order 0, 1, 2
Integrated lawszero: \([\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\) linear; first: \(\ln[\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\); second: \(1/[\ce{A}]\) vs \(t\). Slope is \(-k\), \(-k\), \(+k\)
Mechanism testssteps must sum to the overall equation AND predict the observed rate law. Elementary steps only: molecularity gives order
Collision modelrate depends on collision frequency, energy (\(\ge E_a\)) and orientation. \(k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}\)

Half-lives, and what they tell you

  • Zero order: \(t_{1/2} = [\ce{A}]_0/2k\) — gets shorter as the reaction proceeds.
  • First order: \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\) — constant, the only one independent of concentration. All radioactive decay.
  • Second order: \(t_{1/2} = 1/(k[\ce{A}]_0)\) — gets longer as the reaction proceeds.
  • A half-life that does not change is therefore direct evidence of first-order kinetics.

Mechanisms in five lines

  • Slow step first \(\Rightarrow\) the rate law is that step's, written straight from its molecularity.
  • Fast step first \(\Rightarrow\) write the slow step's rate law, then replace the intermediate using \(K\) of step 1.
  • Intermediate: produced then consumed. Never in a final rate law.
  • Catalyst: consumed then regenerated. Present at the end.
  • Matching the rate law makes a mechanism consistent, never proven.

The traps, one line each

  • Reading orders off the balanced equation. (\(\ce{2N2O5 -> 4NO2 + O2}\) is first order.)
  • Forgetting the coefficient when converting between species rates.
  • Saying “the graph is linear” without naming the plotted quantity.
  • Using \(t_{1/2} = 0.693/k\) for a reaction that is not first order.
  • Leaving an intermediate in a final rate law.
  • Saying a catalyst shifts the equilibrium or changes \(\Delta H\).
  • Reporting \(k\) without units — the units are a free check on your orders.

How to study for this exam

Redo WS 4's FRQ cold — the exam's long free-response is its twin in shape and length. Then drill the chain table \(\to\) orders \(\to\) rate law \(\to\) \(k\) with units until it runs without stopping, because it opens almost every kinetics FRQ.

For every “explain” item, say what the particles are doing before naming the effect: more collisions, more energetic collisions, a lower barrier, a different pathway. And whenever a graph is mentioned, name the axis.
