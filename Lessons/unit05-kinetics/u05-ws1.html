Worksheet 1 • Rates and Rate Laws

CED 5.1–5.2 • orders come from data, never from coefficients

Directions & data
\(\text{rate} = -\tfrac{1}{a}\Delta[\ce{A}]/\Delta t = +\tfrac{1}{c}\Delta[\ce{C}]/\Delta t\) •\ \(\text{rate} = k[\ce{A}]^m[\ce{B}]^n\) • orders are determined experimentally.
1. CED 5.1 For \(\ce{2N2O5(g) -> 4NO2(g) + O2(g)}\), oxygen forms at 2.0e-3 M/s.
  1. The rate of the reaction: 2.0e-3 M/s
  2. Rate of disappearance of \(\ce{N2O5}\): 4.0e-3 M/s
  3. Rate of appearance of \(\ce{NO2}\): 8.0e-3 M/s
  4. Why do the three numbers differ?
2. CED 5.1 For \(\ce{N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3}\), \(\ce{H2}\) is consumed at 0.030 M/s. Find the reaction rate and the rate of formation of \(\ce{NH3}\).
rate \(= (1/3)(0.030) = \mathbf{0.010}~\mathrm{M/s}\); \(\ce{NH3}\) forms at \(2(0.010) = \mathbf{0.020}~\mathrm{M/s}\)
3. CED 5.1 A plot of \([\ce{A}]\) against time is steep at first and flattens.
  1. Average rate is the slope of: a straight line between two points
  2. Instantaneous rate is the slope of: the tangent at that point
  3. Why does the curve flatten?
  4. Why are rate laws determined from initial rates?
4. CED 5.2 Use the data to find the rate law and \(k\).
Exp\([\ce{A}]_0\) (M)\([\ce{B}]_0\) (M)rate (M/s)
10.100.10\(2.0\times10^{-3}\)
20.200.10\(8.0\times10^{-3}\)
30.100.20\(4.0\times10^{-3}\)
  1. Order in A, with the comparison you used:
  2. Order in B: 1
  3. Overall order: 3
  4. Rate law: rate \(= k[\ce{A}]^2[\ce{B}]\)
  5. Calculate \(k\) with units.
    \(k = 2.0\times10^{-3}/[(0.10)^2(0.10)] = 2.0\times10^{-3}/1.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{2.0}~\mathrm{/M^2/s}\)
