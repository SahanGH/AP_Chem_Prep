**1.** Entropy measures dispersal. Name the two things that disperse,
and give an example of each.

**2.** Give the sign of $\Delta S^\circ$ for each change and a
one-phrase reason:

1. H₂O(s) → H₂O(l)
   positive — solid to liquid
2. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2SO₃(g)
   negative — 3 mol gas to 2
3. NH₄NO₃(s) → NH₄+(aq) + NO₃⁻(aq)
   positive — ordered lattice to free ions
4. A gas is compressed into half its volume
   negative — less space to occupy
5. CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g)
   positive — gas produced from a solid
6. A sample of argon is warmed from 300 to 400 K
   positive — energy spread over more states
7. H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g)
   

**3.** Explain why counting moles of gas is the first move, using
water as evidence. 

**4.** State the second law and explain why it refers to the universe
rather than the system.

**5.** $\Delta S_{\text{surr}} = -\Delta H/T$.

1. For an exothermic reaction, the sign of $\Delta S_{\text{surr}}$ is
   positive
2. Why does the same quantity of heat raise the surroundings' entropy
   more at low temperature than at high?
   
3. Use that to explain why water freezes below 0 °C but
   melts above it, given that $\Delta H$ and $\Delta S$ for freezing
   barely change over that range.
   

**6.** For the combustion of carbon, C(gr) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g):

1. Predict the sign of $\Delta S^\circ$ before calculating, and say
   why the prediction is hard here.
   
2. Calculate $\Delta S^\circ$. 
   *(working space)*
3. The reaction is strongly exothermic. What does that tell you about
   $\Delta S_{\text{univ}}$? 

**7.** A classmate says: “Life creates order out of disorder, so living
things break the second law.” Refute this.

**8.** Rank by molar entropy, lowest first: H₂O(s), H₂O(l),
H₂O(g). Then explain what would change if the comparison were made at
500 K instead of 298 K.
