> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> **CED 9.10 is reasoning, not calculation** — the Nernst
> equation is not required. Standard conditions are $Q = 1$; at equilibrium
> $Q = K$ and $E = 0$. $E^\circ$ (V): Ag+/Ag $+0.80$ •\
> Cu²⁺/Cu $+0.34$ • Zn²⁺/Zn $-0.76$.

**1.** State the central principle of this block in one sentence, then
give the two consequences.

**2.** Fill in:

1. Standard conditions correspond to $Q =$ 1
2. At that point, $E$ equals $E^\circ$
3. At equilibrium, $Q =$ $K$ and $E =$
   0
4. A “dead” battery is one that has reached
   equilibrium

**3.** Explain why a dead battery is not an empty one.

**4.** For the Daniell cell,
Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s):

1. Write $Q$ for this reaction.
   $[\text{Zn²⁺}]/[\text{Cu²⁺}]$
2. Why do Zn(s) and Cu(s) not appear?
   they are pure solids
3. Increasing $[\text{Cu²⁺}]$ changes $Q$ how, and $E$ how?
   
4. Increasing $[\text{Zn²⁺}]$ changes $Q$ how, and $E$ how?
   
5. Adding more solid zinc to the anode changes $E$ how?
   not at all — it is a pure solid

**5.** For each change to a running Daniell cell, state whether $E$
rises, falls, or is unchanged:

1. Water is added to the cathode compartment, diluting Cu²⁺
   falls
2. Na₂S is added to the cathode compartment, precipitating
   CuS falls
3. Solid CuSO₄ is dissolved in the cathode compartment
   rises
4. The zinc electrode is replaced with a larger zinc electrode
   unchanged
5. The cell is allowed to run for several hours
   falls

**6.** Explain your answer to 5(b) fully — why does precipitating the
cathode ion have a larger effect than simple dilution?

**7.** A student justifies an answer by writing “by Le Ch\^atelier's
principle, removing a product shifts the equilibrium forward, so the voltage
rises.” Explain why this reasoning is rejected here and give the correct
version.

**8.** Concentration cells. Consider
$\text{Cu} \mid \text{Cu²⁺}~(0.010\,\mathrm{M}) \parallel \text{Cu²⁺}~(1.0\,\mathrm{M}) \mid \text{Cu}$.

1. Calculate $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$. $0.00$ V
2. Explain why the cell nevertheless produces a voltage.
   
3. Which half-cell is the anode? Justify by asking what must happen to
   reach equilibrium. 
4. In which direction do electrons flow?
   from the dilute to the concentrated half-cell
5. What happens to $E$ as the cell runs, and what is its final value?
   

**9.** A concentration cell is built from silver electrodes in
0.10 M and 2.0 M AgNO₃.

1. Identify the cathode. the 2.0 M side
2. Predict what happens to the mass of each electrode.
   
3. Would a cell built from 1.0 M and 1.0 M
   AgNO₃ produce any voltage? Explain.
