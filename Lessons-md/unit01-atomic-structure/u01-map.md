## By the end of this unit you can…

- Convert among grams, moles, and particles, and justify each step
   with dimensional analysis. `CED 1.1`
- Read a mass spectrum: estimate average atomic mass from peak
   positions and heights, and identify the element. `CED 1.2`
- Determine empirical and molecular formulas from percent composition
   or combustion-style data. `CED 1.3`
- Distinguish pure substances from mixtures using particulate
   diagrams, and compute composition of mixtures. `CED 1.4`
- Write ground-state electron configurations for atoms and ions, and
   spot excited states. `CED 1.5`
- Interpret a photoelectron spectrum: peak position $\to$ binding
   energy, peak area $\to$ electron count; identify the element. `CED 1.6`
- Explain radius, ionization energy, and electronegativity trends
   using Coulomb's law — not just recite them. `CED 1.7`
- Predict ionic charges from electron configurations and write
   correct ionic formulas. `CED 1.8`

> ⚠️ **Where students lose points in this unit**
>
> Averaging isotope masses without weighting; confusing peak *height*
> (abundance) with peak *position* (mass) on spectra; writing configurations
> for ions as if they were atoms; ranking trends correctly but “explaining”
> them by restating the trend instead of using nuclear charge, distance, and
> shielding.
