> 📌 **How this unit is graded — and what makes it different**
>
> Unit exam **Fri Nov 20**: 25 multiple choice (**no calculator**)
> $+$ 1 short and 1 long free-response, 69 minutes, 39 points.
> 
> Every other unit so far has asked *what* happens. This one asks
> *how fast*, and the answer can never be read off a balanced equation.
> **Orders come from experiment, always** — the single exception being
> an elementary step, where molecularity does give the order. Knowing which
> case you are in is most of the unit.

## By the end of this unit you can…

- Express a reaction rate in terms of any reactant or product, using
   the coefficients correctly. `CED 5.1`
- Determine a rate law and rate constant from initial-rate data.
   `CED 5.2`
- Identify reaction order from which graph is linear, and use the
   integrated rate law and half-life. `CED 5.3`
- Write the rate law for an elementary step from its molecularity.
   `CED 5.4`
- Explain rate in terms of collision frequency, energy, and
   orientation. `CED 5.5`
- Read and draw a reaction energy profile, identifying $E_a$ and
   $\Delta H$. `CED 5.6`
- Evaluate whether a proposed mechanism is consistent with the
   overall equation and the observed rate law. `CED 5.7`, `CED 5.8`
- Derive a rate law when the first step is a fast equilibrium.
   `CED 5.9`
- Interpret a multistep energy profile and identify the
   rate-determining step. `CED 5.10`
- Explain how a catalyst changes the rate without changing $\Delta H$
   or $K$. `CED 5.11`

> ⚠️ **Where students lose points in this unit**
>
> Reading orders off the balanced equation; forgetting the coefficient when
> converting between reactant and product rates; calling a graph “linear”
> without saying *which* quantity was plotted; using $t_{1/2} = 0.693/k$
> for a reaction that is not first order; leaving an intermediate in a final
> rate law; and saying a catalyst “shifts the equilibrium” or “makes the
> reaction more favorable.”
