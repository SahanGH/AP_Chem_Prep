**1.** `CED 5.9` Overall 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂, experimental
rate $= k[\text{NO}]^2[\text{O₂}]$. Mechanism: step 1 (fast equilibrium)
2NO ⇌ N₂O₂; step 2 (slow) N₂O₂ + O₂ → 2NO₂.

1. Write the rate law for the slow step as it stands.
   rate $= k_2[\text{N₂O₂}][\text{O₂}]$
2. Why is that not yet an acceptable rate law?
   
3. Use step 1's equilibrium to express $[\text{N₂O₂}]$.
   $[\text{N₂O₂}] = K_1[\text{NO}]^2$
4. Substitute and give the predicted rate law.
   *(working space)*
5. What does it mean that the observed $k$ equals $k_2K_1$?

**6.** **FRQ (10 points).** `CED 5.2` `CED 5.3` `CED 5.8`
The gas-phase reaction 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g) was studied at
constant temperature.

| **Exp** | $[\text{NO}]_0$ (M) | $[\text{O₂}]_0$ (M) | **initial rate** (M/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.010 | 0.010 | $2.5\times10^{-5}$ |
| 2 | 0.020 | 0.010 | $1.0\times10^{-4}$ |
| 3 | 0.010 | 0.020 | $5.0\times10^{-5}$ |

1. Determine the order in NO and in O₂, showing the
   comparison used for each. 
2. Write the rate law and calculate $k$ with units.
   *(working space)*
3. Predict the initial rate when $[\text{NO}] = 0.030$ and
   $[\text{O₂}] = 0.015$.
   *(working space)*
4. A student proposes that the reaction occurs in a single
   termolecular step. State whether this is consistent with the rate
   law, and whether it is likely. 
5. Show that the mechanism 2NO ⇌ N₂O₂ (fast equilibrium)
   followed by N₂O₂ + O₂ → 2NO₂ (slow) predicts the same rate
   law.
   *(working space)*
