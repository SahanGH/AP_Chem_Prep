> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> $\text{rate} = -\tfrac{1}{a}\Delta[\text{A}]/\Delta t = +\tfrac{1}{c}\Delta[\text{C}]/\Delta t$ •\
> $\text{rate} = k[\text{A}]^m[\text{B}]^n$ • orders are determined
> experimentally.

**1.** `CED 5.1` For 2N₂O₅(g) → 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g), oxygen forms at
2.0e-3 M/s.

1. The rate of the reaction: 2.0e-3 M/s
2. Rate of disappearance of N₂O₅:
   4.0e-3 M/s
3. Rate of appearance of NO₂:
   8.0e-3 M/s
4. Why do the three numbers differ? 

**2.** `CED 5.1` For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, H₂ is consumed at
0.030 M/s. Find the reaction rate and the rate of
formation of NH₃. 

*(working space)*

**3.** `CED 5.1` A plot of $[\text{A}]$ against time is steep at first and
flattens.

1. Average rate is the slope of: a straight line
   between two points
2. Instantaneous rate is the slope of:
   the tangent at that point
3. Why does the curve flatten? 
4. Why are rate laws determined from *initial* rates?
   

**4.** `CED 5.2` Use the data to find the rate law and $k$.

| **Exp** | $[\text{A}]_0$ (M) | $[\text{B}]_0$ (M) | **rate** (M/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.10 | 0.10 | $2.0\times10^{-3}$ |
| 2 | 0.20 | 0.10 | $8.0\times10^{-3}$ |
| 3 | 0.10 | 0.20 | $4.0\times10^{-3}$ |

1. Order in A, with the comparison you used: 
2. Order in B: 1
3. Overall order: 3
4. Rate law: rate $= k[\text{A}]^2[\text{B}]$
5. Calculate $k$ with units. 
   *(working space)*
