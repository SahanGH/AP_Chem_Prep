## The five tools

| **Rate definition** | rate $= -\tfrac{1}{a}\Delta[\text{A}]/\Delta t =   +\tfrac{1}{c}\Delta[\text{C}]/\Delta t$. *Always divide by the
  coefficient* — otherwise “the rate” depends on which species you
  watched |
|---|---|
| **Rate law** | rate $= k[\text{A}]^m[\text{B}]^n$, orders from
  *experiment*. Method of initial rates: change one concentration,
  see what the rate does. Doubling $\to$ $\times1$, $\times2$, $\times4$
  means order 0, 1, 2 |
| **Integrated laws** | zero: $[\text{A}]$ vs $t$ linear; first:
  $\ln[\text{A}]$ vs $t$; second: $1/[\text{A}]$ vs $t$. Slope is $-k$, $-k$,
  $+k$ |
| **Mechanism tests** | steps must sum to the overall equation AND
  predict the observed rate law. Elementary steps only: molecularity gives
  order |
| **Collision model** | rate depends on collision frequency, energy
  ($\ge E_a$) and orientation. $k = Ae^{-E_a/RT}$ |

## Half-lives, and what they tell you

- **Zero order:** $t_{1/2} = [\text{A}]_0/2k$ — gets
   *shorter* as the reaction proceeds.
- **First order:** $t_{1/2} = 0.693/k$ — *constant*, the
   only one independent of concentration. All radioactive decay.
- **Second order:** $t_{1/2} = 1/(k[\text{A}]_0)$ — gets
   *longer* as the reaction proceeds.
- A half-life that does not change is therefore direct evidence of
   first-order kinetics.

## Mechanisms in five lines

- **Slow step first** $\Rightarrow$ the rate law is that step's,
   written straight from its molecularity.
- **Fast step first** $\Rightarrow$ write the slow step's rate
   law, then replace the intermediate using $K$ of step 1.
- **Intermediate:** produced then consumed. Never in a final
   rate law.
- **Catalyst:** consumed then regenerated. Present at the end.
- Matching the rate law makes a mechanism *consistent*, never
   *proven*.

## The traps, one line each

- Reading orders off the balanced equation.
   (2N₂O₅ → 4NO₂ + O₂ is *first* order.)
- Forgetting the coefficient when converting between species rates.
- Saying “the graph is linear” without naming the plotted quantity.
- Using $t_{1/2} = 0.693/k$ for a reaction that is not first order.
- Leaving an intermediate in a final rate law.
- Saying a catalyst shifts the equilibrium or changes $\Delta H$.
- Reporting $k$ without units — the units are a free check on your
   orders.
