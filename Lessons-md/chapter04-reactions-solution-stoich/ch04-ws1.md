> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Show the setup for every calculation. For classification
> questions, state what happens to the solute in water — not just the
> label.

**1.** Classify each as a strong electrolyte, weak electrolyte, or
nonelectrolyte, and state what species are present in solution:

1. KBr: strong; K+ and Br-
2. HC₂H₃O₂: weak; mostly intact molecules
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose): nonelectrolyte; molecules
4. HNO₃: strong; H+ and NO₃-
5. NH₃: weak; mostly NH₃ molecules

**2.** Two beakers each hold 0.10 M solutions. Beaker A
(HCl) lights a conductivity bulb brightly; beaker B (HF) lights it
dimly.

1. Explain the difference. 
2. A student concludes “beaker A is more concentrated.” Evaluate.
   

**3.** Explain at the particle level why water dissolves NaCl but
CCl₄ does not. 

**4.** Molarity calculations:

1. Molarity of 29.2 g NaCl in 500. mL:
   1.00 M
2. Mass of KMnO₄ ($M = 158.03$) in 250. mL of
   0.0200 M: 0.790 g
3. Moles in 40.0 mL of 0.125 M:
   5.00e-3 mol
4. Volume of 0.250 M containing 0.0500 mol:
   200. mL

**5.** Dilutions:

1. 15.0 mL of 18.0 M H₂SO₄ diluted to
   500. mL: 0.540 M
2. Volume of 12.0 M HCl needed for
   100. mL of 0.500 M:
   4.17 mL
3. State the safety rule for diluting concentrated acid, and why.
   

**6.** Ion concentrations in strong electrolyte solutions:

1. $[\text{K+}]$ and $[\text{SO₄²⁻}]$ in 0.30 M K₂SO₄:
   0.60 M; 0.30 M
2. $[\text{NO₃-}]$ in 0.15 M Fe(NO₃)₃:
   0.45 M
3. Total ion concentration in 0.20 M Na₃PO₄:
   0.80 M

**7.** Describe, step by step, how to prepare 250.0 mL
of 0.100 M K₂Cr₂O₇ ($M = 294.20$) from the solid.
