> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Every net ionic equation here depends on whether the acid is
> strong or weak — state that classification first, then write the
> equation.

**1.** Classify each as strong acid, weak acid, strong base, or weak
base:

1. HClO₄: strong acid
2. HC₂H₃O₂: weak acid
3. Ba(OH)₂: strong base
4. NH₃: weak base
5. HBr: strong acid
6. HF: weak acid

**2.** Write the net ionic equation for each neutralization:

1. HNO₃(aq) + KOH(aq)
   H+ + OH⁻ → H₂O(l)
2. HF(aq) + NaOH(aq)
   HF + OH⁻ → F⁻ + H₂O
3. HCl(aq) + NH₃(aq)
   H+ + NH₃ → NH₄+

**3.** Explain why (a) and (b) above have different net ionic equations
even though both are “acid plus strong base.”

**4.** Give the Bronsted–Lowry definitions and explain why they are
more general than the Arrhenius definitions. Use NH₃ as your example.

**5.** Titration calculations.

1. 25.0 mL of HCl requires
   31.5 mL of 0.200 M NaOH. Find
   $[\text{HCl}]$.
   *(working space)*
2. 20.0 mL of 0.150 M H₂SO₄ is
   titrated with 0.100 M NaOH. What volume of base is
   required?
   *(working space)*
3. 0.500 g of an unknown monoprotic acid requires
   22.8 mL of 0.250 M NaOH. Find the
   acid's molar mass.
   *(working space)*

**6.** Define the equivalence point and explain how an indicator signals
it. 

**7.** A student titrating H₂SO₄ with NaOH uses a 1:1 mole
ratio and reports a concentration exactly half the true value. Explain the
error.
