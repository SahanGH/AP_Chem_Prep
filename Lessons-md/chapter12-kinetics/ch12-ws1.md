**1.** For 2 N₂O₅ → 4 NO₂ + O₂, write the rate of reaction in
terms of each species.

**2.** For 4 NH₃ + 5 O₂ → 4 NO + 6 H₂O, O₂ disappears at
0.050 M/s.

1. Rate of NH₃ disappearance:
   0.040 M/s
2. Rate of H₂O formation:
   0.060 M/s
3. Rate of the reaction:
   0.010 M/s

**3.** Explain the difference between average rate, instantaneous rate,
and initial rate, and state why initial rates are used to determine rate
laws. 

**4.** For rate $= k[\text{A}][\text{B}]^2$, state the effect on the rate
of each change:

1. Doubling [A]: doubles
2. Doubling [B]: quadruples
3. Doubling both: $\times 8$
4. Halving [B]: quarters
5. Overall order: 3

**6.** Determine the rate law from these data for A + 2B → C:

| Trial | $[\text{A}]$ (M) | $[\text{B}]$ (M) | rate (M/s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.20 | 0.20 | $6.0\times10^{-3}$ |
| 2 | 0.40 | 0.20 | $1.2\times10^{-2}$ |
| 3 | 0.40 | 0.40 | $1.2\times10^{-2}$ |

**7.** A student writes the rate law for 2 N₂O₅ → 4 NO₂ + O₂ as
rate $= k[\text{N₂O₅}]^2$, reasoning from the coefficient. Zumdahl's data show
that halving $[\text{N₂O₅}]$ halves the rate. Identify the error and give the
correct rate law.
