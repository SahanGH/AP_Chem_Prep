> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> $q = mc\Delta T$ • $q = n\Delta H$ for a phase change
> • $c$: water 4.18, ice 2.09, steam 2.03
> J/g/°C •\
> $\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 6.02\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$,
> $\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 40.7\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$ •\
> $M(\text{H₂O}) = 18.02\,\mathrm{g/mol}$.

**1.** `CED 6.4` Straight substitution.

1. Energy to raise 50.0 g of water from 20.0 °C
   to 80.0 °C:
   *(working space)*
2. Energy released when 250.0 g of water cools by
   15.0 °C: -15.7 kJ
3. Why is water's specific heat unusually large, and name one
   consequence. 

**2.** `CED 6.4` A reaction is run in 100.0 g of water in a
coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature rises 5.20 °C, and
0.0250 mol of the limiting reactant was consumed.

1. Calculate $q_{\text{water}}$. 
   *(working space)*
2. State $q_{\text{rxn}}$ and explain the sign.
   
3. Calculate $\Delta H$ in kJ/mol.
   *(working space)*
4. Why is the answer reported per mole rather than in kilojoules
   alone? 

**3.** `CED 6.4` A 50.0 g metal block at 100.0 °C
is dropped into 100.0 g of water at 22.0 °C. The
final temperature is 24.6 °C.

1. Calculate the heat gained by the water. 
   *(working space)*
2. Calculate the specific heat of the metal. 
   *(working space)*
3. The metal's temperature changed by more than 75 °C
   while the water's changed by less than 3. Give two reasons.
   

**4.** `CED 6.5` Phase changes.

1. Energy to melt 36.0 g of ice at 0 °C:
   *(working space)*
2. Energy to vaporize 18.0 g of water at
   100 °C: 40.7 kJ
3. Energy to melt 90.0 g of ice: 
   *(working space)*
4. Vaporizing a mole costs about seven times what melting a mole
   costs. Explain in terms of intermolecular forces.
   

**5.** `CED 6.5` Why can $q = mc\Delta T$ not be used during melting?

**6.** `CED 6.5` Take 25.0 g of ice at -10.0 °C
all the way to steam at 110.0 °C.

1. Which equation applies to the sloped regions of the heating curve?
   $q = mc\Delta T$
2. Which applies to the flat regions?
   $q = n\Delta H$
3. Calculate the total energy required. 
   *(working space)*
4. Which single step dominates, and what fraction of the total is it?
   boiling, about 74%

**7.** `CED 6.5` Explain why a steam burn at 100 °C is
worse than a burn from liquid water at the same temperature.
