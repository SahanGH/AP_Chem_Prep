> 📌 **How this unit is graded — and what makes it different**
>
> Unit exam **Fri Dec 11**: 20 multiple choice (**no calculator**)
> $+$ 2 short free-response, 48 minutes, 28 points.
> 
> This is the shortest unit in the course and the one with the cleanest
> one-to-one match to the textbook — Zumdahl Chapter 6 is essentially the
> whole unit, with two topics borrowed from elsewhere.
> 
> What makes it different is that almost every question is a
> **sign question**. Getting $\Delta H$ right is usually easy; getting
> the sign right, and saying which way heat flowed and from whose point of
> view, is where the points actually are.

## By the end of this unit you can…

- Classify a process as endothermic or exothermic from the sign of
   $q$ or from an observation. `CED 6.1`
- Draw and interpret an energy diagram for a reaction. `CED 6.2`
- Explain heat transfer and thermal equilibrium at the particle
   level. `CED 6.3`
- Use $q = mc\Delta T$ and calorimetry data to find $\Delta H$ per
   mole. `CED 6.4`
- Calculate the energy of a phase change and read a heating curve.
   `CED 6.5`
- Relate $\Delta H$ to the amount of substance reacting. `CED 6.6`
- Estimate $\Delta H$ from average bond enthalpies. `CED 6.7`
- Calculate $\Delta H^\circ$ from enthalpies of formation. `CED 6.8`
- Apply Hess's law to combine reactions. `CED 6.9`

> ⚠️ **Where students lose points in this unit**
>
> Losing the sign of $q$ when switching between system and surroundings;
> using $q = mc\Delta T$ *during* a phase change, where the temperature
> does not change; forgetting to divide by moles when reporting
> kJ/mol; reversing a reaction in Hess's law without
> flipping the sign; using *formed minus broken* for bond enthalpies
> (it is broken minus formed); and treating an element's
> $\Delta H_f^\circ$ as anything other than zero.
