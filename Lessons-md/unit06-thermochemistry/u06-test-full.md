---

> 📌 **How this paper runs**
>
> **Two sessions.** Session 1 works through every worked example in
> Zumdahl Chapter 6 and §8.8, in the book's own order — the techniques
> build on each other. Session 2 is the chapter's own Challenge Problem set
> in full.
> 
> Calculator, periodic table, and equations sheet are permitted throughout.
> **Show every step.** A bare final answer earns the answer mark only;
> the setup marks are for work you can point to. Carry units through each
> line and round only at the end.

> 📌 **Scope — read before you start**
>
> Two CED exclusions shape this paper. Under `CED 6.6`: **the
> technical distinction between enthalpy and internal energy is not
> assessed**. Most AP reactions run at constant pressure, where
> $q_p = \Delta H$ — that equivalence is all the CED asks for. Under
> `CED 6.9`: **the formal concept of a state function is not
> assessed** — Hess's law itself is fully examinable, the thermodynamic
> formalism behind *why* it works is not.
> 
> Chapter 6 itself is a general-chemistry chapter, and several of its early
> worked examples are built entirely around $\Delta E = q + w$ and $w = -P\Delta V$ — squarely the excluded material. Those parts are flagged
> **[beyond CED]** throughout. They are here because the calculation is
> short, standard, and worth knowing — not because the AP exam will ask
> for it.

*(working space)*

A weather balloon expands from 4.00e6 L to
4.50e6 L while 1.3e8 J of heat is added, against a
constant external pressure of 1.0 atm.
($1\,\mathrm{L\cdot atm} = 101.3\,\mathrm{J}$.)

Find $\Delta E$ for the gas inside the balloon.

*(working space)*

### Free-response 4 • short • 4 points

890 kJ.

Find $\Delta H$ for burning 5.8 g of CH₄.

*(working space)*

### Free-response 5 • long • 6 points

1.00 L of 1.00 M Ba(NO₃)₂ and 1.00 L

in a coffee-cup calorimeter. The temperature rises to
28.1 °C. Take the specific heat of the mixed solution as
4.18 J/g/°C and its density as
1.0 g/mL.

1. Write the net ionic equation. 
   *(working space)*
2. Find $\Delta H$ per mole of BaSO₄ formed.
   *(working space)*

### Free-response 6 • long • 6 points

Which fuel releases more energy *per gram*, and by what factor?

*(working space)*

### Free-response 7 • short • 5 points

## Section B • Worked Examples — Hess's Law and Formation
  Enthalpies 7 questions • 48 points

### Free-response 8 • long • 7 points

Diborane, B₂H₆(g), was once studied as a rocket fuel. Find
$\Delta H$ for 2B(s) + 3H₂(g) → B₂H₆(g) using:

(a) 2B(s) + 3/2O₂(g) → B₂O₃(s)    $\Delta H = -1273$ kJ  

(b) B₂H₆(g) + 3O₂(g) → B₂O₃(s) + 3H₂O(g)    $\Delta H = -2035$ kJ  

(c) H₂(g) + 1/2O₂(g) → H₂O(l)    $\Delta H = -286$ kJ  

(d) H₂O(l) → H₂O(g)    $\Delta H = +44$ kJ

*(working space)*

### Free-response 9 • long • 6 points

4NH₃(g) + 7O₂(g) → 4NO₂(g) + 6H₂O(l). Given
$\Delta H_f^\circ$: NH₃(g) $=-46$; NO₂(g) $=+34$;
H₂O(l) $=-286$ (all kJ/mol; O₂ is an element).

Find $\Delta H^\circ$ for the reaction.

*(working space)*

### Free-response 10 • short • 4 points

2Al(s) + Fe₂O₃(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(s). Given
$\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{Fe₂O₃}) = -826$, $\Delta H_f^\circ(\text{Al₂O₃}) = -1676$ kJ/mol.

Find $\Delta H^\circ$ for the reaction.

*(working space)*

### Free-response 11 • long • 8 points

Given $\Delta H_f^\circ$ (kJ/mol): CO₂(g) $=-394$; H₂O(l)
$=-286$; CH₃OH(l) $=-239$; C₈H₁₈(l) $=-269$.

1. Find the enthalpy of combustion of methanol *per gram*.
   2CH₃OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l).
   *(working space)*
2. Find the enthalpy of combustion of octane *per gram*.
   2C₈H₁₈(l) + 25O₂(g) → 16CO₂(g) + 18H₂O(l).
   *(working space)*
3. Octane releases about twice the energy per gram that methanol
   does. Methanol was nonetheless once preferred in race-car
   engines. Suggest why.
   *(working space)*

### Free-response 15 • long • 8 points

2.00 mol of an ideal gas moves from state A
($P=2.00\,\mathrm{atm}$, $V=10.0\,\mathrm{L}$) to state B
($P=1.00\,\mathrm{atm}$, $V=30.0\,\mathrm{L}$) by two different routes.

**Pathway 1** (via point C, $P=2.00\,\mathrm{atm}$,
$V=30.0\,\mathrm{L}$): A$\to$C at constant pressure, then C$\to$B at
constant volume.

**Pathway 2** (via point D, $P=1.00\,\mathrm{atm}$,
$V=10.0\,\mathrm{L}$): A$\to$D at constant volume, then D$\to$B at
constant pressure.

1. Find the total work for each pathway, in joules.
   *(working space)*
2. Is work a state function? Explain using your answer above.
   *(working space)*
