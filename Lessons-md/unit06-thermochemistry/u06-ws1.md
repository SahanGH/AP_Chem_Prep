> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Signs are written from the **system's** point of view.
> Exothermic: heat leaves the system, $q  0$, surroundings cool.

**1.** `CED 6.1` Classify each and give the sign of
$q_{\text{system}}$:

1. Combustion of propane
   exothermic, $q  0$
3. Water freezing
   exothermic, $q  0$
5. Photosynthesis
   endothermic, $q > 0$

**2.** `CED 6.1` A reaction is run in solution and the thermometer
reading rises from 22.0 °C to 28.4 °C.

1. Sign of $q_{\text{water}}$: positive
2. Sign of $\Delta H_{\text{rxn}}$: negative
3. Explain why the two signs are opposite even though only one event
   occurred. 
4. A student writes “the temperature went up, so the reaction is
   endothermic.” Correct them in one sentence.
   

**3.** `CED 6.2` Energy diagrams.

1. Products drawn below reactants means the reaction is:
   exothermic
2. $\Delta H$ on a diagram is measured between:
   reactants and products
3. Does the height of the barrier between them affect $\Delta H$?
   
4. Sketch, in words, the diagram for an endothermic reaction with a
   large activation barrier. 

**4.** `CED 6.3` A hot metal block is dropped into cool water.

1. In which direction does energy flow?
   hot to cold
2. Explain the transfer at the particle level.
   
3. When does the transfer stop, and what is happening then?
   
4. Why is “the block has a lot of heat” a poor way to put it?
   

**5.** `CED 6.1` An ammonium nitrate cold pack drops from
21.0 °C to 4.0 °C.

1. Sign of $\Delta H$ for the dissolving:
   positive
2. Where did the energy come from?
   
3. Does an endothermic process being favourable contradict anything
   you know? Answer briefly. 

**6.** `CED 6.3` Two identical blocks, one at 80 °C and one
at 20 °C, are placed in contact and insulated from
everything else. Predict the final temperature and justify.
