> 📌 **Scope**
>
> Covers `CED 6.1` through `CED 6.9`, worth
> 7 %–9 % of the exam. Note that `CED 6.5`
> (energy of phase changes) is sourced from Zumdahl §10.8 and `CED 6.7`
> (bond enthalpies) from §8.8 — both sit outside the thermochemistry
> chapter, and both are examined here.
> 
> Topics `CED 6.6` and `CED 6.9` carry an exclusion covering the
> **enthalpy-versus-internal-energy distinction** and formal treatment
> of **state functions**. Hess's law itself is fully examinable; the
> thermodynamic formalism behind it is not.
> 
> Take $c(\text{H₂O}) = 4.18\,\mathrm{J/g/{}^\circ C}$,
> $\Delta H_{\text{fus}} = 334\,\mathrm{J/g}$ and
> $\Delta H_{\text{vap}} = 2260\,\mathrm{J/g}$.

### Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

Consider these data at 298 K:

| **Reaction** | $\Delta H^\circ$ (kJ) |
|---|---|
| N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g) | $+180.5$ |
| 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g) | $-114.1$ |

1. Use Hess's law to find $\Delta H^\circ$ for
   N₂(g) + 2O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g).
   *(working space)*
2. Hence determine $\Delta H_f^\circ$ for NO₂(g), and explain
   the relationship.
   \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
3. State $\Delta H^\circ$ for 2NO₂(g) → 2NO(g) + O₂(g) and
   justify.
   \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
4. Using average bond enthalpies N#N 941, O=O 495 and
   N=O 607 kJ/mol, estimate $\Delta H$ for
   N₂ + O₂ → 2NO and compare with the tabulated $+180.5$ kJ.
   *(working space)*
