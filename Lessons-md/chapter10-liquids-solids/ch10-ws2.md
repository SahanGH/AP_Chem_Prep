> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> For every property question, name the *particles* occupying
> the lattice and the *force* between them before naming the property.

**1.** Classify each solid and predict whether it conducts as a solid:

1. W, mp 3422 °C, malleable, lustrous:
   metallic; yes
2. SiC, mp 2730 °C, extremely hard:
   network covalent; no
3. CaF₂, mp 1418 °C, brittle:
   ionic; no
4. S₈, mp 115 °C, soft, brittle:
   molecular; no

**2.** Explain, at the particle level, why metals are malleable but
ionic solids are brittle. 

**3.** Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon.

1. Hybridization of carbon in each:
   diamond sp$^3$; graphite sp$^2$
2. Which conducts electricity, and why?
   
3. Why is graphite soft enough to use as a lubricant while diamond is
   the hardest known material? 

**4.** Alloys:

1. Brass substitutes zinc for copper; steel inserts carbon into iron.
   Name each type. brass substitutional; steel
   interstitial
2. Why is steel harder than pure iron?
   
3. Does alloying destroy electrical conductivity? Explain.
   

**5.** Molecular and ionic solids:

1. Melting ice breaks which forces? Support with numbers.
   
2. Explain why ice is less dense than liquid water.
   
3. Why does NaCl conduct when molten but not as a solid?
   

**6.** Rank by increasing melting point and justify each step:
Ar, HCl, KCl, diamond.
