> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Name the force AND the structural feature producing it. “Stronger
> IMFs” restates the question — say which force and why it is present.

**1.** List every intermolecular force present:

1. CH₃CH₃: dispersion only
2. CH₃Cl: dispersion $+$ dipole–dipole
3. CH₃OH: dispersion, dipole–dipole, H-bonding
4. HF: dispersion, dipole–dipole, H-bonding

**2.** C₂H₅OH boils at 78 °C while CH₃OCH₃ boils
at -24 °C. Both have the formula C₂H₆O. Explain the
100 °C gap. 

**3.** Zumdahl reports BI₃ (dispersion only) boiling at
209.5 °C and NH₃ (hydrogen bonding) at
-33.0 °C.

1. This appears to contradict “hydrogen bonding is the strongest
   IMF.” Resolve it. 
2. State the working rule this illustrates.
   

**4.** Rank each set by increasing boiling point with a one-line reason:

1. Ne, Ar, Kr
   
2. CH₄, CH₃F, CH₃OH
   

**5.** Surface tension, viscosity, capillary action:

1. Explain surface tension at the particle level.
   
2. Water forms a concave meniscus in glass; mercury forms a convex
   one. State which force dominates in each case.
   
3. Give two reasons glycerol (C₃H₈O₃) is more viscous than
   ethanol. 

**6.** Which liquid in each pair has the higher vapour pressure at
25 °C? Justify.

1. Water or acetone (bp 56 °C):
   acetone — weaker IMFs
2. Hexane or octane:
   hexane — fewer electrons, weaker dispersion
