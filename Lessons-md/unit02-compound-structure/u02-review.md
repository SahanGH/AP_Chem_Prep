## The five tools

| **Bond classifier** | $\Delta$EN small + nonmetals $\to$ covalent
  (polar if unequal); metal + nonmetal $\to$ ionic; metal + metal $\to$
  metallic. It's a continuum — argue with $\Delta$EN |
|---|---|
| **PE curve** | minimum's position $=$ bond length; depth $=$ bond
  energy; short-range wall $=$ nuclear repulsion. Breaking bonds always
  costs energy |
| **Lattice ranking** | $\|E\| \propto \|q_1q_2\|/r$: charges first, radii
  as tiebreak |
| **Lewis recipe** | count (charge-adjusted!) $\to$ skeleton (least EN
  central, never H) $\to$ outer octets $\to$ leftovers on center $\to$
  multiple bonds if short. Check count AND octets |
| **Shape chain** | Lewis $\to$ domains (multiple bond $=$ 1) $\to$
  geometry $\to$ angle (lone pairs compress) $\to$ hybridization ($2/3/4 =$
  sp/sp$^2$/sp$^3$) $\to$ polarity (dipoles + cancellation) |

## Formal charge and resonance in four lines

- $FC =$ valence $-$ nonbonding $- \frac12$(bonding). Sum must equal
   the overall charge — always check.
- Best structure: FCs near zero, negative FC on the most
   electronegative atom.
- Resonance = one static *average*, never flipping. Evidence:
   equal bond lengths.
- Bond order $=$ bonding pairs $\div$ positions: NO₃-/CO₃²⁻
   $= 4/3$; NO₂-/O₃ $= 3/2$.

## The traps, one line each

- Forgetting to add/subtract charge electrons in the count — kills
   the whole structure.
- “Polar bonds, so polar molecule” — symmetry cancels
   (CO₂, BF₃, CCl₄).
- Quoting 109.5$^\circ$ for NH₃/H₂O — lone pairs compress
   (107$^\circ$/104.5$^\circ$).
- Ranking lattices by size when charges differ — charges dominate.
- Explaining brittleness or conductivity without naming the mobile (or
   immobile) charged particles.
- Hybridization beyond sp$^3$ — not assessed; don't burn time on it.
