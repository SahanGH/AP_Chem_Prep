> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Every ranking needs its reason — “charges first, then radii”
> is a full sentence here. One-word answers earn half credit at best.

**1.** Classify the bonding in each (nonpolar covalent / polar
covalent / ionic / metallic) with a one-phrase reason:

1. N₂: nonpolar covalent — identical atoms
2. SrCl₂: ionic — metal + nonmetal
3. ICl: polar covalent — nonmetals, small $\Delta$EN
4. bronze (Cu/Sn): metallic — delocalized e$^-$ sea

**2.** Rank by increasing bond polarity, then state the trend that
decides it: O-F, O-H, O-O.

**3.** A potential-energy curve for Cl₂ has its minimum at
199 pm and $-243$ kJ/mol.

1. Bond length: 199 pm   
   Bond energy: 243 kJ/mol
2. At distances shorter than 199 pm, the curve rises steeply. What
   interaction dominates there?
   nucleus–nucleus repulsion
3. Br₂'s well: 228 pm, $-193$ kJ/mol. On shared axes, sketch both
   curves, labeled.
   *(working space)*
4. Which bond is easier to break, and what number says so?
   Br₂ — smaller well depth (193 kJ/mol)

**4.** Rank by lattice energy magnitude, largest first:
KBr, CaO, KCl, SrO.

**5.** Solid MgO melts at 2852$^\circ$C; solid NaF at
993$^\circ$C. Both lattices have similar ion sizes. Explain the gap with
Coulomb's law. 

**6.** A jeweler's gold alloy substitutes Cu atoms (similar radius)
into the Au lattice; steel inserts small C atoms into holes in the Fe
lattice.

1. Name each alloy type: Au/Cu substitutional; steel
   interstitial
2. Both alloys are harder than the pure metal. Give the particle-level
   reason. 
3. Why does the alloy still conduct electricity?
