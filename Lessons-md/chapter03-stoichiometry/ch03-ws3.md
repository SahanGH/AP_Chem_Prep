> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> State which reactant is limiting *and* how you decided,
> every time. A correct number with no identification of the limiting
> reactant is an incomplete answer.

**1.** A sandwich needs 2 slices of bread, 1 slice of cheese, and 3
slices of meat. You have 10 bread, 7 cheese, 12 meat.

1. How many sandwiches? 4
2. Limiting ingredient: meat
3. What is left over? 2 bread, 3 cheese
4. You started with more meat than cheese. Explain how meat can still
   be limiting. 

**2.** Complete the BCA table for 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O starting with
7 mol H₂ and 2 mol O₂.

|  | 2 H₂ | O₂ | 2 H₂O |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Before** | 7 | 2 | 0 |
| **Change** | $-4$ | $-2$ | $+4$ |
| **After** | 3 | 0 | 4 |

Limiting reactant: O₂ — identified because its
After value is zero.

**3.** 50.0 g of N₂ reacts with 10.0 g of
H₂: N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃.

1. Moles of each reactant: N₂ 1.78 mol;
   H₂ 4.96 mol
2. Identify the limiting reactant, showing the comparison.
   *(working space)*
3. Theoretical yield of NH₃ in grams: 
   *(working space)*
4. Mass of excess reactant left over: 
   *(working space)*
5. If 48.0 g of NH₃ is isolated, find the percent yield.
   85.3%

**4.** 10.0 g of CaCO₃ is treated with 10.0 g
of HCl: CaCO₃ + 2 HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂.

1. Limiting reactant (show the comparison): 
   *(working space)*
2. Theoretical mass of CO₂: 4.40 g
3. Moles of HCl left unreacted: 0.074 mol

**5.** A reaction has a theoretical yield of 12.5 g. A student
reports an actual yield of 13.1 g and a percent yield of 105%.

1. Is a yield above 100% chemically possible? Explain.
   
2. Give two plausible experimental explanations.
