> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Balance before you calculate — an unbalanced equation makes
> every downstream number wrong. Never change a subscript to balance.

**1.** Balance each equation:

1. Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂
   2 Na + 2 H₂O → 2 NaOH + H₂
2. C₄H₁₀ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O
   2 C₄H₁₀ + 13 O₂ → 8 CO₂ + 10 H₂O
3. Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂
   Fe₂O₃ + 3 CO → 2 Fe + 3 CO₂
4. Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + H₂SO₄ → CaSO₄ + H₃PO₄
   Ca₃(PO₄)₂ + 3 H₂SO₄ → 3 CaSO₄ + 2 H₃PO₄

**2.** A student "balances" H₂ + O₂ → H₂O by writing
H₂ + O₂ → H₂O₂. Explain what is wrong in one sentence, then balance it
correctly. 

**3.** For 2 KClO₃ → 2 KCl + 3 O₂:

1. Moles of O₂ from 4.0 mol KClO₃:
   6.0 mol
2. Moles of KClO₃ needed for 1.5 mol O₂:
   1.0 mol
3. Mass of O₂ from 24.5 g KClO₃
   ($M = 122.55$):
   *(working space)*

**4.** Ammonia burns: 4 NH₃ + 5 O₂ → 4 NO + 6 H₂O. Starting with
34.0 g NH₃ and excess oxygen:

1. Moles of NH₃: 2.00 mol
2. Mass of NO produced: 
   *(working space)*
3. Mass of O₂ consumed: 
   *(working space)*
4. Mass of water produced, then verify conservation of mass across the
   whole reaction.
   *(working space)*

**5.** Thermite: Fe₂O₃ + 2 Al → 2 Fe + Al₂O₃. What mass of
aluminum is required to produce 100.0 g of iron?

*(working space)*

**6.** Explain, in one or two sentences, why the coefficients of a
balanced equation give mole ratios but *not* mass ratios.
