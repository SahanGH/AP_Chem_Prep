# Worksheet 2 • The Energetics of Dissolving

*Chapter 11 • Properties of Solutions*  
Zumdahl §11.2 • three steps, two endothermic, one exothermic

[← all lessons](../index.md)

---

> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Every explanation on this sheet should name which of the three
> steps is responsible. “Like dissolves like” is a conclusion, not a
> reason.

**1.** The three-step model of solution formation:

1. Name each step and give the sign of its $\Delta H$.
   
2. Write the expression for $\Delta H_{\text{soln}}$.
   $\Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3$
3. For an ionic solute, what are steps 1 and 3 usually called?
   lattice energy; hydration energy

**2.** Predict the temperature change and identify a use:

1. NH₄NO₃, $\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = +25.7\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$:
   gets cold — instant cold pack
2. CaCl₂, $\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = -82.8\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$:
   gets hot — hand warmer, de-icer
3. NaCl, $\Delta H_{\text{soln}} = +3.9\,\mathrm{kJ/mol}$:
   very slightly cold

**3.** NaCl has a very large lattice energy, yet it dissolves
readily in water with $\Delta H_{\text{soln}}$ of only
+3.9 kJ/mol. Explain how such a small net value arises
from such large individual terms.

**4.** Use the three-step model to explain each observation:

1. Oil does not dissolve in water. 
2. Ethanol mixes with water in all proportions.
   
3. NaCl does not dissolve in hexane.
   

**5.** A student states: “Dissolving is always exothermic because new
attractions form.” Evaluate this claim.

**6.** Some substances dissolve even though $\Delta H_{\text{soln}}$ is
positive. What does this reveal about the driving force for dissolving?

**7.** Explain, energetically, why “like dissolves like” works —
without simply restating the phrase.
