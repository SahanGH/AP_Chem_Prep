> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> Name the force AND the structural feature that produces it. “It
> has stronger IMFs” restates the question — say *which* force and
> *why* it is present.

**1.** List every intermolecular force present in each substance:

1. CO₂: LDF only (nonpolar)
2. CH₃F: LDF + dipole–dipole
3. NH₃: LDF + dipole–dipole + H-bonding
4. KBr dissolved in water: ion–dipole

**2.** HF boils at 19.5 °C while HCl boils at
-85 °C, even though HCl has the greater molar mass.
Explain. 

**3.** Zumdahl reports these boiling points: BI₃
209.5 °C (LDF only), PCl₃ 76.1 °C
(LDF + dipole–dipole), NH₃ -33.0 °C (LDF + H-bonding).

1. This ordering appears to contradict “hydrogen bonding is
   strongest.” Resolve the apparent contradiction.
   
2. State the rule this illustrates in one sentence.
   

**4.** Classify each solid and predict whether it conducts
electricity as a solid:

1. SiC, mp 2730 °C, extremely hard:
   covalent network; no
2. Ag, mp 962 °C, malleable:
   metallic; yes
3. K₂S, mp 840 °C, brittle:
   ionic; no (yes when molten)
4. I₂, mp 114 °C, soft:
   molecular; no

**5.** A student claims that melting ice breaks the O–H bonds in
water. Evaluate this claim and give the correct account.

**6.** Rank by vapor pressure at 25 °C, highest first,
with a one-line reason: H₂O, CH₃OH, C₆H₁₄ (hexane).
