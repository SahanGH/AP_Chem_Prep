> 📌 **Scope — one CED exclusion applies**
>
> Covers `CED 9.1` through `CED 9.11`, worth
> 7 %–9 % of the exam. This unit joins
> thermodynamics to electrochemistry through a single bridge,
> $\Delta G^\circ = -nFE^\circ$, and the questions below are built to make
> that link explicit.
> 
> Topic `CED 9.8` excludes **labelling an electrode as positive or
> negative**, since the convention differs between cell types. Anode and
> cathode *are* assessed, and the rule never changes: oxidation occurs
> at the anode in both galvanic and electrolytic cells.
> 
> $R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J/mol/K}$,
> $F = 96485\,\mathrm{C/mol}$. Standard reduction potentials:
> Ag+/Ag $= +0.80$ V, Cu²⁺/Cu $= +0.34$ V.

### Free-response 2 • long • 10 points

A galvanic cell is built from a copper electrode in
1.0 M Cu(NO₃)₂ and a silver electrode in
1.0 M AgNO₃.

1. Write the half-reaction at each electrode, identify the anode, and
   give the balanced overall equation.
   \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_
2. Calculate $E^\circ_{\text{cell}}$ and $\Delta G^\circ$.
   *(working space)*
3. Calculate the cell potential when
   $[\text{Cu²⁺}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}$ and
   $[\text{Ag+}] = 0.010\,\mathrm{M}$.
   *(working space)*
4. Silver is deposited from AgNO₃ solution by electrolysis at
   1.50 A for 20.0 min. Calculate the mass
   deposited.
   *(working space)*
