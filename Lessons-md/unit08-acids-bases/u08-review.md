## First: which of the four cases are you in?

| **Strong acid or base alone** | complete ionization; pH directly.
  Double $[\text{OH-}]$ for Ba(OH)₂, Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂ |
|---|---|
| **Weak acid or base alone** | one equilibrium.
  $[\text{H+}] = \sqrt{K_aC_0}$, or for a base
  $[\text{OH-}] = \sqrt{K_bC_0}$ — then convert through pOH |
| **Both members of a pair** | buffer.
  pH $=$ p$K_a + \log(\text{base}/\text{acid})$ |
| **Strong reagent added** | react to completion *first*, in moles;
  then whichever case is left |

## The numbers and relationships

- $\mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = 14.00$;
   $K_aK_b = K_w = 1.0\times10^{-14}$;
   p$K_a = -\log K_a$.
- Six strong acids: HCl, HBr, HI, HNO₃,
   H₂SO₄, HClO₄. HF is *not* one.
- Neutral means $[\text{H+}] = [\text{OH-}]$ — **not** pH 7.
- Optimal buffering at pH $=$ p$K_a$; useful range p$K_a \pm 1$;
   indicators change over p$K_a \pm 1$ too.

## Titration curve, region by region

| **Region** | **Major species** | **Method** |
|---|---|---|
| before any base | HA | weak-acid ICE |
| before equivalence | HA $+$ A- | buffer, H–H |
| **halfway** | equal amounts | **pH $=$ p$K_a$** |
| at equivalence | A- only | weak-base ICE, $K_b = K_w/K_a$ |
| after equivalence | excess OH- | strong base alone |

Equivalence pH: strong$+$strong $= 7.00$ exactly; weak acid $+$ strong base
