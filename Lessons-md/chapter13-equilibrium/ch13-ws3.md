> 📌 **Directions & data**
>
> For every prediction, state the direction of shift AND whether the
> value of $K$ changes. Those are two separate questions and both are
> examinable.

**1.** For 2 SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2 SO₃(g),
$\Delta H = -198\,\mathrm{kJ}$, predict the shift and state whether $K$
changes:

1. Add SO₂: right; $K$ unchanged
2. Remove SO₃: right; $K$ unchanged
3. Decrease the volume: right (3 mol $\to$ 2 mol);
   $K$ unchanged
4. Increase the temperature:
   left; $K$ *decreases*
5. Add a catalyst: no shift; $K$ unchanged
6. Add helium at constant volume:
   no shift; $K$ unchanged

**2.** Explain each of the three "no shift" cases above:

1. Why does a catalyst cause no shift?
   
2. Why does adding an inert gas at constant volume cause no shift?
   
3. Why does adding more of a pure solid cause no shift?
   

**3.** Only one kind of change alters the numerical value of $K$.

1. Name it. temperature
2. Explain why the others do not. 
3. For an endothermic reaction, does raising the temperature increase
   or decrease $K$? Explain using heat as a reactant.
   

**4.** Zumdahl's arsenic roasting reaction:
As₄O₆(s) + 6 C(s) ⇌ As₄(g) + 6 CO(g). Predict the effect of each
change:

1. Adding CO: shifts left
2. Adding more carbon: no shift (pure solid)
3. Removing As₄ gas as it forms:
   shifts right
4. Increasing the container volume:
   shifts right (more gas moles)

**5.** **FRQ (10 points).** The Haber process synthesizes ammonia:

$$ \text{N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g)} \qquad \Delta H = -92\,\mathrm{kJ} $$

1. Write the $K_c$ expression, and determine $\Delta n$ and the
   relationship between $K_p$ and $K_c$.
   
2. A reaction vessel at equilibrium contains
   $[\text{N₂}] = 0.50\,\mathrm{M}$, $[\text{H₂}] = 1.0\,\mathrm{M}$,
   and $[\text{NH₃}] = 0.20\,\mathrm{M}$. Calculate $K_c$.
   *(working space)*
3. Some NH₃ is then removed. State the direction of shift, and
   state whether $K_c$ changes. 
4. Predict and explain the effect on the equilibrium yield of NH₃
   of (i) increasing the pressure and (ii) increasing the temperature.
   
5. Industrial plants nevertheless operate at roughly
   450 °C. Explain this apparent contradiction.
