**1.** Write $K$ for each heterogeneous equilibrium:

1. Fe₃O₄(s) + 4 H₂(g) ⇌ 3 Fe(s) + 4 H₂O(g):
   $[\text{H₂O}]^4/[\text{H₂}]^4$
2. 2 NaHCO₃(s) ⇌ Na₂CO₃(s) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(g):
   $[\text{CO₂}][\text{H₂O}]$
3. Explain why pure solids and liquids are excluded.
   

**2.** Interpreting the magnitude of $K$:

1. $K = 3\times10^{12}$: the equilibrium mixture is mostly
   products
2. $K = 2\times10^{-9}$: mostly
   reactants
3. Does a large $K$ imply a fast reaction? Explain.
   

**3.** For N₂(g) + 3 H₂(g) ⇌ 2 NH₃(g), $K_c = 0.500$. A flask
contains $[\text{N₂}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}$, $[\text{H₂}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}$,
$[\text{NH₃}] = 1.00\,\mathrm{M}$.

1. Calculate $Q$. 
   *(working space)*
2. Compare $Q$ with $K$ and predict the direction of shift.
   
3. What would $Q$ have to equal for no shift to occur?
   0.500

**4.** ICE table setup. Write the equilibrium row for each:

1. 2 HI ⇌ H₂ + I₂, starting with 0.400 M HI:
   $[\text{HI}] = 0.400-2x$; $[\text{H₂}] = [\text{I₂}] = x$
2. PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂, starting with 1.50 M PCl₅:
   $1.50-x$; $x$; $x$

**5.** N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2 NO₂(g) has $K_c = 4.6\times10^{-3}$. A flask
initially contains 1.00 M N₂O₄.

1. Write the ICE table.
   
2. Solve using the small-$x$ approximation. 
   *(working space)*
3. Perform the 5% check and state whether the approximation is valid.
   
4. Give both equilibrium concentrations.
   $[\text{NO₂}] = 0.068$ M;
   $[\text{N₂O₄}] = 0.966$ M

**6.** H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2 HI(g) has $K = 50.0$. A flask initially
contains 1.00 M of each reactant and no HI.

1. Explain why the small-$x$ approximation cannot be used here.
   
2. Solve for $x$. (The expression is a perfect square.)
   *(working space)*
3. Give all three equilibrium concentrations and verify against $K$.
   *(working space)*
